# Appendix A: Quick Reference - First Battle of El Alamein

This appendix provides essential tables and references for quick gameplay during First Battle of El Alamein scenarios (July 1942).

## BattleGroup Core Rules Summary

### Turn Sequence

1. **Orders Phase**: Both players draw battle rating (BR) chits if needed, issue orders
2. **Aircraft Phase**: Resolve air strikes, air-to-air combat
3. **Initiative Phase**: Roll for initiative (higher roll chooses who activates first)
4. **Action Phase**: Alternate unit activations until both players pass
5. **Rally Phase**: Attempt to rally pinned units, repair breakdowns
6. **End Phase**: Remove transient markers, check victory conditions

### Key Mechanics

**Morale/Battle Rating**: Each force has BR (typically 30-60 points for battalion-level games). When you take a BR chit (from losses, objectives lost, etc.), draw randomly from your pool. Game ends when a side's BR is reduced to 0 or below.

**Orders/Command**: Most units need orders to act aggressively. Roll 2D6 vs command rating. Artillery always needs orders. Units within command range of HQ don't need to test for defensive actions.

**Movement Rates**: Infantry 5" per action, motorized 10", fast vehicles 12-16", slow tanks 8-10"

**Shooting**: Roll to hit (modified by range, cover, movement), then target rolls armor save (if armored) or cover save (if infantry/soft target). Penetrating hits on vehicles roll on damage table.

**Pinning**: Units taking fire that causes casualties become pinned. Pinned units have reduced effectiveness and must rally before moving or firing effectively.

## Special Rules Alphabetical Index

### A-C

**APCR Ammunition**: Limited tungsten-core rounds. +2 penetration up to 30". German 50mm and 75mm guns. Max 3 shots per game.

**APDS Ammunition** (Experimental): Extremely rare British 6-pdr rounds. +3 penetration. Only 2 shots per gun per game, if available.

**Australian Forces**: 9th Australian Division highly effective. +1 to assault actions, no penalties for night combat, +1 to morale checks.

**Breakdown - Crusader**: Roll D6 when moving at full speed. On 1, immobilized. -1 modifier to all breakdown rolls.

**Breakdown - General**: D6 roll when moving at max speed over rough terrain. On 1, vehicle breaks down for D3 turns (1-4) or permanently (5-6).

**Breakdown - Extreme Heat**: Non-German, non-American vehicles suffer -1 additional to breakdown rolls in extreme heat scenarios.

**Breakdown - M13/40/M14/41**: Italian tanks suffer -1 to breakdown rolls in extreme heat (engine stress).

**Commonwealth Diversity**: First Alamein featured Australian, New Zealand, South African, Indian forces. Each has unique characteristics (see force organization).

### D-H

**Defensive Boxes**: British defensive positions. Prepared trenches (+1 cover), AT gun positions (hull-down +1), minefields.

**Desert Terrain**: Open terrain provides minimal cover. Most areas are open ground (no cover save) unless specifically marked.

**Dual Purpose (25-pdr)**: May fire HE (indirect artillery) or AP (direct AT). Switching modes requires 1 full action.

**Escarpment**: Impassable except at marked passes. Units on top get +6" spotting range. Reverse slope provides hard cover.

**Extreme Heat**: July 1942 temperatures brutal. Double water consumption, -1 to initiative rolls during midday turns, infantry exhaustion tests.

**Grant High Profile**: Grant tanks cannot claim soft cover bonuses (only hard cover). Enemy spotters get +1 to detect.

**Grant Limited Traverse**: 75mm sponson has 30° traverse each side. Tank must face target. 37mm turret has full 360°.

**Hull-Down**: Tanks in hull-down positions get +1 armor save vs frontal shots.

### I-R

**Indian Forces**: Steady under fire. +1 to morale checks when under artillery fire, +1 to hit in defensive fire from prepared positions.

**Italian Morale**: Most Italian units have lower morale. Use scenario-specific values (typically -1 vs German/British).

**Mechanically Unreliable**: See Breakdown rules above.

**Minefield - Extensive**: First Alamein featured massive minefields. Some particularly dense fields require additional clearing time (D6+2 turns per lane).

**New Zealand Forces**: 2nd NZ Division excellent combined arms. +1 to rally attempts, +1 to morale when operating with tank support within 6".

**Night Combat**: Australian forces excel. No penalties (other forces -1 to hit, visibility 12").

**No HE - 6-pounder**: Most 6-pdr guns lack HE ammunition in July 1942. Cannot engage infantry effectively.

**Panzer IV G/F2 Priority Target**: British forces prioritize long 75mm Panzer IVs. When spotted, all AT assets check if can engage.

**Portee Mounting**: AT guns mounted on trucks. Move at truck speed but must dismount (1 action) to fire. Vulnerable when mounted.

### S-Z

**Sherman Reliability**: American-built Sherman receives +1 to breakdown rolls. Excellent mechanical reliability in desert.

**Sherman Stabilizer** (Experimental): Few early Shermans had gyrostabilizer. +1 to hit when moving if so equipped (rare, scenario-specific).

**Smoke Rounds**: Create 6" diameter smoke screen, blocks LOS. Lasts 2 turns. Limited: British 4 rounds, German 2, Italian 1 per gun per game.

**South African Forces**: Determined defensive troops. +1 to cover saves in prepared positions, +1 to morale when defending objectives.

**Stuka Dive Bomber**: +1 to hit stationary/slow targets. Siren causes morale check (-1) to units within 12" of target.

**Supply - Water Critical**: Units cut off from water for 2+ turns suffer -1 to all actions (cumulative). After 4 turns, additional -1 morale penalty.

**Truck Vulnerability**: Trucks have no armor save. Any hit automatically damages the vehicle.

**Veteran Crews (German)**: German tank crews get +1 to shooting when firing first in an engagement. Superior training and experience.

**Wadi Crossing**: Only tracked vehicles may cross wadis. Roll D6: on 1-2, vehicle stuck and immobilized.

**Withdrawal Option**: In some scenarios, forces may voluntarily withdraw off table edge to preserve BR. Must exit designated edge.

## Force Organization Quick Reference

### British Force Structure (July 1942)

**Battalion-level game** (typical BR 35-45):
- 1x HQ (command value 8-9)
- 2-3x Infantry platoons (30-35 men each)
- 1x Support platoon (mortars, MGs, carriers)
- 1x Tank squadron (12-15 tanks: Grants, Crusaders, Valentines, or few Shermans)
- 1x Artillery battery (4-6 guns, typically 25-pdr)
- 1x AT gun section (2-4 guns, 6-pdr standard)

**Sherman Availability**: Very limited in July 1942. Only 1-2 Shermans per squadron if present. Allocate as special assets with premium points.

### Commonwealth Force Variations

**Australian (9th Australian Division)**:
- High morale (+1 to checks)
- Night combat capable (no penalties)
- Aggressive in assault (+1 to assault actions)
- Command value: 8-9

**New Zealand (2nd NZ Division)**:
- Steady troops (+1 to rally attempts)
- Combined arms (+1 morale when with tanks within 6")
- Reliable coordination (no scatter deployment penalties)
- Command value: 8-9

**South African (1st/2nd SA Division)**:
- Defensive specialists (+1 cover save in prepared positions)
- Determined when defending (+1 morale on objectives)
- Commonwealth cooperation (no coordination penalties)
- Command value: 8

**Indian (4th/5th Indian Division)**:
- Steady under artillery (+1 morale vs artillery fire)
- Defensive fire specialists (+1 to hit from prepared positions)
- Desert veterans (no environmental penalties)
- Command value: 8

### German Force Structure (July 1942)

**Battalion-level game** (typical BR 35-45):
- 1x HQ (command value 9-10, veteran)
- 2-3x Panzergrenadier platoons (30-35 men each)
- 1x Support platoon (mortars, MG34/MG42s, PAK)
- 1x Panzer company (12-18 tanks: Panzer III/IV mix)
- 1x Artillery battery (4-6 guns, 105mm or 88mm)
- 1x AT gun section (2-4 guns, PAK 40 or 88mm)

**Panzer Company Typical Mix** (July 1942): 8-10x Panzer III (50mm L/60), 4-6x Panzer IV F2/G (75mm L/43/L/48), 0-2x Panzer III N (short 75mm support).

**Afrika Korps Exhaustion**: By July 1942, some German units show signs of exhaustion. Scenario-specific: some units may have reduced BR or -1 to morale if depleted.

### Italian Force Structure (July 1942)

**Battalion-level game** (typical BR 26-36):
- 1x HQ (command value 7-8)
- 3-4x Infantry platoons (30-35 men each)
- 1x Support platoon (mortars, Breda HMGs)
- 1x Tank/Assault Gun company (8-12 M13/40, M14/41, or Semovente)
- 1x Artillery battery (4-6 guns, 75mm)
- 1x AT gun section (4-6 guns, 47mm, rare 90mm if available)

**Italian Morale Issues**: By First Alamein, Italian morale declining. Most units -1 to morale checks. Some elite units (Folgore, Ariete remnants) maintain normal morale.

## Combat Example: Sherman vs Panzer IV G at Ruweisat Ridge

**Situation**: American Sherman M4 tank (76mm cast frontal armor, 75mm gun) engages German Panzer IV G (50mm+ frontal armor, 75mm L/48 gun) at 24" range.

**Turn 1**: Both tanks spot each other. Sherman fires first (initiative roll).

**Sherman fires at Panzer IV G**:
- To Hit Roll: Base 4+, range 24" (no penalty under 30"), target in open: +0
- **Result: Needs 4+ to hit**
- American rolls 5 - **HIT**

**Penetration**:
- Sherman 75mm M3 penetration @ 24": 65mm (vs Panzer IV G 50mm+ frontal armor)
- Panzer IV armor save: Base 4+, improved G model armor: +1
- **Final save needed: 5+**

German rolls 4 - **PENETRATED**

**Damage Roll**:
- Base D6 roll: 4
- No special modifiers
- **Result: 4 = Knocked Out**

**Result**: Panzer IV knocked out, crew bails out, German player draws BR chit.

**German Response** (if Panzer IV had fired first):

**Panzer IV G fires at Sherman**:
- To Hit: Base 4+, range 24": +0, veteran crew first shot: +1
- **Result: Needs 3+ to hit**
- German rolls 4 - **HIT**

**Penetration**:
- 75mm L/48 penetration @ 24": 95mm (vs Sherman 76mm cast armor)
- Sherman armor save: Base 4+
- 75mm L/48 powerful gun: -1
- **Final save needed: 5+**

American rolls 3 - **PENETRATED**

**Damage Roll**:
- Base D6 roll: 5
- Powerful 75mm L/48: +1
- **Final: 6 = Destroyed (catastrophic)**

**Result**: Sherman destroyed, crew casualties, British player draws BR chit.

**Historical Note**: The Sherman's arrival at First Alamein provided British forces with a reliable, well-armored tank with excellent HE capability. However, the Panzer IV G's long 75mm L/48 gun could penetrate Sherman frontal armor, making engagements dangerous for both sides. Sherman's reliability and numbers would prove decisive, but individual tank-vs-tank combat remained lethal.

## First Battle of El Alamein Key Equipment Summary

### British Strengths (July 1942)
- **Sherman arrives**: Excellent reliability, good armor, effective HE
- **6-pdr widespread**: Can penetrate German armor effectively
- **Grant tanks**: Still effective dual-armament platform
- **Commonwealth diversity**: Australian, NZ, SA, Indian forces each with strengths
- **Defensive positions**: Well-prepared boxes with minefields
- **American reliability**: Sherman and Grant mechanically sound

### British Weaknesses
- **Limited Shermans**: Only small numbers available in July
- **Crusader still unreliable**: ~25-30% mechanical issues
- **6-pdr lacks HE**: Cannot engage infantry effectively
- **Scattered positions**: Boxes can be isolated
- **Extreme heat**: July temperatures affect operations

### German Strengths
- **Panzer IV G**: Improved long 75mm L/48 gun, better armor
- **75mm PaK 40**: Widespread powerful AT gun
- **Veteran crews**: Still superior training despite exhaustion
- **88mm dominance**: Devastating vs all Allied tanks
- **Tactical skill**: Combined arms mastery

### German Weaknesses
- **Exhausted forces**: Extended campaign taking toll
- **Supply difficulties**: Long supply lines vulnerable
- **Outnumbered**: British numerical superiority growing
- **Limited reserves**: Few replacement tanks/crews

### Italian Weaknesses
- **Morale declining**: Many units -1 to morale by July
- **M13/40 obsolete**: Cannot penetrate Allied armor
- **47mm useless**: AT gun ineffective vs British tanks
- **Extreme heat stress**: Italian vehicles suffer in July heat
- **Low effectiveness**: Most units less combat capable

### Commonwealth Diversity (Unique Strengths)
- **Australians**: High morale, night combat capable, aggressive
- **New Zealanders**: Steady, excellent combined arms
- **South Africans**: Defensive specialists in prepared positions
- **Indians**: Steady under artillery, defensive fire effective
