# Orders of Battle - First Battle of El Alamein

## British/Commonwealth Forces (July 1942)

**Commander-in-Chief Middle East:** General Sir Claude Auchinleck (assumed direct command of Eighth Army after relieving Ritchie)  
**Eighth Army Commander:** General Sir Claude Auchinleck (acting)  
**Deputy Commander:** Lieutenant-General Thomas Corbett

### XXX Corps (Northern Sector)
**Commander:** Lieutenant-General William Ramsden

#### 1st South African Division
**Commander:** Major-General Dan Pienaar

**Composition:**
- 1st, 2nd, 3rd South African Infantry Brigades
- South African artillery and support
- Northern coastal sector
- Approximately 14,000 troops (2nd SA Division lost at Tobruk)

#### 9th Australian Division
**Commander:** Major-General Leslie Morshead

**Composition:**
- 20th, 24th, 26th Australian Infantry Brigades
- Australian artillery (25-pounders)
- Divisional support units
- **Elite formation—masters of night operations**
- Approximately 15,000 troops

**Performance:** Outstanding. Repeatedly attacked and gained ground at Tel el Eisa and Alam el Onsol. Night assault tactics among most effective Allied operations of entire desert war.

#### 50th Infantry Division (Northumbrian) - Reformed
**Commander:** Major-General William Ramsden (prior to XXX Corps command)

**Composition:**
- 69th, 151st Infantry Brigades (150th Brigade destroyed at Gazala)
- Rebuilt after Gazala
- Approximately 11,000 troops

**XXX Corps Total:** ~40,000 troops

---

### XIII Corps (Central/Southern Sector)
**Commander:** Lieutenant-General William Gott (killed August 7 in air attack; succeeded by Montgomery)

#### 2nd New Zealand Division
**Commander:** Lieutenant-General Bernard Freyberg VC

**Composition:**
- 4th, 5th, 6th New Zealand Infantry Brigades
- 28th (Maori) Battalion
- New Zealand Divisional Cavalry
- New Zealand artillery
- Elite veteran formation
- Approximately 12,000 troops

**Performance:** Fought at Ruweisat Ridge (July 14-16) with mixed results; night attacks succeeded but supporting armor coordination failed. Still among best Commonwealth formations.

#### 5th Indian Division
**Commander:** Major-General Harold Briggs

**Composition:**
- 9th, 10th, 29th Indian Infantry Brigades
- 18th Indian Brigade (destroyed at Deir el Shein July 1—heroic 24-hour delaying action)
- Indian artillery and support
- Approximately 11,000 troops

**Performance:** 18th Brigade's sacrifice at Deir el Shein bought crucial 24 hours that saved Egypt.

#### 4th Indian Division (Reserve)
**Commander:** Major-General Francis Tuker

**Composition:**
- 5th, 7th, 11th Indian Infantry Brigades
- Reserve, not heavily engaged
- Approximately 10,000 troops

**XIII Corps Total:** ~35,000 troops

---

### Armored Forces (Distributed Between Corps)

#### 1st Armoured Division
**Commander:** Major-General Herbert Lumsden

**Composition:**
- **2nd Armoured Brigade**
  - Queen's Bays (Grant medium tanks)
  - 9th Lancers (Grant medium tanks)
  - 10th Hussars (Grant medium tanks)

- **22nd Armoured Brigade** (Reformed after Gazala)
  - Various tank regiments
  - Crusader and Grant tanks

**Tank Strength:** Approximately 150 tanks operational (Grant, Crusader, Valentine mix), with reserves arriving continuously

#### 7th Armoured Division (The "Desert Rats") - Depleted
**Commander:** Major-General John Harding

**Composition:**
- **4th Light Armoured Brigade**
  - Honey Stuart M3 light tanks
  - Armored car reconnaissance

- **7th Motor Brigade**
  - Motorized infantry support

**Tank Strength:** Approximately 100 tanks (primarily Honey Stuart light tanks, some Grant), severely depleted from Gazala

#### 23rd Armoured Brigade (NEW - Catastrophic Debut)
**Commander:** Brigadier George Richards

**Composition:**
- 40th, 46th, 50th Royal Tank Regiments
- **Equipped with Valentine infantry tanks** (slow, thin armor)
- Approximately 110 Valentine tanks

**Performance:** Annihilated July 21-22 at El Mreir Depression—80+ tanks destroyed in single day. One of worst British armor disasters of desert war.

**Total Armored Strength:** Approximately 400 tanks operational (including reserves), with continuous replacements arriving from Egypt

**Tank Types:**
- **M3 Grant:** 75mm sponson gun, 37mm turret gun, effective but tall profile
- **Crusader III:** 6-pounder gun (improved from 2-pounder), mechanically unreliable
- **Valentine:** 2-pounder or 6-pounder gun, slow (15 mph), reliable but vulnerable
- **Honey Stuart M3:** 37mm gun, light tank, excellent reconnaissance and reliability

---

### Artillery and Support

**Royal Artillery:**
- 600+ 25-pounder field guns (18/25-pounders, excellent range and HE effectiveness)
- 6-pounder anti-tank guns (increasing numbers, effective against most German tanks)
- 2-pounder anti-tank guns (obsolescent but still deployed)

**Desert Air Force:**
**Commander:** Air Vice-Marshal Arthur Coningham

**Aircraft:** 500+ aircraft
- Hurricane IIc fighters (4x 20mm cannon)
- Kittyhawk fighters (P-40)
- Baltimore and Boston light bombers
- **Maintained air superiority throughout battle**

---

**Total Commonwealth Eighth Army Strength:**
- **Personnel:** ~150,000 troops
- **Tanks:** 400+ operational (continuous replacement from Egypt)
- **Artillery:** 600+ guns (massive advantage)
- **Aircraft:** 500+ (air superiority)

**Organizational Innovation:**

Auchinleck reorganized forces into **combined brigade groups**—integrating tanks, infantry, artillery, and anti-tank guns into cohesive formations. This copied successful German tactics and marked significant improvement over previous piecemeal British organization.

## Axis Forces (July 1942)

**Commander:** Generalfeldmarschall Erwin Rommel (Panzerarmee Afrika)

### German Forces - Afrika Korps (Severely Depleted)

#### 15th Panzer Division
**Commander:** Generalmajor Gustav von Vaerst

**Composition:**
- **8th Panzer Regiment** (depleted)
  - Approximately 50-60 operational tanks (Panzer III, Panzer IV F2)
  - Down from 120 tanks at Gazala start

- **104th and 115th Panzergrenadier Regiments**
  - Motorized infantry at reduced strength
  - Approximately 3,000 men (down from 4,000)

- **33rd Artillery Regiment**
  - 105mm, 150mm howitzers
  - 88mm FlaK batteries (ammunition rationed)

- **33rd Panzerjäger Battalion**
  - PAK 38 50mm, PAK 40 75mm AT guns

**Division Strength:** ~55 tanks, ~6,000 men (heavily depleted)

#### 21st Panzer Division
**Commander:** Generalmajor Georg von Bismarck

**Composition:**
- **5th Panzer Regiment** (depleted)
  - Approximately 50-60 operational tanks (Panzer III, Panzer IV F2)
  - Down from 130 tanks at Gazala start

- **104th and 155th Rifle Regiments** (motorized infantry)
  - Approximately 3,000 men (reduced)

- **155th Artillery Regiment**
  - Mixed artillery, 88mm FlaK batteries

- **39th Panzerjäger Battalion**
  - PAK 38, PAK 40 AT guns

**Division Strength:** ~55 tanks, ~6,000 men (heavily depleted)

#### 90th Light Division (Afrika Division)
**Commander:** Generalmajor Ulrich Kleemann

**Composition:**
- 155th, 200th, 361st Regiments (motorized infantry)
- Reconnaissance battalion
- Artillery and AT support
- Approximately 7,000 men (down from 9,000)
- No tanks (pure motorized infantry)

#### 164th Light Division (NEW—arrived late June)
**Commander:** Generalmajor Carl-Hans Lungershausen

**Composition:**
- Infantry division (not motorized)
- Approximately 6,000 men
- Deployed in northern sector; engaged by Australian attacks

**German Tank Types:**
- **Panzer III Ausf J/L:** 50mm KwK 39 L/60 long gun, effective
- **Panzer IV Ausf F2:** 75mm KwK 40 L/43 long gun—still superior firepower to most British tanks
- **Panzer II:** Light tank, limited combat value

**Critical German Assets:**
- **88mm FlaK 18/36/37:** Still devastating, but ammunition rationed (~40 guns operational)
- **PAK 40 75mm:** Excellent AT gun, increasing numbers (~30-35 guns)
- **PAK 38 50mm:** Standard AT gun (~60-80 guns)

**Total German Strength:** ~110-120 tanks operational, ~28,000 men

**Critical German Weaknesses:**
- **Fuel Crisis:** Severe shortages limited operations; tanks often immobilized
- **Ammunition Shortages:** Artillery and AT guns rationed
- **Exhaustion:** Continuous combat since May 26
- **Extended Supply Lines:** 1,400 miles from Tripoli; Tobruk capture didn't solve fundamental problem
- **Mechanical Attrition:** Tank strength down 60% from combat and breakdowns

## Force Comparison

| Category | Commonwealth | Axis (German + Italian) |
|----------|--------------|-------------------------|
| **Personnel** | 150,000 | ~60,500 (28,000 German, 32,500 Italian) |
| **Tanks** | 400+ operational (continuous replacement) | ~220 operational (110-120 German, 110 Italian) |
| **Tank Quality** | Grant effective, Crusader/Valentine mixed | Panzer IV F2 still superior; Italian tanks obsolete |
| **Anti-Tank** | 6-pounder improving, 2-pounder obsolete | 88mm/PAK 40 excellent but ammunition rationed |
| **Artillery** | 600+ 25-pounders (massive advantage) | Mixed German/Italian (adequate but low ammunition) |
| **Air Power** | 500+ aircraft (total superiority) | ~200-250 aircraft (inadequate, fuel-starved) |
| **Supply Situation** | Secure, short lines to Egypt | **CRITICAL CRISIS—fuel and ammunition shortages** |
| **Troop Condition** | Shaken from Gazala but reorganizing | **Exhausted from continuous operations since May 26** |

**Commonwealth Advantages:**
- Numerical superiority: 2.5:1 troops, 1.8:1 tanks, 3:1 artillery
- **Defensive position:** El Alamein narrow front (40 miles) eliminated flanking maneuvers
- Short supply lines: Cairo 60 miles, Alexandria 100 miles
- **Air superiority:** RAF Desert Air Force dominated
- Continuous reinforcements and replacements
- **Combined brigade groups:** Improved organization (Auchinleck's reform)

**Axis Advantages:**
- Superior crew training and gunnery (German forces)
- Panzer IV F2 long 75mm still provided firepower advantage
- Integrated combined-arms tactics (German doctrine)
- Tactical leadership (Rommel's genius)
- **BUT:** Advantages negated by fuel/ammunition crisis, exhaustion, and numerical inferiority

**Critical Factors:**

1. **Axis Supply Crisis:** Fuel shortages immobilized armor; ammunition rationing limited artillery/AT effectiveness
2. **Narrow Front:** El Alamein position eliminated Rommel's favored flanking maneuvers
3. **Exhaustion:** Axis forces depleted from 60+ days continuous combat
4. **British Numbers:** Material superiority finally decisive; Axis couldn't sustain attrition
5. **Australian Effectiveness:** 9th Australian Division's night attacks particularly effective

## Special Engagements and Performance Notes

### Deir el Shein (July 1)
- **Indian 18th Brigade** (~4,000 men) held fortified box 24 hours
- Destroyed by German 90th Light Division + captured Grants + 88mm guns
- **Heroic delaying action saved Egypt—bought time for defense organization**

### Tel el Eisa (July 10-12)
- **9th Australian Division** attacked German 164th Light + Italian Brescia
- Australian success created salient, inflicted disproportionate casualties
- Demonstrated Allied offensive capability

### Ruweisat Ridge (July 14-16)
- **2nd New Zealand Division** night attacks initially successful
- Tank-infantry coordination failures led to German dawn counter-attacks defeating both separately
- Heavy casualties; demonstrated continued British tactical difficulties despite improvements

### El Mreir Depression (July 21-22)
- **23rd Armoured Brigade** (110 Valentine tanks) attacked into killing ground
- 80+ tanks destroyed by concealed 88mm, PAK 40, Panzer IV F2
- **One of worst British armor disasters—"tank graveyard"**

### Alam el Onsol (July 26-27)
- **9th Australian Division** night attack
- Overran German/Italian positions using infiltration tactics
- Extended salient; demonstrated Australian mastery of night operations

---

**Notes:**
- Axis forces down 60% from Gazala start due to combat losses, mechanical breakdowns, fuel shortages
- British continuous replacement maintained strength; Axis could not replace losses
- 9th Australian Division emerged as star performer—aggressive night operations models for future Allied tactics
- First El Alamein was defensive battle; Second El Alamein (October 1942) would be Montgomery's offensive using Auchinleck's defensive foundation
