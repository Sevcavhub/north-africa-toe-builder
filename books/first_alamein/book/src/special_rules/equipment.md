# Equipment Special Rules

This section provides special rules for key equipment types used during the First Battle of El Alamein. These rules reflect the unique characteristics and performance of weapons and vehicles in desert conditions during July 1942.

## British Armor

### Grant Medium Tank

The American-supplied Grant remained the most capable British tank at First Alamein, with its dual armament providing both anti-tank and infantry support capabilities.

**Dual Armament**: The Grant mounts both a 75mm gun in a sponson and a 37mm gun in the turret. It may fire both weapons in the same turn at different targets, or concentrate both on one target for +1 to hit.

**75mm HE Capability**: The Grant's 75mm receives +1 to hit rolls against infantry and soft targets. This HE capability was crucial for British combined arms operations.

**High Profile**: The Grant's tall silhouette makes concealment difficult. It may not claim soft cover bonuses (only hard cover applies). Enemy spotters receive +1 to detect Grant tanks.

**Limited Traverse**: The 75mm sponson gun has limited traverse (30° each side). The tank must be oriented toward the target. The turret-mounted 37mm has full 360° traverse.

**Good Armor**: Solid frontal armor proof against most Axis guns except the 50mm L/60 at close range, the new 75mm PaK 40, and the 88mm. Receives +1 to armor saves against 37mm and Italian 47mm guns.

**Reliable**: American-built reliability. Add +1 to all breakdown rolls.

### Stuart (Honey) Light Tank

American Stuart light tanks remained in service for reconnaissance despite their vulnerability to improved Axis anti-tank weapons.

**Reliable**: Never suffers breakdown penalties. Add +1 to all reliability rolls.

**Fast**: Movement rate of 16" on roads, 12" cross-country. Excellent for reconnaissance and pursuit.

**37mm Gun**: Increasingly inadequate against improved Axis armor. Effective only against Panzer II and light vehicles. Struggles against Panzer III and IV frontal armor.

**Light Armor**: Vulnerable to all Axis anti-tank weapons. Receives -1 to armor saves.

**Scout**: When performing reconnaissance, the Stuart may make an extra 6" move before regular movement phase (represents aggressive scouting).

### Valentine Infantry Tank

The Valentine was the standard British infantry tank at First Alamein, providing reliable armored support.

**Good Armor**: Solid frontal armor. Receives +1 to armor saves against 37mm, 47mm, and 50mm L/42 guns. Can resist 50mm L/60 at longer ranges (20"+).

**Slow But Steady**: Movement rate of 10" on roads, 8" cross-country. More reliable than Crusader - add +1 to breakdown rolls.

**6-Pounder Armed**: Most Valentines at First Alamein mounted the 6-pounder (57mm) gun, providing good anti-tank capability against all Axis tanks except at extreme range.

**No HE**: The 6-pounder lacks HE ammunition at this period. Cannot engage infantry in open effectively (treat as MG fire only).

**Infantry Support**: Designed to support infantry. When operating within 6" of friendly infantry, both tank and infantry receive +1 to morale checks.

### Crusader II/III Cruiser Tank

By First Alamein, the Crusader remained in service but was being phased out due to reliability issues.

**Fast Cruiser**: Movement rate of 16" on roads and 12" cross-country. May perform breakthrough moves and pursuit actions with +2" bonus.

**Still Unreliable**: Roll D6 at start of each turn for each Crusader. On a 1, the tank suffers engine breakdown and is immobilized for the rest of the game.

**Crusader III**: Most Crusaders at First Alamein were Crusader III armed with 6-pounder gun. Can penetrate Panzer III and IV reliably at normal ranges. Lacks HE ammunition.

**Light Armor**: The Crusader's thin armor is vulnerable to all Axis anti-tank guns. Receives -1 to armor saves against 50mm and larger guns.

### Matilda II Infantry Tank

A few Matildas remained in limited service, though largely replaced by Valentines by July 1942.

**Thick Armor**: Still immune to most Axis guns at range. Receives +2 armor save against guns smaller than 50mm.

**Very Slow**: Maximum movement rate 8" on roads, 6" cross-country. May not perform breakthrough or pursuit moves.

**Obsolescent Armament**: Armed with 2-pounder gun, inadequate by 1942 standards. No HE capability. Effective mainly in infantry support role against other tanks at close range only.

**Rare**: By First Alamein, Matildas were scarce. Reduced point cost reflecting obsolescence.

## British Anti-Tank Guns

### 6-Pounder (57mm) Anti-Tank Gun

The 6-pounder was now the standard British anti-tank gun, replacing the inadequate 2-pounder.

**Powerful**: Can penetrate all Axis tanks of this period at normal ranges (up to 30"). Receives +2 to penetration rolls compared to the 2-pounder.

**Effective Against Panzer IV F2**: The 6-pounder is one of the few British weapons that can reliably engage the new long-barrel Panzer IV at range.

**Limited HE**: Some 6-pounders had HE ammunition by July 1942, though availability varied. In scenarios where HE is specified, the 6-pounder can engage infantry targets at -1 effectiveness compared to dedicated HE weapons.

**Portee Mounting**: Many 6-pounders were mounted on trucks for mobility. Use standard Portee rules (must dismount to fire, dismounting takes one action).

### 25-Pounder Gun-Howitzer

Britain's versatile field gun, extensively used at First Alamein in both artillery and anti-tank roles.

**Dual Purpose**: May fire as field artillery (indirect HE) or anti-tank gun (direct AP). Switching between roles requires one full action.

**Effective HE**: Standard field artillery with 3" burst radius. Excellent smoke capability.

**Adequate AP**: Can penetrate Panzer III and most Panzer IV variants at ranges up to 20". Against the new Panzer IV F2/G with improved armor, requires close range (under 15") for reliable penetration.

**Ubiquitous**: The most common British field gun, available in large numbers throughout First Alamein battles.

### 2-Pounder (40mm) Anti-Tank Gun

Some 2-pounders remained in service in secondary positions and defensive boxes.

**Obsolete**: By July 1942, the 2-pounder is clearly obsolete. Struggles against improved Axis armor. Effective only against light vehicles and side/rear armor at close range.

**No HE Capability**: Cannot engage infantry or soft targets effectively.

**Being Phased Out**: Reduced points cost reflecting obsolescence. Only present in defensive positions or secondary units.

## German Armor

### Panzer IV (Ausf F2/G models) - "Long 75mm"

The game-changing Panzer IV with long 75mm gun was appearing in increasing numbers by First Alamein, dominating British armor.

**Devastating Gun**: The 75mm L/43 gun (F2) or L/48 gun (G) could penetrate any British tank at normal combat ranges. Receives +2 to penetration rolls against all targets.

**Dual Capability**: Unlike the short 75mm, the long gun fires excellent AP and HE ammunition. No penalties when switching between ammunition types.

**Priority Target**: British forces learned to prioritize these tanks. When spotted, all available AT assets must check if they can engage (represents tactical priority to eliminate these dangerous vehicles).

**Limited But Growing Numbers**: More numerous at First Alamein than at Gazala, but still valuable. Allocate as important support units with premium point costs.

**Improved Armor**: F2/G models receive +1 to frontal armor saves compared to earlier Panzer IV variants.

### Panzer III (Ausf J/L/M models)

Still the most common German tank at First Alamein, now with improved armor and long 50mm gun.

**50mm L/60 Gun**: The longer 50mm gun provides excellent penetration. Can engage Grant and Valentine frontally at ranges up to 30", though close range preferred for certain kills.

**Spaced Armor**: Most Panzer IIIs by July 1942 had additional armor plates. These models receive +1 to armor saves.

**Veteran Crews**: Afrika Korps crews were highly experienced. All Panzer III tanks receive +1 to shooting when firing first in an engagement.

**Desert Modifications**: German tanks had excellent air filtration. No special breakdown penalties in desert conditions.

**Reliable**: German tanks maintained their mechanical reliability advantage. No modifier to breakdown rolls (standard reliability).

### Panzer IV (Ausf E/F1 models) - "Short 75mm"

The older infantry support version was being phased out but some remained in service.

**Infantry Support**: The short 75mm excels at HE fire. Receives +1 to hit rolls against infantry and soft targets.

**Poor Anti-Tank**: The short 75mm has inadequate penetration against modern armor. Suffers -2 to penetration rolls against tanks. Should avoid tank combat.

**Vulnerable**: By July 1942, the short Panzer IV's armor was barely adequate. British 6-pounders and 25-pounders penetrate easily at normal ranges.

**Being Replaced**: Reduced points cost. These tanks were being replaced by long-barrel F2/G models as quickly as possible.

### Panzer II Light Tank

Obsolete but still used for reconnaissance and screening.

**Fast Reconnaissance**: Movement rate 16" on roads, 12" cross-country. +2" when withdrawing from combat.

**Light Armor**: Vulnerable to all British AT weapons. Receives -1 to all armor saves.

**20mm Autocannon**: Only effective against soft targets and infantry. Cannot engage tanks effectively (treat as heavy MG).

**Expendable Scout**: Reduced points cost reflecting role as disposable reconnaissance vehicle.

## German Anti-Tank Guns

### 88mm FlaK 18/36/37 Anti-Aircraft/Anti-Tank Gun

The legendary "88" remained the dominant anti-tank weapon in the desert, feared by all Allied tank crews.

**Devastating Power**: Can penetrate any British tank at ranges up to 40". Receives +3 to penetration rolls against all targets.

**Long Range**: May engage targets up to 60". Ignores range penalties for first 40".

**High Profile**: Cannot claim soft cover bonuses. Enemy artillery observers receive +1 to spot 88mm positions.

**Dual Purpose**: May engage aircraft or ground targets. Switching requires full action.

**Extremely Valuable**: Premium point cost. Usually carefully sited in defensive positions or with mobile battle groups. Losing an 88mm is serious blow to Axis forces.

### 75mm PaK 40 Anti-Tank Gun

The powerful new German anti-tank gun was more common by First Alamein, supplementing the 50mm PaK 38.

**Powerful**: Can penetrate all British tanks at normal ranges (up to 35"). Receives +2 to penetration rolls.

**Long Range**: Effective range of 45", significantly outranging British AT guns except the 25-pounder.

**Heavy**: Requires two actions to unlimber due to weight. Less mobile than 50mm PaK 38.

**Increasing Availability**: More common at First Alamein than earlier battles. Available as support weapons in most scenarios.

### 50mm PaK 38 Anti-Tank Gun

Germany's former standard towed anti-tank gun, being supplemented by the PaK 40.

**Good Penetration**: Effective against Crusaders and Stuarts at all ranges. Can penetrate Grants and Valentines at close-medium range (under 25").

**Mobile**: Light and easy to deploy. Requires only one action to unlimber.

**APCR Available**: Limited APCR ammunition provides +2 to penetration (up to 3 rounds per gun per game).

**Being Supplemented**: Still effective but being supplemented by the more powerful PaK 40. Standard availability with moderate point cost.

### 37mm PaK 36 Anti-Tank Gun

Completely obsolete by 1942, found only in secondary or Italian-supported units.

**Obsolete**: Cannot penetrate any British tank except Stuart at close range. Essentially ineffective as anti-tank weapon.

**Emergency Use Only**: Should only be included in scenarios representing depleted or second-line units.

**Minimal Value**: Very cheap point cost reflecting uselessness against 1942 armor.

## Italian Armor

### M13/40 Medium Tank

Italy's main tank, completely outclassed by July 1942 but still in service.

**Dual Machine Guns**: Twin hull-mounted MGs provide extra anti-infantry firepower.

**47mm Gun**: Inadequate against British armor. Can only penetrate Stuarts and possibly Crusaders at close range. Cannot penetrate Grants or Valentines frontally.

**Sand Filter Problems**: At game start, roll D6 for each M13/40. On 1-2, reduce movement by -4" for entire game due to engine sand ingestion.

**Riveted Construction**: When penetrated, add +1 to crew casualty rolls due to armor spalling.

**Obsolete**: By July 1942, the M13/40 was hopelessly outclassed. Very reduced point cost reflects this.

### M14/41 Medium Tank

Improved M13/40 with better engine but still inadequate against British armor.

**Better Reliability**: No sand filter problems. Use standard breakdown rules.

**Otherwise Identical**: All other characteristics match M13/40. Still obsolete by 1942 standards.

**Slightly Better**: Marginal improvement over M13/40. Slight point cost increase but still very low.

### Semovente 75/18 Self-Propelled Gun

Italy's assault gun, mounting a short 75mm howitzer on M14/41 chassis.

**75mm Howitzer**: Effective HE for infantry support. Receives +1 to hit infantry and soft targets.

**Limited Anti-Tank**: Short 75mm has poor armor penetration. Can engage light armor at close range only. Should avoid tank combat.

**Low Profile**: Receives +1 to cover saves when hull-down.

**Limited Traverse**: Fixed casemate gun has 30° traverse each side. Vehicle must face target.

**Support Role**: Best used as artillery support for infantry, not for tank combat.

## Italian Anti-Tank Guns

### 47mm 47/32 Anti-Tank Gun

Italy's standard AT gun, completely inadequate by July 1942.

**Inadequate**: Cannot penetrate Grant, Valentine, or Matilda frontal armor. Only effective against light armor (Stuart) and side/rear shots at close range.

**Reliable**: Mechanically reliable. Requires only one action to unlimber.

**Obsolete**: Very reduced point cost. Only useful in defensive positions where side shots might be possible or against light vehicles.

### Cannone da 90/53 Anti-Aircraft/Anti-Tank Gun

Italy's answer to the 88mm, a powerful 90mm AA/AT gun - rare but effective.

**Powerful**: Can penetrate all British tanks at ranges up to 35". Receives +2 to penetration rolls.

**Long Range**: May engage targets up to 50". Ignores range penalties for first 35".

**Dual Purpose**: May engage aircraft or ground targets. Switching requires full action.

**High Profile**: Cannot claim soft cover. Enemy observers receive +1 to spot.

**Very Rare**: Extremely limited numbers available. Premium point cost when allocated. Represents valuable asset for Italian forces.

### Cannone da 75/32 modello 37 Field Gun

Modern Italian 75mm field gun occasionally used in AT role.

**Field Gun Primary**: Designed for indirect HE fire. When used as AT gun, suffers -1 to hit moving targets.

**Adequate HE**: Effective as field artillery with standard characteristics.

**Poor Armor Penetration**: When firing AP, has limited effectiveness against modern armor. Can engage light tanks only with any chance of success.

## Special Ammunition

### Armor-Piercing Composite Rigid (APCR)

Limited quantities of tungsten-cored ammunition available to some German guns.

**Limited Supply**: German 50mm and 75mm guns may have up to 3 APCR rounds per gun per game.

**Superior Penetration**: APCR adds +2 to penetration rolls at ranges up to 30". No bonus beyond 30".

**German Only**: British forces did not have APCR available during First Alamein. Italian forces never received APCR.

### Armor-Piercing Discarding Sabot (APDS)

Experimental British ammunition for 6-pounder guns, very limited availability.

**Experimental**: A few British 6-pounder guns had experimental APDS ammunition.

**Excellent Penetration**: APDS adds +3 to penetration rolls at any range up to 40".

**Extremely Limited**: Only 2 rounds per gun per game. Only available if specifically noted in scenario special rules.

### Smoke Rounds

**British**: 25-pounders have plentiful smoke. May fire up to 4 smoke rounds per gun per game.

**German**: 50mm and 75mm guns have limited smoke (2 rounds per gun per game).

**Italian**: Very limited smoke capability (1 round per gun per game, if any).

**Effect**: Creates 6" diameter smoke screen blocking line of sight for two full turns.

## Vehicle Reliability

### Breakdown Rules

Desert conditions continued to cause mechanical failures, especially in the brutal July heat.

**Breakdown Test**: When vehicle moves at full speed or over rough terrain, roll D6. On 1, vehicle suffers breakdown.

**Reliability Modifiers**:
- American tanks (Grant, Stuart): +1 (notably reliable)
- British Crusader: -1 (notoriously unreliable, worse in heat)
- British Valentine: +1 (reliable)
- British Matilda: +1 (very reliable, if present)
- German vehicles: No modifier (excellent filtration)
- Italian M13/40: -1 in extreme heat (sand filter problems)
- Italian M14/41: No modifier in normal conditions; -1 in extreme heat

**Extreme Heat**: At First Alamein (July), all vehicles except German and American suffer -1 additional to breakdown rolls during scenarios with extreme heat conditions (scenario dependent).

### Recovery

**Field Repairs**: During rally phase, roll D6 for immobilized vehicle with mechanics/engineers within 4". On 5+, vehicle repaired and may move next turn at half speed.

**Recovery Vehicles**: May tow immobilized vehicles at half speed. Towed vehicle removed from play.

**Desert Recovery**: In extreme heat, recovery operations suffer -1 to repair rolls (crew exhaustion, tool heating).

## Aircraft Special Rules

### Stuka Dive Bomber (Ju-87)

The Stuka remained effective at First Alamein despite growing Allied air presence.

**Precision Bomber**: +1 to hit stationary or slow targets when dive bombing.

**Siren Effect**: Enemy units within 12" of target must pass morale check at -1.

**Vulnerable**: Increasingly vulnerable to improved Allied fighters. Shot down on 4+ by fighters.

### Bf-109 Fighter

German fighter maintaining air superiority at First Alamein despite RAF presence.

**Air Superiority**: Bf-109 receives +1 in air combat against Hurricane/Kittyhawk.

**Ground Attack**: May strafe with cannon fire, though not primary role. -1 to hit ground targets.

### Hurricane/Kittyhawk Fighter-Bomber

Allied fighters performing ground attack missions.

**Ground Attack**: +1 to hit soft targets and unarmored vehicles.

**Outclassed**: Bf-109 was superior in air combat. RAF fighters receive -1 when engaged by Bf-109.

**Limited Bomb Load**: Each aircraft may make only 1 attack run per game before departing.

### Baltimore/Boston Medium Bomber

Allied medium bombers conducting tactical bombing missions.

**Medium Bomber**: Heavy bomb load. Uses medium bomber attack profile.

**Formation Bombing**: If 2+ bombers attack same target, receive +1 to hit (represents concentrated bombing).

**Fighter Cover**: Bomber formations often had fighter escort. May include fighters if specified in scenario.

**Vulnerable**: Slow bombers vulnerable to fighters if unescorted. Shot down on 3+ by enemy fighters if no friendly fighter cover.

## Transport and Supply

### Truck Vulnerability

Soft-skinned vehicles remained extremely vulnerable.

**No Armor**: Any hit automatically damages truck. No armor saves.

**Cargo Loss**: Destroyed truck loses cargo. Passengers take casualties as if under MG fire.

**Movement**: 18" road, 12" cross-country. May not enter rough terrain without immobilization test.

### Supply Rules

First Alamein scenarios may include supply considerations reflecting the extended defensive battle.

**Water**: Critical in July heat. Units cut off from water for 2+ turns suffer -1 to all actions (cumulative). After 4 turns, additional -1 morale penalty.

**Fuel**: Track fuel points per scenario. Vehicles without fuel immobilized.

**Ammunition**: Heavy weapons may have limited ammunition. Track expenditure per scenario rules.

**Heat Effects**: In extreme heat scenarios, water consumption doubles. Supply difficulties increased.

## Terrain Interaction

### Wadis

**Vehicle Restrictions**: Only tracked vehicles may cross. Wheeled vehicles need designated crossings.

**Crossing Test**: Tracked vehicles roll D6. On 1-2, immobilized in wadi.

### Escarpments

**Impassable**: Except at designated passes or gaps.

**Observation**: Units on top receive +6" spotting range.

**Reverse Slope**: Hard cover, cannot be seen without direct line of sight.

### Minefields

First Alamein featured extensive minefields, both British defensive fields and Axis fields.

**Movement**: Units entering minefield must test. Roll D6: on 1-2, unit stops and takes D3 casualties.

**Vehicle Mines**: Vehicles entering minefields suffer more damage. On 1-3, vehicle immobilized and takes D3 hits.

**Clearing**: Engineer teams can clear 6" lane in D3 turns.

**Marking**: Cleared lanes should be marked for subsequent movement.

**Density**: British minefields at First Alamein were extensive. Scenarios may specify particularly dense fields requiring additional clearing time.

### Depression Boxes

British defensive "boxes" at First Alamein featured prepared positions in depressions.

**Hull-Down Positions**: Defenders in box positions receive +1 to cover saves.

**Prepared Defenses**: Defensive positions with trenches, dugouts, barbed wire.

**Supply Dumps**: Boxes contain supply dumps - if captured, provide supply points or victory points.

### Extreme Heat

July 1942 temperatures at El Alamein were brutal. Some scenarios may include heat effects.

**Heat Exhaustion**: Infantry units moving at full speed in extreme heat must test. Roll D6: on 1-2, unit becomes pinned (exhaustion).

**Water Critical**: Double water consumption rates in extreme heat scenarios.

**Vehicle Stress**: Non-German, non-American vehicles suffer -1 to breakdown rolls in extreme heat.

**Reduced Tempo**: In extreme heat scenarios, initiative rolls suffer -1 during hottest part of day (midday turns).

## Commonwealth Forces Special Rules

First Alamein featured diverse Commonwealth forces, each with distinct characteristics.

### Australian Infantry

9th Australian Division was particularly effective at First Alamein.

**Aggressive**: Australian infantry receive +1 to assault actions.

**Night Operations**: Australian forces were skilled at night attacks. No penalties for night combat (other forces suffer -1).

**High Morale**: +1 to morale checks. Australian forces rarely broke under pressure.

### New Zealand Infantry

2nd New Zealand Division provided reliable infantry and combined arms coordination.

**Steady**: New Zealand forces receive +1 to rally attempts.

**Combined Arms**: When operating with tank support within 6", both tanks and infantry receive +1 to morale.

**Reliable**: New Zealand forces maintain cohesion. No additional penalties for scattered deployment.

### South African Infantry

South African forces defended key positions at First Alamein.

**Defensive**: South African forces in prepared positions receive additional +1 to cover saves (cumulative with position bonuses).

**Determined**: +1 to morale when defending objectives.

**Commonwealth Cooperation**: South African forces operate well with other Commonwealth units. No coordination penalties.

### Indian Infantry

Indian divisions provided crucial infantry strength.

**Steady Under Fire**: Indian infantry receive +1 to morale checks when under artillery fire.

**Defensive Specialists**: When in prepared positions, Indian infantry receive +1 to hit in defensive fire.

**Desert Veterans**: By First Alamein, Indian troops were experienced desert fighters. No environmental penalties.

## Special Rules Summary

This section provides equipment-specific rules for authentic First Battle of El Alamein scenarios. Key features of July 1942 period:

- **German Long 75mm Dominance**: Panzer IV F2/G was most feared tank on battlefield
- **British 6-Pounder Advantage**: Finally had effective AT gun against Axis armor
- **Grant Tank Dual Role**: 75mm HE + 37mm AT provided flexibility
- **Extreme Heat**: July desert temperatures affected operations
- **Commonwealth Diversity**: Australian, New Zealand, South African, Indian forces each had distinct characteristics
- **Defensive Focus**: First Alamein was primarily defensive battle with British holding positions

Cross-reference with standard BattleGroup rules for complete gameplay mechanics.

**Historical Note**: First Battle of El Alamein (July 1-27, 1942) halted Axis advance at Egypt's doorstep. Exhausted Axis forces could not break through determined Commonwealth defense. Strategic British victory - saved Egypt and Suez Canal. Both sides regrouped for Second Battle of El Alamein (October 1942), which would prove decisive turning point of North African campaign.
