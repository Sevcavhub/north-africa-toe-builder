# Equipment Special Rules

This section provides special rules for key equipment types used during Operation Crusader. These rules reflect the unique characteristics and performance of weapons and vehicles in desert conditions during November-December 1941.

## British Armor

### Matilda II Infantry Tank

The Matilda II remained the most heavily armored British tank in North Africa, virtually immune to most Axis anti-tank weapons.

**Thick Armor**: The Matilda II has armor values that make it immune to most German and Italian anti-tank guns. Only the 88mm FlaK gun and 50mm PaK 38 at close range can reliably penetrate its frontal armor. When hit by any gun smaller than 50mm (including the German 37mm PaK 36 and Italian 47mm), the Matilda receives an additional +2 armor save modifier.

**Slow and Ponderous**: The Matilda's maximum movement rate is reduced to 8" on roads and 6" cross-country. It may not perform breakthrough moves or pursuit actions. However, its mechanical reliability is excellent - it never suffers additional breakdown penalties.

**Limited Anti-Tank Capability**: Armed with the 2-pounder (40mm) gun, the Matilda has good penetration but limited high-explosive capability. When firing HE at infantry or soft targets, reduce effectiveness by -1 on hit rolls.

### Crusader Cruiser Tank

By Operation Crusader, the Crusader was more numerous but still suffered from mechanical problems in desert conditions.

**Fast Cruiser**: The Crusader has a movement rate of 16" on roads and 12" cross-country. It may perform breakthrough moves and benefits from +2" movement when conducting pursuit actions.

**Mechanically Unreliable**: At the start of each turn, roll a D6 for each Crusader. On a roll of 1, the tank suffers an engine breakdown and is immobilized for the rest of the game. Crew may bail out normally. This represents the chronic mechanical problems that plagued early Crusader models.

**Under-Gunned**: Most Crusaders mounted the 2-pounder gun without HE capability. The Crusader may only fire AP ammunition and may not engage infantry in the open effectively (treat as MG fire only). A few Crusader II models with 3" howitzers provide close support.

### Valentine Infantry Tank

The Valentine was beginning to appear in North Africa by late 1941, supplementing the Matilda.

**Good Armor**: The Valentine has solid frontal armor, proof against most German guns except the 50mm and 88mm. It receives +1 to armor saves against 37mm and 47mm guns.

**Slow But Steady**: Movement rate of 10" on roads, 8" cross-country. The Valentine is more reliable than the Crusader - add +1 to breakdown rolls.

**2-Pounder Armed**: Like most British tanks of this period, the Valentine mounts the 2-pounder gun with no HE capability.

### Stuart (Honey) Light Tank

American-supplied Stuart light tanks, called "Honey" by British crews for their reliability.

**Reliable**: The Stuart was notably reliable compared to British tanks. It never suffers breakdown penalties and adds +1 to any reliability rolls.

**Fast**: Movement rate of 16" on roads, 12" cross-country. May perform reconnaissance and pursuit actions.

**37mm Gun**: The American 37mm gun is adequate against early Panzers but struggles with improved armor. Effective against Panzer II and III (early models), less effective against Panzer III (late) and IV.

**Light Armor**: The Stuart's thin armor is vulnerable to all German anti-tank weapons. It receives -1 to armor saves but its speed often protects it.

## British Anti-Tank Guns

### 2-Pounder (40mm) Anti-Tank Gun

The standard British anti-tank gun of 1941, still effective against most German tanks.

**Good Penetration**: The 2-pounder has excellent penetration at normal combat ranges (0-30"). Against Panzer III and IV models of this period, it penetrates reliably at ranges under 20".

**No HE Capability**: The 2-pounder cannot fire high-explosive ammunition. It may not engage infantry in the open or soft targets effectively.

**Portee Mounting**: When mounted on a truck (Portee), the 2-pounder can move at truck speed (18" road, 12" cross-country) but must dismount to fire. Dismounting takes a full action.

### 25-Pounder Gun-Howitzer

Britain's excellent field gun, seeing increased use in the anti-tank role.

**Dual Purpose**: The 25-pounder may fire as field artillery (indirect HE) or as an anti-tank gun (direct AP). Switching between roles requires one full action.

**Effective HE**: When firing HE, the 25-pounder uses the standard field artillery rules with a 3" burst radius. It can lay smoke screens effectively.

**Adequate AP**: When firing AP ammunition in direct fire mode, the 25-pounder can penetrate Panzer III and IV at ranges up to 20".

### 6-Pounder (57mm) Anti-Tank Gun

The new 6-pounder was just beginning to appear during Crusader, a significant improvement over the 2-pounder.

**Powerful**: The 6-pounder can penetrate all German tanks of this period at normal ranges. It receives +2 to penetration rolls compared to the 2-pounder.

**Limited Availability**: Only a handful of 6-pounders reached North Africa by November 1941. They are allocated as special support weapons in scenarios.

**HE Available**: Unlike the 2-pounder, the 6-pounder eventually received HE ammunition, though this was not widely available during Crusader.

## German Armor

### Panzer III (Ausf H/J/L models)

The Afrika Korps' main battle tank, now with improved 50mm gun on later models.

**Well-Balanced Design**: The Panzer III represents excellent balance of firepower, armor, and mobility. It receives no penalties for mixed terrain.

**Veteran Crews**: German panzer crews were highly trained. All Panzer III tanks receive a +1 bonus to shooting when firing first in an engagement.

**50mm L/42 Gun** (Early models): Effective against all British tanks except Matilda II. Against Matildas, must close to within 10" or target side/rear armor.

**50mm L/60 Gun** (Ausf J Special, L): The longer 50mm gun significantly improved penetration. These models can engage Matilda IIs at ranges up to 20" with chance of penetration.

**Spaced Armor** (Some Ausf J): Some Panzer IIIs received additional armor plates. These models receive +1 to armor saves against British 2-pounder guns.

### Panzer IV (Ausf E/F models)

The close-support tank now being up-armored and up-gunned.

**Infantry Support Role**: The Panzer IV (short 75mm) is primarily an HE platform. When firing HE, it receives a +1 bonus to hit rolls against infantry and soft targets.

**Limited Anti-Tank**: The short 75mm gun has poor armor penetration. When firing AP ammunition, the Panzer IV suffers a -2 penalty to penetration rolls against armored targets.

**Improved Armor** (Ausf F): Late model Panzer IVs received additional frontal armor. Ausf F models receive +1 to frontal armor saves.

### Panzer II Light Tank

Still used for reconnaissance despite being obsolete for tank combat.

**Fast Reconnaissance**: Movement rate of 16" on roads and 12" cross-country. May perform reconnaissance actions and benefits from +2" when withdrawing.

**Light Armor**: Vulnerable to all British anti-tank weapons. Receives -1 penalty to all armor saves.

**20mm Autocannon**: Effective against soft targets and infantry but cannot engage tanks. Treat as heavy MG fire.

## German Anti-Tank Guns

### 88mm FlaK 18/36/37 Anti-Aircraft/Anti-Tank Gun

The legendary "88" remained the most feared weapon in North Africa.

**Devastating Power**: The 88mm can penetrate any British tank at ranges up to 40". When hitting a Matilda II or Valentine, the 88mm receives a +3 bonus to penetration rolls.

**Long Range**: May engage targets at ranges up to 60" (compared to standard AT gun range of 40"). Ignores range penalties for the first 40".

**High Profile**: Difficult to conceal. May not claim soft cover bonuses. Enemy artillery observers receive +1 to spot 88mm positions.

**Dual Purpose**: May engage aircraft as well as ground targets. Switching between AA and AT modes requires a full action.

**Limited Availability**: Carefully deployed valuable asset. Usually allocated as special support with high point costs.

### 50mm PaK 38 Anti-Tank Gun

Germany's standard anti-tank gun by late 1941, now more common than the 37mm.

**Good Penetration**: Can penetrate all British Cruiser tanks at normal ranges (up to 30"). Against Matilda II and Valentine, requires close range (under 15") or side/rear shots.

**Mobile**: Relatively light and mobile. May be towed by light vehicles and deployed quickly (one action to unlimber).

**APCR Available**: Some guns had access to limited APCR ammunition for enhanced penetration (see Special Ammunition section).

### 37mm PaK 36 Anti-Tank Gun

Germany's obsolete pre-war gun, being phased out but still in service.

**Obsolete**: Cannot penetrate Matilda II or Valentine armor at any frontal angle. May only attempt side and rear shots at close range (under 10").

**Adequate Against Light Armor**: Remains effective against British Cruisers and Stuart tanks at ranges up to 20".

**Cheap and Plentiful**: Reduced points cost reflecting limited capability.

## Italian Armor

### M13/40 Medium Tank

Italy's main battle tank, adequate in 1940 but increasingly outclassed by late 1941.

**Dual Machine Guns**: Twin hull-mounted machine guns provide additional anti-infantry capability.

**47mm Gun**: Limited armor penetration. Can engage Crusaders and Stuarts at normal ranges but struggles against Valentines and Matildas.

**Sand Filter Problems**: At game start, roll D6 for each M13/40. On 1-2, reduce movement by -4" for entire game due to engine sand ingestion.

**Riveted Construction**: When penetrated, add +1 to crew casualty rolls due to armor spalling.

### M14/41 Medium Tank

An improved version of the M13/40 with better engine and air filtration.

**Improved Reliability**: The M14/41 does not suffer the sand filter problems of the M13/40. Use standard breakdown rules.

**Otherwise Identical**: All other characteristics match the M13/40. The improved engine provides slightly better speed but this is not reflected significantly in game terms.

### Semovente 47/32 Self-Propelled Gun

Italy's first self-propelled anti-tank gun, mounting the 47mm on an M14/41 chassis.

**Low Profile**: The Semovente's low silhouette provides concealment benefits. It receives +1 to cover saves when hull-down.

**Limited Traverse**: The fixed casemate gun has limited traverse (30° each side). The vehicle must be oriented toward target for effective firing.

**47mm Gun**: Use standard Italian 47mm anti-tank gun characteristics (limited penetration, adequate against light armor).

## Italian Anti-Tank Guns

### 47mm 47/32 Anti-Tank Gun

Italy's standard anti-tank gun, increasingly inadequate by late 1941.

**Limited Penetration**: Can penetrate British Cruisers and Stuarts at ranges up to 20". Cannot penetrate Valentine or Matilda frontal armor at any range - only side/rear shots at close range have any chance.

**Reliable**: Mechanically reliable and easy to handle. Requires only one action to unlimber.

**Common**: Widely available with reduced points cost.

### Cannone da 75/27 modello 06/11 Field Gun

An old WWI-era field gun pressed into anti-tank service.

**Obsolete Design**: This gun was designed in 1906 and is completely obsolete by 1941. When used in AT role, it suffers -2 to hit rolls against moving targets.

**Adequate HE**: When used for indirect fire HE, it performs adequately as a light field gun.

**Emergency AT Only**: Should only engage tanks in emergency situations. Even then, only effective against light armor at close range.

## Special Ammunition

### Armor-Piercing Composite Rigid (APCR)

Limited quantities of tungsten-cored ammunition available to some German guns.

**Limited Supply**: Units with APCR may fire up to 3 shots per game. Track usage carefully.

**Superior Penetration**: APCR adds +2 to penetration rolls at ranges up to 30". Beyond 30", it provides no bonus.

**Availability**: Only German 50mm PaK 38 and Panzer III Ausf J Special/L had access to APCR during Crusader period.

### Smoke Rounds

**Available**: British 25-pounders (plentiful), German 50mm guns (limited), Italian 47mm guns (very limited).

**Effect**: Creates 6" diameter smoke screen blocking line of sight. Dissipates at end of turn after next (remains two full turns).

**Limited Supply**: Each gun may fire maximum of 2 smoke rounds per game unless scenario specifies otherwise.

## Vehicle Reliability

### Breakdown Rules

Desert conditions caused significant mechanical failures.

**Breakdown Test**: When vehicle moves at full speed or over rough terrain, roll D6. On 1, the vehicle suffers breakdown:
- **Minor Breakdown** (D6 roll 1-4): Immobilized for D3 turns
- **Major Breakdown** (D6 roll 5-6): Permanently immobilized

**Reliability Modifiers**:
- British Matilda II: +1 (more reliable)
- British Crusader: -1 (notoriously unreliable)
- American Stuart: +1 (notably reliable)
- Italian M13/40: -1 in extreme heat (sand filter problems)
- Italian M14/41: No modifier (improved filtration)
- German vehicles: No modifier (standard reliability)

### Recovery and Repair

**Field Repairs**: Immobilized vehicles may attempt repair during rally phase. Unit with mechanics/engineers within 4" may assist. Roll D6: on 5+, vehicle repaired and may move next turn at half speed.

**Recovery Vehicles**: Dedicated recovery vehicles may tow immobilized vehicles at half movement rate. Recovered vehicle removed from play.

## Aircraft Special Rules

### Stuka Dive Bomber (Ju-87)

**Precision Bomber**: +1 to hit rolls when dive bombing stationary or slow-moving targets.

**Siren Effect**: Enemy units within 12" of target must take morale check at -1.

**Vulnerable**: Highly vulnerable to fighters. Shot down on 4+ by any fighter aircraft.

### Hurricane/Kittyhawk Fighter-Bomber

**Ground Attack**: +1 to hit rolls against soft targets and unarmored vehicles.

**Air Superiority**: May intercept bombers. Roll opposed D6 - higher roll drives off opponent.

**Limited Bomb Load**: Each aircraft may make only 1 attack run per game.

### Blenheim Light Bomber

**Medium Bomber**: Carries heavier bomb load than fighters. Uses medium bomber attack profile.

**Vulnerable**: Slow and vulnerable to fighters. Shot down on 3+ by enemy fighters.

**Formation Flying**: If 2+ Blenheims attack same target, they receive +1 to hit rolls (representing concentrated bombing).

## Transport and Supply

### Truck Vulnerability

**No Armor Save**: Trucks do not receive armor saves. Any hit automatically damages vehicle.

**Cargo Loss**: If truck carrying supplies/troops destroyed, cargo lost. Passengers roll for casualties (treat as under MG fire).

**Fast Movement**: 18" on roads, 12" cross-country. May not enter rough terrain without immobilization test.

### Supply Considerations

**Water**: Units cut off from water for 2+ turns suffer -1 to all actions (cumulative).

**Fuel**: Track fuel points as specified in scenarios. Vehicles without fuel are immobilized.

**Ammunition**: Heavy weapons may have limited ammunition. Track expenditure per scenario rules.

## Terrain and Equipment Interaction

### Wadis and Steep Slopes

**Vehicle Restrictions**: Only tracked vehicles may attempt wadi crossing. Wheeled vehicles must use designated crossings.

**Crossing Test**: Tracked vehicles crossing wadi roll D6. On 1-2, vehicle stuck and immobilized.

### Escarpments

**Impassable**: Impassable to all vehicles except at marked passes.

**Observation**: Units on top receive +6" to spotting range.

**Reverse Slope**: Units on reverse slope receive hard cover and may not be seen until enemy has direct line of sight.
