The Battle of Gazala

Orders of Battle - The Battle of Gazala

British/Commonwealth Forces (May-June 1942)

Commander-in-Chief Middle East: General Sir Claude Auchinleck
Eighth Army Commander: Lieutenant-General Neil Ritchie (relieved after Gazala; Auchinleck assumed direct command)

XIII Corps (Northern/Coastal Sector)

Commander: Lieutenant-General William Gott

1st South African Division

Commander: Major-General Dan Pienaar

Composition:

  • 1st, 2nd, 3rd South African Infantry Brigades
  • Holding fortified "boxes" along northern coastal sector
  • Approximately 18,000 troops

50th Infantry Division (Northumbrian)

Commander: Major-General William Ramsden

Composition:

  • 69th, 150th, 151st Infantry Brigades
  • 150th Brigade Box in center of Gazala Line (destroyed May 29-June 1)
  • 69th and 151st Brigades in other boxes
  • Approximately 15,000 troops

1st Free French Brigade

Commander: Général de Brigade Pierre Koenig

Composition:

  • 13th Demi-Brigade of the Foreign Legion
  • 1st and 2nd Battalions Fusiliers Marins (Naval Infantry)
  • 1st Battalion Pacific (colonial troops)
  • Bir Hacheim fortified box—southern anchor of Gazala Line
  • Artillery and anti-tank guns
  • Approximately 3,600 troops (Foreign Legion, French, colonial)
  • Mission: Hold Bir Hacheim; accomplished for 14 days against overwhelming odds

XIII Corps Total: ~40,000 troops in fortified boxes

XXX Corps (Armored/Mobile Forces)

Commander: Lieutenant-General Willoughby Norrie

1st Armoured Division

Commander: Major-General Herbert Lumsden

Composition:

  • 2nd Armoured Brigade

    • The Queen's Bays (Grant medium tanks)
    • 9th Lancers (Grant medium tanks)
    • 10th Hussars (Grant medium tanks)

  • 22nd Armoured Brigade

    • 2nd Royal Gloucestershire Hussars (Crusader cruiser tanks)
    • 3rd County of London Yeomanry (Crusader)
    • 4th County of London Yeomanry (Crusader)

Tank Strength: Approximately 330 tanks

  • 167x M3 Grant medium tanks (75mm sponson gun + 37mm turret gun—NEW, game-changing firepower)
  • 163x Crusader II/III cruiser tanks (2-pounder or 6-pounder gun, mechanically unreliable)

7th Armoured Division (The "Desert Rats")

Commander: Major-General Frank Messervy

Composition:

  • 4th Armoured Brigade

    • 3rd Royal Tank Regiment (Grant tanks)
    • 5th Royal Tank Regiment (Grant tanks)
    • 8th Hussars (Honey Stuart)

  • 7th Motor Brigade (motorized infantry)

    • 2nd Rifle Brigade
    • Support units

Tank Strength: Approximately 240 tanks

  • 90x M3 Grant medium tanks
  • 110x Honey Stuart M3 light tanks (37mm gun, excellent reliability and reconnaissance)
  • 40x Valentine infantry tanks (2-pounder gun, slow but reliable)

32nd Army Tank Brigade (Infantry Support)

  • 4th and 7th Royal Tank Regiments
  • Approximately 110 Valentine and Matilda II infantry tanks
  • Close support for infantry divisions

XXX Corps Total: ~680 tanks, 25,000 troops

Reserve and Tobruk Garrison

2nd South African Division

  • Line of communication troops and reserves
  • Elements later trapped in Tobruk (33,000 total prisoners June 21)

4th Indian Division

  • Reserve, not heavily engaged in main battle

Tobruk Garrison (June 1942)

  • 2nd South African Division (majority)
  • 32nd Army Tank Brigade elements
  • British infantry units
  • Artillery and fortress troops
  • Approximately 35,000 troops total (captured June 21)

Desert Air Force

Commander: Air Vice-Marshal Arthur Coningham

Aircraft: Approximately 500-600 aircraft

  • Hurricane IIc fighters (4x 20mm cannon)
  • Kittyhawk fighters (American P-40)
  • Boston and Baltimore light bombers
  • Maintained air superiority initially, losses mounted during battle

Total Commonwealth Eighth Army Strength:

  • Personnel: ~110,000 troops
  • Tanks: 850 (including reserves and Tobruk)—680 with mobile corps, 110 infantry tanks, 60+ in reserve
  • Artillery: 600+ guns (primarily 25-pounders, 6-pounder anti-tank)
  • Aircraft: 500-600

Tank Types:

  • M3 Grant: 75mm M2 gun (sponson-mount, limited traverse), 37mm M6 gun (turret), 50mm frontal armor, American Lend-Lease, tall profile but effective firepower
  • Crusader II/III: 2-pounder (Mk II) or 6-pounder (Mk III), fast but mechanically unreliable, thin armor
  • Honey Stuart M3: 37mm gun, light tank, excellent reliability, superb reconnaissance
  • Valentine: 2-pounder gun, slow (15 mph), heavily armored, reliable infantry support
  • Matilda II: 2-pounder gun, very slow, thick armor, obsolescent by 1942

Axis Forces (May-June 1942)

Commander: Generaloberst Erwin Rommel (Panzerarmee Afrika)
(Promoted to Generalfeldmarschall—Field Marshal—June 22 after Tobruk capture)

German Forces - Afrika Korps

15th Panzer Division

Commander: Generalmajor Gustav von Vaerst

Composition:

  • 8th Panzer Regiment

    • I Battalion (Panzer III Ausf J/L long 50mm, Panzer IV Ausf F2 long 75mm)
    • II Battalion (Panzer III, Panzer IV F2)
    • Approximately 120 tanks operational

  • 104th and 115th Panzergrenadier Regiments

    • Motorized infantry
    • Approximately 4,000 men total

  • 33rd Artillery Regiment

    • 105mm leFH 18, 150mm sFH 18 howitzers
    • 88mm FlaK 18/36 batteries (anti-tank role)

  • 33rd Panzerjäger Battalion

    • 50mm PAK 38, PAK 40 75mm anti-tank guns (increasing numbers)

Division Strength: ~120 tanks, ~8,000 men

21st Panzer Division

Commander: Generalmajor Georg von Bismarck

Composition:

  • 5th Panzer Regiment

    • I Battalion (Panzer III Ausf J/L long 50mm, Panzer IV F2)
    • II Battalion (Panzer III, Panzer IV)
    • Approximately 130 tanks operational

  • 104th and 155th Rifle Regiments (motorized infantry)

    • Approximately 4,000 men

  • 155th Artillery Regiment

    • Mixed artillery, 88mm FlaK batteries

  • 39th Panzerjäger Battalion

    • PAK 38, PAK 40 anti-tank guns

Division Strength: ~130 tanks, ~8,000 men

90th Light Division (Afrika Division)

Commander: Generalmajor Ulrich Kleemann

Composition:

  • 155th, 200th, 361st Regiments (motorized infantry)
  • Reconnaissance battalion
  • Artillery and AT support
  • Elite desert fighters, highly mobile
  • Approximately 9,000 men, no tanks (pure motorized infantry)

German Tank Types:

  • Panzer III Ausf J/L: 50mm KwK 39 L/60 long gun, 50mm frontal armor, excellent anti-tank capability
  • Panzer IV Ausf F2: 75mm KwK 40 L/43 LONG GUN—game-changer, penetrates all British tanks at 2,000m, 50mm frontal armor, 5-man crew. This tank gave Germans decisive firepower advantage.
  • Panzer II: Light tank, 20mm gun, reconnaissance only

Critical German Assets:

  • 88mm FlaK 18/36/37: Still the ultimate anti-tank weapon; approximately 48 guns deployed
  • PAK 40 75mm: New towed AT gun, excellent penetration, increasing numbers (30-40 guns)
  • PAK 38 50mm: Standard AT gun, effective against most British tanks

Total German Strength: ~250 tanks (including reserves), ~29,000 men

Italian Forces

Ariete Armored Division

Commander: Generale Baldassarre

Composition:

  • 132nd Armored Regiment

    • Approximately 130 tanks/assault guns (M13/40, M14/41, Semovente 75/18)

  • 8th Bersaglieri Regiment (elite motorized infantry)

    • Approximately 2,500 men

  • 132nd Artillery Regiment

    • 75mm, 100mm guns, 47mm AT

Division Strength: ~130 armored vehicles, ~9,000 men
Performance: Heavily damaged attacking Bir Hacheim; Italian losses high

Littorio Armored Division (New—Italy's strongest)

Commander: Generale Bitossi

Composition:

  • 133rd Armored Regiment

    • Approximately 100 tanks (M14/41 medium tanks, Semovente 75/18, Semovente 105/25 assault guns)

  • 12th Bersaglieri Regiment (motorized infantry)

  • 133rd Artillery Regiment

Division Strength: ~100 armored vehicles, ~8,000 men

Trieste Motorized Division (101st)

Commander: Generale La Ferla

Composition:

  • 65th and 66th Infantry Regiments (truck-mobile)
  • Artillery and support
  • Approximately 11,000 men

Brescia, Pavia, Trento Infantry Divisions

  • Static infantry divisions
  • Defensive positions and siege lines
  • Approximately 24,000 men combined

Italian Tank Types:

  • M13/40: 47mm gun, 30mm armor, underpowered, unreliable (being phased out)
  • M14/41: Improved M13/40 with better engine, still inferior to Allied/German tanks
  • Semovente 75/18: Self-propelled 75mm gun, effective infantry support, low profile
  • Semovente 105/25: Self-propelled 105mm gun (limited numbers), powerful but slow

Total Italian Strength: ~41,000 men, ~230 tanks/assault guns

Commonwealth Advantages:

  • Numerical superiority (1.6:1 troops, 1.5:1 tanks)
  • M3 Grant 75mm gun matched German firepower (finally!)
  • Better artillery (numbers and supply)
  • Air superiority initially
  • Secure logistics
  • Fortified defensive positions (Gazala Line boxes)

Axis Advantages:

  • Panzer IV F2 long 75mm gun—decisive firepower advantage
  • Superior crew training and gunnery
  • Masterful combined-arms coordination (German forces)
  • Tactical leadership (Rommel at his peak)
  • Operational mobility and flexibility
  • Initiative (attacking vs. defending)

Critical Factors:

  • Panzer IV F2 Game-Changer: Long 75mm gun penetrated all British tanks at ranges British guns couldn't match; provided Germans decisive qualitative edge despite numerical inferiority
  • "Box" System Failure: British fortified boxes proved isolated and vulnerable; could be surrounded and reduced individually
  • Piecemeal British Commitment: British brigades continued attacking sequentially, allowing Germans to defeat each in turn
  • Crusader Unreliability: Mechanical failures plagued British armor throughout battle
  • Free French at Bir Hacheim: 14-day defense bought time but couldn't prevent strategic disaster

Special Engagements

The Cauldron (May 28-June 1)

  • Rommel trapped against minefields
  • German integrated defense defeated piecemeal British attacks
  • 150th Brigade Box destroyed
  • British lost 200+ tanks in Cauldron fighting

Bir Hacheim (May 27-June 11)

  • Free French 1st Brigade (3,600 men) held 14 days
  • Repulsed Italian Ariete and later German assaults
  • Evacuated successfully June 10-11 night breakout
  • Symbol of Free French resistance

Knightsbridge (June 5-13)

  • Series of massive tank battles
  • British 22nd Armoured Brigade virtually destroyed June 12
  • Grant tanks fought well but couldn't overcome German tactical superiority
  • British lost 200+ tanks; position became untenable

Tobruk (June 20-21)

  • Fell in single day (contrast to 242-day siege in 1941)
  • 33,000 prisoners (mostly 2nd South African Division)
  • 2,000 vehicles, massive stores captured
  • Worst British defeat since Singapore (February 1942)

Notes:

  • M3 Grant tank was stopgap pending M4 Sherman arrival (late 1942); 75mm gun effective but sponson mount limited traverse
  • Panzer IV F2 with long 75mm gave Germans first decisive firepower advantage in desert war
  • Crusader mechanical unreliability remained chronic problem; breakdown rate ~40-50%
  • Free French defense of Bir Hacheim among most heroic actions of North Africa campaign
  • British numerical advantages negated by continued doctrinal failures and German tactical superiority
