# Equipment Special Rules

This section provides special rules for key equipment types used during the Battle of Gazala. These rules reflect the unique characteristics and performance of weapons and vehicles in desert conditions during May-June 1942.

## British Armor

### Grant Medium Tank

The American-supplied Grant was a game-changer, mounting a powerful 75mm gun that could fire high-explosive ammunition.

**Dual Armament**: The Grant mounts both a 75mm gun in a sponson and a 37mm gun in the turret. It may fire both weapons in the same turn at different targets, or concentrate both on one target for +1 to hit.

**75mm HE Capability**: For the first time, British tank units had effective HE ammunition. The Grant's 75mm receives +1 to hit rolls against infantry and soft targets. This was a revolutionary capability for British armored units.

**High Profile**: The Grant's tall silhouette makes concealment difficult. It may not claim soft cover bonuses (only hard cover applies). Enemy spotters receive +1 to detect Grant tanks.

**Limited Traverse**: The 75mm sponson gun has limited traverse (30° each side). The tank must be oriented toward the target. The turret-mounted 37mm has full 360° traverse.

**Good Armor**: Solid frontal armor proof against most German guns except the 50mm L/60 at close range and the 88mm. Receives +1 to armor saves against 37mm and Italian 47mm guns.

**Reliable**: American-built reliability. Add +1 to all breakdown rolls.

### Crusader II/III Cruiser Tank

By mid-1942, the Crusader was the most numerous British tank but still mechanically problematic.

**Fast Cruiser**: Movement rate of 16" on roads and 12" cross-country. May perform breakthrough moves and pursuit actions with +2" bonus.

**Still Unreliable**: Roll D6 at start of each turn for each Crusader. On a 1, the tank suffers engine breakdown and is immobilized for the rest of the game.

**Crusader II**: Mounts 2-pounder gun (no HE capability) plus 3" howitzer for close support. May fire both weapons or choose one.

**Crusader III**: Armed with new 6-pounder (57mm) gun providing much better anti-tank capability. Can penetrate Panzer III and IV reliably at normal ranges. Still lacks HE ammunition.

### Valentine Infantry Tank

The Valentine was now the standard British infantry tank, replacing the Matilda.

**Good Armor**: Solid frontal armor. Receives +1 to armor saves against 37mm, 47mm, and 50mm L/42 guns.

**Slow But Steady**: Movement rate of 10" on roads, 8" cross-country. More reliable than Crusader - add +1 to breakdown rolls.

**6-Pounder Armed** (Valentine VIII and later): Most Valentines by Gazala mounted the 6-pounder gun, significantly improving anti-tank capability compared to the 2-pounder.

**Infantry Support**: Designed to support infantry. When operating within 6" of friendly infantry, both tank and infantry receive +1 to morale checks.

### Stuart (Honey) Light Tank

American Stuart tanks remained valuable for reconnaissance despite light armor.

**Reliable**: Never suffers breakdown penalties. Add +1 to all reliability rolls.

**Fast**: Movement rate of 16" on roads, 12" cross-country. Excellent for reconnaissance and pursuit.

**37mm Gun**: Adequate against Panzer II and early Panzer III, increasingly inadequate against improved German armor.

**Light Armor**: Vulnerable to all German anti-tank weapons. Receives -1 to armor saves.

**Scout**: When performing reconnaissance, the Stuart may make an extra 6" move before regular movement phase (represents aggressive scouting).

### Matilda II Infantry Tank

A few Matildas remained in service though being replaced by Valentines.

**Thick Armor**: Still immune to most Axis guns. Receives +2 armor save against guns smaller than 50mm.

**Very Slow**: Maximum movement rate 8" on roads, 6" cross-country. May not perform breakthrough or pursuit moves.

**Obsolescent**: By 1942, the Matilda's 2-pounder gun was clearly inadequate. Effective mainly in infantry support role.

## British Anti-Tank Guns

### 6-Pounder (57mm) Anti-Tank Gun

The 6-pounder had finally replaced the 2-pounder as the standard British anti-tank gun.

**Powerful**: Can penetrate all German tanks of this period at normal ranges (up to 30"). Receives +2 to penetration rolls compared to the 2-pounder.

**APDS Available** (Limited): A few guns had experimental armor-piercing discarding sabot ammunition. This provides +3 to penetration but only 2 rounds available per gun per game.

**HE Ammunition**: Unlike the 2-pounder, the 6-pounder could fire HE, though this was not always available. In scenarios where HE is available, the 6-pounder can engage infantry effectively.

**Portee Mounting**: Like the 2-pounder, some 6-pounders were mounted on trucks for mobility. Use standard Portee rules.

### 2-Pounder (40mm) Anti-Tank Gun

Some 2-pounders remained in service, particularly in defensive positions.

**Adequate**: Still effective against Panzer III (early models) and light armor, but struggles against improved German tanks.

**No HE Capability**: Cannot engage infantry or soft targets effectively.

**Being Phased Out**: Reduced points cost reflecting obsolescence.

### 25-Pounder Gun-Howitzer

Britain's versatile field gun, increasingly important in the anti-tank role.

**Dual Purpose**: May fire as field artillery (indirect HE) or anti-tank gun (direct AP). Switching requires one full action.

**Effective HE**: Standard field artillery with 3" burst radius. Excellent smoke capability.

**Adequate AP**: Can penetrate Panzer III and IV at ranges up to 20". At longer ranges, penetration becomes unreliable.

**Ubiquitous**: The most common British field gun, available in large numbers.

## German Armor

### Panzer III (Ausf J/L/M models)

Still the most common German tank, now with improved armor and longer gun.

**50mm L/60 Gun** (Ausf J Special, L, M): The longer 50mm gun provides excellent penetration. Can engage Grant and Valentine frontally at ranges up to 30", though close range preferred.

**Spaced Armor**: Many Panzer IIIs by mid-1942 had additional armor plates. These models receive +1 to armor saves.

**Veteran Crews**: Afrika Korps crews were highly experienced. All Panzer III tanks receive +1 to shooting when firing first in an engagement.

**Desert Modifications**: German tanks had excellent air filtration. No special breakdown penalties in desert conditions.

### Panzer IV (Ausf F2/G models) - "Long 75mm"

The game-changing Panzer IV with the long 75mm gun was appearing in small numbers by Gazala.

**Devastating Gun**: The 75mm L/43 gun (F2) or L/48 gun (G) could penetrate any British tank at normal combat ranges. Receives +2 to penetration rolls against all targets.

**Dual Capability**: Unlike the short 75mm, the long gun fires excellent AP and HE ammunition. No penalties when switching between ammunition types.

**Priority Target**: British forces quickly learned to prioritize these tanks. When spotted, all available AT assets must check if they can engage (represents tactical priority).

**Limited Numbers**: During Gazala, only a few long-barreled Panzer IVs were available. Allocate as special support units with premium point costs.

**Frontal Armor**: Improved frontal armor on late models. F2/G models receive +1 to frontal armor saves.

### Panzer IV (Ausf E/F1 models) - "Short 75mm"

The older infantry support version still equipped many units.

**Infantry Support**: The short 75mm excels at HE fire. Receives +1 to hit rolls against infantry and soft targets.

**Poor Anti-Tank**: The short 75mm has inadequate penetration against modern armor. Suffers -2 to penetration rolls against tanks. Should avoid tank combat.

**Vulnerable**: By 1942, the short Panzer IV's armor was barely adequate. The new British 6-pounder penetrates easily at normal ranges.

### Panzer II Light Tank

Obsolete but still used for reconnaissance and screening.

**Fast Reconnaissance**: Movement rate 16" on roads, 12" cross-country. +2" when withdrawing from combat.

**Light Armor**: Vulnerable to all British AT weapons. Receives -1 to all armor saves.

**20mm Autocannon**: Only effective against soft targets. Cannot engage tanks effectively.

**Disposable**: Reduced points cost reflecting role as expendable scout.

### Panzer III (Ausf N) Close Support

A variant mounting the short 75mm howitzer for infantry support.

**75mm Howitzer**: Uses same HE characteristics as short Panzer IV. Receives +1 to hit infantry and soft targets.

**Smoke Capability**: Can fire smoke rounds (up to 3 per game) for screening maneuvers.

**Limited Anti-Tank**: Like the short Panzer IV, avoid tank combat. The short 75mm is inadequate against British armor.

**Command Tank**: Often used by platoon/company commanders for its smoke and HE capability.

## German Anti-Tank Guns

### 88mm FlaK 18/36/37 Anti-Aircraft/Anti-Tank Gun

The legendary "88" remained dominant in the desert.

**Devastating Power**: Can penetrate any British tank at ranges up to 40". Receives +3 to penetration rolls against all targets.

**Long Range**: May engage targets up to 60". Ignores range penalties for first 40".

**High Profile**: Cannot claim soft cover bonuses. Enemy artillery observers receive +1 to spot.

**Dual Purpose**: May engage aircraft or ground targets. Switching requires full action.

**Extremely Valuable**: Premium point cost. Usually carefully sited in defensive positions or with mobile battle groups.

### 50mm PaK 38 Anti-Tank Gun

Germany's standard towed anti-tank gun by 1942.

**Good Penetration**: Effective against Crusaders and Stuarts at all ranges. Can penetrate Grants and Valentines at close-medium range (under 25").

**Mobile**: Light and easy to deploy. Requires only one action to unlimber.

**APCR Available**: Limited APCR ammunition provides +2 to penetration (up to 3 rounds per gun per game).

**Outclassed**: By mid-1942, even the 50mm was struggling with improved British armor. Being supplemented by the new PaK 40.

### 75mm PaK 40 Anti-Tank Gun

The new, powerful German anti-tank gun was beginning to appear by Gazala.

**Powerful**: Can penetrate all British tanks at normal ranges (up to 35"). Receives +2 to penetration rolls.

**Long Range**: Effective range of 45", significantly outranging British AT guns.

**Heavy**: Requires two actions to unlimber due to weight. Less mobile than 50mm PaK 38.

**Limited Availability**: Only small numbers available during Gazala. Allocate as special support weapons.

### 37mm PaK 36 Anti-Tank Gun

Completely obsolete by 1942, only remaining in secondary units.

**Obsolete**: Cannot penetrate any British tank except light Stuarts at close range. Essentially ineffective.

**Emergency Use Only**: Should only be included in scenarios representing depleted or second-line units.

**Very Cheap**: Minimal point cost reflecting uselessness against modern armor.

## Italian Armor

### M13/40 Medium Tank

Italy's main tank, now completely outclassed but still in service.

**Dual Machine Guns**: Twin hull MGs provide extra anti-infantry firepower.

**47mm Gun**: Inadequate against British armor. Can only penetrate Stuarts and Crusaders at close range. Cannot penetrate Grants or Valentines frontally.

**Sand Filter Problems**: At game start, roll D6 for each M13/40. On 1-2, reduce movement by -4" for entire game.

**Riveted Construction**: When penetrated, add +1 to crew casualty rolls.

**Obsolete**: By 1942, the M13/40 was hopelessly outclassed. Reduced point cost reflects this.

### M14/41 Medium Tank

Improved M13/40 with better engine but still inadequate.

**Better Reliability**: No sand filter problems. Use standard breakdown rules.

**Otherwise Identical**: All other characteristics match M13/40. Still obsolete by 1942 standards.

### Semovente 75/18 Self-Propelled Gun

Italy's assault gun, mounting a short 75mm howitzer on M14/41 chassis.

**75mm Howitzer**: Effective HE for infantry support. Receives +1 to hit infantry and soft targets.

**Limited Anti-Tank**: Short 75mm has poor armor penetration. Can engage light armor at close range only.

**Low Profile**: Receives +1 to cover saves when hull-down.

**Limited Traverse**: Fixed casemate gun has 30° traverse each side. Vehicle must face target.

**Support Role**: Best used as artillery support for infantry, not for tank combat.

### Semovente 47/32 Self-Propelled Gun

Self-propelled 47mm AT gun, mobile but still undergunned.

**47mm Gun**: Uses standard Italian 47mm characteristics (limited penetration).

**Low Profile**: Receives +1 to cover saves when hull-down.

**Mobile**: Can keep pace with mechanized forces, providing mobile AT capability.

**Inadequate**: By 1942, even this mobile version of the 47mm struggled against British armor.

## Italian Anti-Tank Guns

### 47mm 47/32 Anti-Tank Gun

Italy's standard AT gun, completely outclassed by 1942.

**Inadequate**: Cannot penetrate Grant or Valentine frontal armor. Only effective against light armor and side/rear shots at close range.

**Reliable**: Mechanically reliable. Requires only one action to unlimber.

**Obsolete**: Reduced point cost. Only useful in defensive positions where side shots might be possible.

### Cannone da 90/53 Anti-Aircraft/Anti-Tank Gun

Italy's answer to the 88mm, a powerful 90mm AA/AT gun.

**Powerful**: Can penetrate all British tanks at ranges up to 35". Receives +2 to penetration rolls.

**Long Range**: May engage targets up to 50". Ignores range penalties for first 35".

**Dual Purpose**: May engage aircraft or ground targets. Switching requires full action.

**High Profile**: Cannot claim soft cover. Enemy observers receive +1 to spot.

**Rare**: Very limited numbers available. Premium point cost when allocated.

### Cannone da 75/32 modello 37 Field Gun

Modern Italian 75mm field gun occasionally used in AT role.

**Field Gun Primary**: Designed for indirect HE fire. When used as AT gun, suffers -1 to hit moving targets.

**Adequate HE**: Effective as field artillery with standard characteristics.

**Poor Armor Penetration**: When firing AP, has limited effectiveness against modern armor. Can engage light tanks and AFVs only.

## Special Ammunition

### Armor-Piercing Composite Rigid (APCR)

**Limited Supply**: German 50mm and 75mm guns may have up to 3 APCR rounds per gun per game.

**Superior Penetration**: APCR adds +2 to penetration rolls at ranges up to 30". No bonus beyond 30".

**German Only**: British forces did not have APCR available during Gazala. Italian forces never received APCR.

### Armor-Piercing Discarding Sabot (APDS)

**Experimental**: A few British 6-pounder guns had experimental APDS ammunition.

**Excellent Penetration**: APDS adds +3 to penetration rolls at any range up to 40".

**Extremely Limited**: Only 2 rounds per gun per game. Only available if specifically noted in scenario special rules.

### Smoke Rounds

**British**: 25-pounders have plentiful smoke. May fire up to 4 smoke rounds per gun per game.

**German**: 50mm and 75mm guns have limited smoke (2 rounds per gun per game).

**Italian**: Very limited smoke capability (1 round per gun per game).

**Effect**: Creates 6" diameter smoke screen blocking line of sight for two full turns.

## Vehicle Reliability

### Breakdown Rules

**Breakdown Test**: When vehicle moves at full speed or over rough terrain, roll D6. On 1, vehicle suffers breakdown.

**Reliability Modifiers**:
- American tanks (Grant, Stuart): +1 (notably reliable)
- British Crusader: -1 (notoriously unreliable)
- British Valentine: +1 (reliable)
- British Matilda: +1 (very reliable)
- German vehicles: No modifier (good filtration)
- Italian M13/40: -1 in extreme heat
- Italian M14/41: No modifier (improved)

### Recovery

**Field Repairs**: During rally phase, roll D6 for immobilized vehicle with mechanics/engineers within 4". On 5+, vehicle repaired.

**Recovery Vehicles**: May tow immobilized vehicles at half speed. Towed vehicle removed from play.

## Aircraft Special Rules

### Stuka Dive Bomber (Ju-87)

**Precision Bomber**: +1 to hit stationary or slow targets.

**Siren Effect**: Enemy within 12" of target must pass morale check at -1.

**Vulnerable**: Shot down on 4+ by fighters.

### Bf-109 Fighter

**Air Superiority**: German fighter dominance was strong during Gazala. Bf-109 receives +1 in air combat.

**Ground Attack**: May strafe with cannon fire, though this is not primary role.

### Hurricane/Kittyhawk Fighter-Bomber

**Ground Attack**: +1 to hit soft targets.

**Outclassed**: Bf-109 was superior. RAF fighters receive -1 when engaged by Bf-109 in air combat.

**Limited Bomb Load**: One attack run per game.

### Baltimore Medium Bomber

**Medium Bomber**: Heavy bomb load. Uses medium bomber attack profile.

**Formation Bombing**: If 2+ bombers attack same target, receive +1 to hit.

**Fighter Cover**: Baltimore formations often had fighter escort. May include fighters if specified in scenario.

## Transport and Supply

### Truck Vulnerability

**No Armor**: Any hit automatically damages truck.

**Cargo Loss**: Destroyed truck loses cargo. Passengers take casualties as if under MG fire.

**Movement**: 18" road, 12" cross-country.

### Supply Rules

**Water**: Units cut off from water for 2+ turns suffer -1 to all actions (cumulative).

**Fuel**: Track fuel points per scenario. Vehicles without fuel immobilized.

**Ammunition**: Heavy weapons may have limited ammunition. Track expenditure per scenario.

**"The Cauldron"**: Special supply rules may apply for units in the Cauldron position, reflecting the intense fighting and supply difficulties in this area.

## Terrain Interaction

### Wadis

**Vehicle Restrictions**: Only tracked vehicles may cross. Wheeled vehicles need designated crossings.

**Crossing Test**: Tracked vehicles roll D6. On 1-2, immobilized.

### Escarpments

**Impassable**: Except at designated passes.

**Observation**: Units on top receive +6" spotting range.

**Reverse Slope**: Hard cover, cannot be seen without direct LOS.

### Minefields

Gazala featured extensive British minefields.

**Movement**: Units entering minefield must test. Roll D6: on 1-2, unit stops and takes D3 casualties.

**Clearing**: Engineer teams can clear 6" lane in D3 turns.

**Marking**: Cleared lanes should be marked for subsequent movement.
