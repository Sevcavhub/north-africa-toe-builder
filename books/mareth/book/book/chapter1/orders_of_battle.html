Battle of Mareth Line

Orders of Battle - Battle of Mareth Line

British/Commonwealth Forces (March 1943)

Commander: General Sir Archibald Wavell (relieved after battle)
Tactical Commander: Lieutenant-General Noel Beresford-Peirse

7th Armoured Division (The "Desert Rats")

Commander: Major-General Sir Michael O'Moore Creagh

Composition:

  • 4th Armoured Brigade

    • 8th Hussars (Crusader I cruiser tanks)
    • 3rd Royal Tank Regiment (Matilda II infantry tanks)
    • 5th Royal Tank Regiment (Matilda II infantry tanks)
    • 1st Royal Horse Artillery (25-pounder guns)

  • 7th Armoured Brigade

    • 2nd Royal Tank Regiment (Crusader I, A9/A10 cruiser tanks)
    • 6th Royal Tank Regiment (Crusader I cruiser tanks)
    • 4th County of London Yeomanry (Crusader I)

  • 7th Support Group

    • 1st King's Royal Rifle Corps (motorized infantry)
    • 2nd Rifle Brigade (motorized infantry)
    • 3rd Royal Horse Artillery (25-pounder guns, portée)

Tank Strength: Approximately 190 tanks

  • 50x Matilda II (Infantry Tank Mk II) - heavy armor, 2-pounder gun, slow
  • 80x Crusader I - cruiser tank, 2-pounder gun, mechanically unreliable
  • 40x Honey Stuart M3 (Lend-Lease from USA) - light tank, 37mm gun, fast
  • 20x A9/A10 Cruiser tanks - older designs, limited capability

4th Indian Division

Commander: Major-General Frank Messervy

Composition:

  • 11th Indian Infantry Brigade

    • 2nd Queen's Own Cameron Highlanders
    • 1st/6th Rajputana Rifles
    • 2nd/5th Mahratta Light Infantry

  • Divisional Artillery

    • 1st Field Regiment (25-pounder guns)
    • 2nd Field Regiment (25-pounder guns)
    • 144th Field Regiment (25-pounder guns)

  • Divisional Support

    • 18th Indian Cavalry (reconnaissance)
    • Anti-tank batteries (2-pounder guns)

Strength: Approximately 8,000 infantry, 72x 25-pounder field guns, 24x 2-pounder anti-tank guns

Army Troops

  • 22nd Guards Brigade
    • 3rd Coldstream Guards
    • Elements of other Guards battalions

Total British Strength: Approximately 25,000 men, 190 tanks, 100+ guns

Axis Forces (March 1943)

Commander: Generalleutnant Erwin Rommel (Afrika Korps)

German Forces - Afrika Korps

15th Panzer Division

Commander: Generalmajor Walther Neumann-Silkow

Composition:

  • 8th Panzer Regiment

    • I Battalion (Panzer III Ausf F/G/H with 50mm KwK 38 L/42 gun)
    • II Battalion (Panzer III, some Panzer IV Ausf D/E with short 75mm)
    • Approximately 80 medium tanks operational

  • 104th Panzergrenadier Regiment

    • I Battalion (motorized infantry)
    • II Battalion (motorized infantry)
    • Strength: ~1,800 men

  • 115th Panzergrenadier Regiment

    • Similar composition to 104th

  • 33rd Artillery Regiment

    • I Battalion (105mm leFH 18 howitzers)
    • II Battalion (150mm sFH 18 howitzers)
    • III Battalion (includes 88mm FlaK 18/36 guns in anti-tank role)
    • Critical Asset: 8-12x 88mm FlaK guns deployed in anti-tank ambush positions

  • 33rd Panzerjäger Battalion

    • 50mm PAK 38 anti-tank guns
    • 37mm PAK 36 anti-tank guns (limited effectiveness)

Total Division Strength: Approximately 80 tanks, 6,000 men

5th Light Division (later redesignated 21st Panzer Division)

Commander: Generalmajor Johann von Ravenstein

Composition:

  • 5th Panzer Regiment

    • I Battalion (Panzer III Ausf F/G/H)
    • II Battalion (Panzer II light tanks, Panzer III)
    • Approximately 50-70 tanks operational

  • 104th Rifle Regiment (motorized infantry)

    • 2 battalions, approximately 1,500 men

  • 155th Motorized Artillery Regiment

    • Mixed 105mm, 150mm artillery
    • Some 88mm FlaK guns

  • 39th Panzerjäger Battalion

    • 50mm PAK 38 anti-tank guns
    • 47mm PAK(t) captured Czech guns

Total Division Strength: Approximately 50-70 tanks, 5,000 men

German Tank Types:

  • Panzer III Ausf F/G/H: 50mm KwK 38 L/42 gun, 30mm frontal armor, 40 km/h speed, 5-man crew
  • Panzer IV Ausf D/E: 75mm KwK 37 L/24 (short barrel, HE focus), 30mm frontal armor, infantry support role
  • Panzer II: 20mm automatic cannon, reconnaissance/light tank, limited combat value against British armor

German Anti-Tank Assets (Critical):

  • 88mm FlaK 18/36: 88mm gun, penetrates all British armor at 2,000m+ range, devastating effectiveness
  • 50mm PAK 38: Standard anti-tank gun, effective against Crusaders, struggles against Matilda II
  • 37mm PAK 36: Obsolete, ineffective against British heavy armor

Italian Forces

Ariete Armored Division

Commander: Generale di Divisione Baldassarre

Composition:

  • 132nd Armored Regiment

    • I Battalion (M13/40 medium tanks)
    • II Battalion (M13/40 medium tanks)
    • Approximately 60-70 M13/40 tanks operational

  • 8th Bersaglieri Regiment (motorized infantry)

    • 2 battalions, approximately 1,200 men

  • 132nd Artillery Regiment

    • 75mm and 100mm guns

M13/40 Tank Specifications:

  • 47mm gun (limited penetration)
  • 30mm frontal armor (vulnerable to British 2-pounder)
  • Underpowered, mechanically unreliable
  • 4-man crew

Bologna Infantry Division

Commander: (Italian garrison units under German tactical control at Fort Capuzzo)

Composition:

  • 2-3 infantry regiments
  • Defensive positions at Fort Capuzzo, Bardia, and along frontier
  • Approximately 6,000-8,000 men
  • Supporting artillery (75mm, 100mm guns)

Total Italian Strength: Approximately 10,000 men, 60-70 tanks (M13/40)

British Advantages:

  • Numerical parity or slight superiority in tanks
  • Matilda II nearly immune to most German guns (except 88mm)
  • Better artillery (25-pounder range and effectiveness)
  • Control of air reconnaissance (RAF Desert Air Force)

Axis Advantages:

  • 88mm FlaK guns in anti-tank role (decisive advantage)
  • Superior crew training and gunnery
  • Better combined-arms coordination
  • Defensive terrain advantage (fortified positions at Halfaya, Capuzzo, Sollum)
  • Superior tactical leadership (Rommel)
  • Better mechanical reliability (German tanks more reliable than Crusaders)

Strategic Context:

  • British: Relieving Tobruk, regaining initiative, testing new tank reinforcements
  • Axis: Holding frontier, maintaining Tobruk siege, preserving forces for future operations

Notes:

  • Tank numbers represent operational strength at start of battle; mechanical breakdowns reduced effective British strength significantly
  • German 88mm deployment (8-12 guns at Halfaya Pass and other positions) proved decisive despite small numbers
  • British numerical advantage negated by poor combined-arms coordination and German defensive tactics
  • Italian forces primarily in garrison/defensive roles; M13/40 tanks relegated to secondary positions
