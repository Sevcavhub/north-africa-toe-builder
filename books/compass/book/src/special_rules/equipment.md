# Equipment Special Rules

This section provides special rules for key equipment types used during Operation Compass. These rules reflect the unique characteristics and performance of weapons and vehicles in desert conditions during December 1940 - February 1941.

## British Armor

### Matilda II Infantry Tank

The Matilda II was the most heavily armored British tank in North Africa during 1941, virtually immune to most Axis anti-tank weapons.

**Thick Armor**: The Matilda II has armor values that make it immune to most German and Italian anti-tank guns. Only the 88mm FlaK gun and 50mm PaK 38 at close range can reliably penetrate its frontal armor. When hit by any gun smaller than 50mm (including the German 37mm PaK 36 and Italian 47mm), the Matilda receives an additional +2 armor save modifier.

**Slow and Ponderous**: The Matilda's maximum movement rate is reduced to 8" on roads and 6" cross-country. It may not perform breakthrough moves or pursuit actions. However, its mechanical reliability is excellent - it never suffers additional breakdown penalties.

**Limited Anti-Tank Capability**: Armed with the 2-pounder (40mm) gun, the Matilda has good penetration but limited high-explosive capability. When firing HE at infantry or soft targets, reduce effectiveness by -1 on hit rolls.

### Crusader Cruiser Tank

The Crusader was the fastest British tank, designed for exploitation and pursuit, but suffered from serious mechanical problems in desert conditions.

**Fast Cruiser**: The Crusader has a movement rate of 16" on roads and 12" cross-country. It may perform breakthrough moves and benefits from +2" movement when conducting pursuit actions.

**Mechanically Unreliable**: At the start of each turn, roll a D6 for each Crusader. On a roll of 1, the tank suffers an engine breakdown and is immobilized for the rest of the game. Crew may bail out normally. This represents the chronic mechanical problems that plagued early Crusader models.

**Under-Gunned**: Early Crusaders mounted the 2-pounder gun without HE capability. The Crusader may only fire AP ammunition and may not engage infantry in the open effectively (treat as MG fire only).

### A9/A10/A13 Cruiser Tanks

These earlier cruiser tanks were being phased out but still saw action during Operation Compass.

**Outdated Armor**: These tanks have thin armor vulnerable to all German anti-tank guns. They receive no special armor bonuses and suffer -1 to armor saves when hit by any gun 37mm or larger.

**Vulnerable to Heat**: In scenarios with extreme heat conditions, these tanks suffer an additional -1 to breakdown rolls due to inadequate cooling systems.

## British Anti-Tank Guns

### 2-Pounder (40mm) Anti-Tank Gun

The standard British anti-tank gun of 1941, effective against early Panzers but struggling against improved German armor.

**Good Penetration**: The 2-pounder has excellent penetration at normal combat ranges (0-30"). Against Panzer III and IV models of this period, it penetrates reliably at ranges under 20".

**No HE Capability**: The 2-pounder cannot fire high-explosive ammunition. It may not engage infantry in the open or soft targets effectively. This was a critical limitation that affected British tactical flexibility.

**Portee Mounting**: When mounted on a truck (Portee), the 2-pounder can move at truck speed (18" road, 12" cross-country) but must dismount to fire. Dismounting takes a full action. While mounted, the gun and crew are vulnerable (use truck armor values).

### 25-Pounder Gun-Howitzer

Britain's excellent field gun, capable of both indirect artillery fire and direct anti-tank engagement.

**Dual Purpose**: The 25-pounder may fire as field artillery (indirect HE) or as an anti-tank gun (direct AP). Switching between roles requires one full action.

**Effective HE**: When firing HE, the 25-pounder uses the standard field artillery rules with a 3" burst radius. It can lay smoke screens effectively.

**Adequate AP**: When firing AP ammunition in direct fire mode, the 25-pounder can penetrate Panzer III and IV at ranges up to 20". Beyond this range, penetration becomes unreliable (-2 to penetration rolls).

**Portee Mobility**: Like the 2-pounder, some 25-pounders were mounted on trucks for mobile operations. Use the same Portee rules as above.

## German Armor

### Panzer III (Ausf F/G/H/J models)

The workhorse of the Afrika Korps panzer divisions, combining good protection, firepower, and mobility.

**Well-Balanced Design**: The Panzer III represents the best combination of firepower, armor, and mobility available to German forces in 1941. It receives no penalties for mixed terrain and maintains full effectiveness in all conditions.

**Veteran Crews**: German panzer crews were highly trained and experienced. All Panzer III tanks receive a +1 bonus to shooting when firing first in an engagement (representing superior crew training and gunnery skills).

**50mm Gun Effectiveness**: The 50mm KwK 38 L/42 gun (later models) is effective against all British tanks except the Matilda II. When engaging Matilda IIs, the Panzer III must close to within 10" for reliable penetration, or target side/rear armor.

**37mm Gun Limitations**: Early Panzer III models (Ausf F/G) armed with the 37mm gun struggle against British Matildas. These models may not penetrate Matilda frontal armor at any range - they must target side or rear armor or use other tactics.

### Panzer IV (Ausf D/E models)

The close-support tank of panzer divisions, armed with a short 75mm gun primarily for infantry support.

**Infantry Support Role**: The Panzer IV (short 75mm) is primarily an HE platform. When firing HE, it receives a +1 bonus to hit rolls against infantry and soft targets due to its excellent close-support capabilities.

**Limited Anti-Tank**: The short 75mm gun has poor armor penetration. When firing AP ammunition, the Panzer IV suffers a -2 penalty to penetration rolls against armored targets. It should not engage British Cruiser or Matilda tanks unless absolutely necessary.

**Solid Armor**: The Panzer IV has good frontal armor, proof against the 2-pounder at longer ranges (20"+). At close range (under 10"), the 2-pounder can penetrate with good hits.

### Panzer II Light Tank

Used primarily for reconnaissance and infantry support, the Panzer II was outclassed by 1941 but remained in service.

**Fast Reconnaissance**: The Panzer II has a movement rate of 16" on roads and 12" cross-country. It may perform reconnaissance actions and benefits from +2" when withdrawing from combat.

**Light Armor**: The Panzer II's thin armor is vulnerable to all British anti-tank weapons, including the 2-pounder. It receives a -1 penalty to all armor saves.

**20mm Autocannon**: The 20mm gun is effective against soft targets and infantry but cannot engage tanks effectively. Treat as heavy MG fire with armor-piercing capability against armored cars only.

## German Anti-Tank Guns

### 88mm FlaK 18/36 Anti-Aircraft/Anti-Tank Gun

The legendary "88" - the most feared anti-tank weapon in North Africa, capable of destroying any Allied tank at extreme ranges.

**Devastating Power**: The 88mm FlaK gun can penetrate any British tank at ranges up to 40". When hitting a Matilda II (the only British tank with significant armor), the 88mm receives a +3 bonus to penetration rolls, effectively negating the Matilda's armor advantage.

**Long Range**: The 88mm may engage targets at ranges up to 60" (compared to standard AT gun range of 40"). It ignores range penalties for the first 40" of range.

**High Profile**: The 88mm is a large, high-profile weapon that is difficult to conceal. It may not claim cover bonuses from terrain that provides soft cover to other guns (only hard cover applies). Enemy artillery observers receive +1 to spot 88mm positions.

**Dual Purpose**: The 88mm may engage aircraft as well as ground targets. When firing at aircraft, use standard AA rules. Switching between AA and AT modes requires a full action.

**Limited Availability**: The 88mm was a valuable asset carefully deployed. In scenarios, 88mm guns are usually allocated as special support assets with point costs reflecting their dominance.

### 50mm PaK 38 Anti-Tank Gun

Germany's modern anti-tank gun, replacing the inadequate 37mm PaK 36.

**Good Penetration**: The 50mm PaK 38 can penetrate all British Cruiser tanks at normal ranges (up to 30"). Against the Matilda II, it requires close range (under 15") or side/rear shots for reliable penetration.

**Mobile**: The 50mm PaK 38 is relatively light and mobile. It may be towed by light vehicles and deployed quickly (takes one action to unlimber, compared to two actions for heavier guns).

**Modern Design**: This gun represents the effective answer to improved Allied armor. It receives no penalties for engaging moving targets at ranges under 20".

### 37mm PaK 36 Anti-Tank Gun

Germany's standard pre-war anti-tank gun, nicknamed "Door Knocker" by troops due to its ineffectiveness against heavy armor.

**Obsolete**: The 37mm PaK 36 cannot penetrate Matilda II armor at any range. Against Matilda IIs, it may only attempt side and rear shots, and even these require very close range (under 10") to be effective.

**Adequate Against Light Armor**: The PaK 36 remains effective against British Cruiser tanks (A9, A10, A13, Crusader) and light tanks at ranges up to 20".

**Cheap and Plentiful**: The 37mm PaK 36 has a reduced points cost, reflecting its limited capability by 1941 standards.

## Italian Armor

### M13/40 Medium Tank

Italy's main battle tank, adequate for its role but outclassed by British and German armor.

**Dual Machine Guns**: The M13/40 mounts twin hull-mounted machine guns in addition to its turret armament. When engaging infantry targets, it may fire these as an additional attack.

**47mm Gun**: The 47mm gun has limited armor penetration. It can engage Crusaders and other Cruiser tanks at normal ranges but struggles against Matildas (requires side/rear hits at close range).

**Sand Filter Problems**: The M13/40 suffered from inadequate air filtration in desert conditions. At the start of each game, roll a D6 for each M13/40. On a roll of 1-2, the tank's engine is suffering from sand ingestion - reduce movement by -4" for the entire game.

**Riveted Construction**: The M13/40's riveted armor could cause spalling (interior fragmentation) when hit. When an M13/40 takes a penetrating hit, add +1 to casualty rolls for crew.

### L3/35 Tankette

A light reconnaissance vehicle, completely obsolete by 1941 but still in service with Italian units.

**Obsolete Armor**: The L3/35 has minimal armor that can be penetrated by machine gun fire. All small arms and MG hits automatically penetrate. Do not roll armor saves against anything except small arms at long range.

**Twin Machine Guns**: Armed only with machine guns, the L3/35 cannot engage armored targets. It is used for reconnaissance and infantry harassment only.

**Fast Scout**: Movement rate of 14" on roads and 10" cross-country. May perform reconnaissance actions.

**Fragile**: The L3/35 is destroyed by any penetrating hit - do not roll for damage severity.

## Italian Anti-Tank Guns

### 47mm 47/32 Anti-Tank Gun

Italy's standard anti-tank gun, barely adequate against British armor of 1941.

**Limited Penetration**: The 47mm gun can penetrate British Cruiser tanks at ranges up to 20" but struggles beyond this range. Against Matilda IIs, it cannot penetrate frontal armor at any range - only side/rear shots at close range (under 10") have any chance.

**Reliable**: Despite limited penetration, the 47mm gun is mechanically reliable and easy to handle. It requires only one action to unlimber and prepare for firing.

**Common**: The 47mm is widely available to Italian units and has a reduced points cost reflecting its limited effectiveness.

### 20mm Breda Anti-Aircraft/Anti-Tank Gun

A light automatic cannon used in both AA and AT roles.

**Anti-Aircraft Primary**: This weapon is primarily for air defense but can engage light vehicles and armored cars. It may not effectively engage tanks (treat hits on tanks as MG fire - no penetration capability).

**High Rate of Fire**: When engaging infantry or soft targets, the Breda 20mm has a high rate of fire. Add +1 to hit rolls against these targets.

**Light and Mobile**: May be towed by light vehicles and deployed in one action.

## Special Ammunition

### Armor-Piercing Composite Rigid (APCR)

Some guns had access to limited quantities of tungsten-cored APCR ammunition with superior penetration.

**Limited Supply**: Units with APCR may fire up to 3 shots per game using this special ammunition. Track usage carefully.

**Superior Penetration**: APCR adds +2 to penetration rolls at all ranges up to 30". Beyond 30", APCR loses velocity rapidly and provides no bonus.

**Availability**: Only German 50mm PaK 38 and some Panzer III (Ausf J) models had access to APCR during Operation Compass period. British forces did not have APCR available in December 1940 - February 1941.

### Smoke Rounds

**Available**: British 25-pounders, German 50mm guns (limited), Italian 47mm guns (limited).

**Effect**: Smoke rounds create a 6" diameter smoke screen that blocks line of sight. The smoke dissipates at the end of the turn after next (remains for two full turns).

**Limited Supply**: Each gun may fire a maximum of 2 smoke rounds per game unless specifically noted in scenario special rules.

## Vehicle Reliability

### Breakdown Rules

All vehicles in desert conditions were subject to mechanical failure due to heat, dust, and wear.

**Breakdown Test**: When a vehicle moves at full speed (maximum movement rate) or over particularly rough terrain (as defined by scenario), roll a D6. On a roll of 1, the vehicle suffers a breakdown:
- **Minor Breakdown** (D6 roll 1-4): Vehicle is immobilized for D3 turns. At the end of this period, roll again - on 4+, the vehicle is repaired and may move normally.
- **Major Breakdown** (D6 roll 5-6): Vehicle is permanently immobilized. Crew may abandon the vehicle.

**Reliability Modifiers**:
- British Matilda II: +1 to breakdown rolls (more reliable)
- British Crusader: -1 to breakdown rolls (notoriously unreliable)
- Italian M13/40: -1 to breakdown rolls in extreme heat (sand filter problems)
- German vehicles: No modifier (standard reliability)

### Recovery and Repair

**Field Repairs**: Immobilized vehicles may attempt repair during the rally phase. A unit with mechanics or engineers within 4" may assist. Roll D6: on 5+, the vehicle is repaired and may move next turn at half speed.

**Recovery Vehicles**: Dedicated recovery vehicles (if available in scenario) may tow immobilized vehicles to safety at half their movement rate. The recovered vehicle is removed from play.

## Aircraft Special Rules

### Stuka Dive Bomber (Ju-87)

**Precision Bomber**: The Stuka receives +1 to hit rolls when dive bombing stationary or slow-moving targets (vehicles moving less than half speed).

**Siren Effect**: The Stuka's sirens affect morale. Enemy units within 12" of the Stuka's target must take a morale check at -1.

**Vulnerable**: If enemy fighters are present, the Stuka is highly vulnerable. It may be shot down on 4+ by any fighter aircraft.

### Hurricane/Kittyhawk Fighter-Bomber

**Ground Attack**: British fighter-bombers are effective against soft targets. They receive +1 to hit rolls against unarmored vehicles and infantry.

**Air Superiority**: If both Allied and Axis aircraft are present, fighters may intercept bombers. Roll opposed D6 - higher roll drives off opponent.

**Limited Bomb Load**: Fighter-bombers have limited ordnance. Each aircraft may make only 1 attack run per game before departing.

## Transport and Supply

### Truck Vulnerability

Trucks and soft-skinned vehicles are extremely vulnerable to all weapons fire.

**No Armor Save**: Trucks do not receive armor saves against any weapons fire (MG or larger). Any hit that strikes automatically damages the vehicle.

**Cargo Loss**: If a truck carrying supplies or troops is destroyed, its cargo is lost. Passengers must roll for casualties (treat as if under MG fire).

**Fast Movement**: Trucks move 18" on roads, 12" cross-country. They may not enter rough terrain or wadis without testing for immobilization.

### Supply Considerations

When scenarios include supply rules:

**Water**: Units must have access to water supplies. Units cut off from water for 2+ turns suffer -1 to all actions (cumulative).

**Fuel**: Vehicles require fuel to operate. Track fuel points as specified in scenario rules. A vehicle without fuel is immobilized.

**Ammunition**: Heavy weapons may have limited ammunition. Track expenditure as noted in scenario special rules. A weapon without ammunition may not fire.

## Terrain and Equipment Interaction

### Wadis and Steep Slopes

**Vehicle Restrictions**: Only tracked vehicles may attempt to cross wadis. Wheeled vehicles must use designated crossing points or be immobilized.

**Crossing Test**: Tracked vehicles crossing a wadi must roll D6. On 1-2, the vehicle is stuck in the wadi and immobilized. Recovery requires assistance.

### Escarpments

**Impassable**: Escarpments are impassable to all vehicles except at specifically marked passes or gaps.

**Observation**: Units on top of escarpments have excellent observation and receive +6" to spotting range.

**Reverse Slope**: Units on the reverse slope (away from enemy) of an escarpment receive hard cover and may not be seen until enemy moves to within direct line of sight.
