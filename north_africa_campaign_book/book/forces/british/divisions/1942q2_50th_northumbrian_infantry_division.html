North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

50th (Northumbrian) Infantry Division (1942Q2)

Command Structure

Commander: Major-General Maj-Gen William Hogarth Clarke Ramsden Parent Formation: XIII Corps Type: Infantry Division

Personnel

  • Total: 17,200 (731 officers, 2,473 NCOs, 13,996 enlisted)

Subordinate Units

69th Infantry Brigade

  • Commander: Brigadier Unknown
  • Strength: 4,286 personnel
  • Composition: Held northern sector of 50th Division's Gazala Line positions. Survived Battle of Gazala intact and participated in withdrawal. Later fought in First El Alamein (July) and Tunisia Campaign.

150th Infantry Brigade

  • Commander: Brigadier C.W. Haydon
  • Strength: 4,500 personnel
  • Composition: DESTROYED 1 JUNE 1942. Moved to southern defensive box in late April with 20-mile perimeter, 6 miles from 69th Brigade north, 10 miles from Free French at Bir Hakeim south. Attacked by Rommel 29 May from rear using 15th Panzer, Trieste Motorised, and 90th Light Divisions plus heavy Luftwaffe bombing. Held 72 hours without resupply. Overrun noon 1 June 1942. All three infantry battalions, attached artillery regiment, engineers, and support units captured. Brigadier Haydon killed in action 1 June. Brigade never reformed.

151st Infantry Brigade

  • Commander: Brigadier Unknown
  • Strength: 4,286 personnel
  • Composition: Held southern sector of main 50th Division line. Survived Battle of Gazala intact and participated in withdrawal with 69th Brigade. Continued service through El Alamein and Tunisia.

Divisional Artillery

  • Commander: Brigadier Unknown
  • Strength: 2,232 personnel
  • Composition: Division initially had 4 field regiments (96 guns). Loss of 90th Field Regiment RA with 150th Brigade reduced field artillery to 72 guns (3 regiments). Anti-tank regiment transitioning from 2-pdrs to 6-pdrs during Q2 1942, process incomplete by June.

2nd Cheshire Machine Gun Battalion

  • Commander: None
  • Strength: 448 personnel
  • Composition: Provided sustained fire support with Vickers medium machine guns. Approximately 96 Vickers guns distributed across battalion. Also served as divisional anti-tank regiment with mix of 6-pdr and 2-pdr guns.

Divisional Support Units

  • Commander: None
  • Strength: 1,948 personnel
  • Composition: Comprehensive support organization typical of British infantry divisions. Significant losses 1 June with 150th Brigade included: 232 Field Company RE, 18th Field Ambulance, 150th Brigade Signals, and attached RASC/workshops. Remaining support units maintained operations for 69th and 151st Brigades through withdrawal.

6th South African Armoured Car Regiment (Attached)

  • Commander: None
  • Strength: 312 personnel
  • Composition: South African reconnaissance unit attached to 50th Division for desert operations. Marmon-Herrington armored cars locally produced in South Africa, well-adapted to desert conditions.

Supply & Logistics

Supply Status: Adequate for defensive operations along Gazala Line during April-May. Division held 25-mile sector with prepared defensive positions and static supply depots. 150th Brigade isolated in southern box 20-mile perimeter, 6 miles from 69th Brigade, 10 miles from Free French at Bir Hakeim. Rommel's offensive (26 May) severed supply lines to 150th Brigade box, which held 72 hours (29-31 May) without resupply before surrender 1 June. Loss of 150th Brigade, 90th Field Regiment RA, and attached units reduced division strength by ~4,500 men. Remainder of division (69th and 151st Brigades) maintained supply lines during withdrawal to Mersa Matruh late June.
