1st South African Infantry Division (1942Q2)

Command Structure

Commander: Major-General Maj-Gen Dan Pienaar Parent Formation: XIII Corps Type: Commonwealth Infantry Division

Personnel

  • Total: 17,000 (723 officers, 2,410 NCOs, 13,867 enlisted)

Subordinate Units

1st South African Infantry Brigade

  • Commander: Brigadier Unknown
  • Strength: 4,129 personnel
  • Composition: Deployed first time with division at full 3-brigade strength on 26 May 1942. Holding northern sector of Gazala Line near coast. Nafziger strength notation: officers/enlisted/natives/C.C. (Cape Coloured personnel).

2nd South African Infantry Brigade

  • Commander: Brigadier William Henry Evered Poole
  • Strength: 4,129 personnel
  • Composition: Holding middle sector of division area along Gazala Line. Successfully repulsed Italian Sabratha Division breakthrough attempt during Battle of Gazala.

3rd South African Infantry Brigade

  • Commander: Brigadier C.E. Borain, MC, VD
  • Strength: 4,129 personnel
  • Composition: Transferred FROM 2nd SA Division TO 1st SA Division on 21 March 1942. First quarter operating under 1st SA Division command. Deployed for first time with all three brigades together on 26 May 1942.

Divisional Artillery

  • Commander: Brigadier Unknown
  • Strength: 2,156 personnel
  • Composition: Concentrated artillery firepower critical during First El Alamein defense (July 1942, subsequent to Gazala withdrawal). Division had 96 field guns (25-pdrs) plus attached medium artillery. Anti-tank capability limited with mostly 2-pdrs, insufficient against upgraded German armor.

Divisional Machine Gun Units

  • Commander: None
  • Strength: 428 personnel
  • Composition: Specialized machine gun units providing sustained fire support. Regiment President Steyn and Regiment Botha provided battalion-level support. B Company from Die Middellandse Regiment attached (remainder with 2nd SA Division).

Divisional Support Units

  • Commander: None
  • Strength: 1,829 personnel
  • Composition: Comprehensive support organization typical of Commonwealth divisions. Includes signals, engineers, medical, supply (Q Services), transport (T Services), workshops, provost, postal, salvage, and hygiene units. Approximately 5,000 non-European personnel integrated throughout support services.

Supply & Logistics

Supply Status: Initially adequate during defensive phase at Gazala Line (April-May). Division held coastal sector with static supply depots. Primary supply route from Tobruk (35km) improved from Q1. Battle of Gazala (26 May - 14 June) disrupted supply lines as Axis forces broke through southern flank. Division successfully withdrew along coastal road to Mersa Matruh by month's end without significant supply collapse, though under constant pressure. Approximately 17,000 men (including 5,000 non-Europeans) maintained throughout quarter despite combat operations.
