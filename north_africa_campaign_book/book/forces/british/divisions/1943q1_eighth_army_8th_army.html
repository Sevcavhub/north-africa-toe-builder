Supply & Logistics

Supply Status: DRAMATICALLY IMPROVED but extended lines remain challenge. Eighth Army supply situation Q1 1943 transformed by capture of Tripoli port (23 January 1943). Supply lines reduced from 1,800km (El Alamein-Libya pursuit) to 600-800km (Tripoli-Mareth Line). Montgomery refused to assault Mareth until adequate stockpiling complete - delayed offensive from early March to 20 March for logistics buildup. X Corps (armoured reserve) well-supplied with fuel, ammunition, water for mobile operations. XXX Corps (infantry assault) required massive ammunition stocks for artillery bombardment. Main constraints: 600-800km still extended distance, intensive combat expected against prepared German defenses, coordination with First Army advancing from west (supply competition). Tripoli port critical - activated quickly despite German demolitions, capacity gradually increased through Q1. Forward supply dumps established near Mareth Line. Water supply adequate in coastal Tunisia (better than Western Desert). Montgomery's 'build-up then attack' doctrine ensured Eighth Army never advanced beyond logistics capacity.
