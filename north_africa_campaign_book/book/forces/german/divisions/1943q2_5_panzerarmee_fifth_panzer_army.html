5. Panzerarmee (Fifth Panzer Army) (1943Q2)

Command Structure

Commander: Generaloberst / Generalleutnant Generaloberst Gustav von Vaerst (until May 9), then Generalleutnant Friedrich Weber Parent Formation: Heeresgruppe Afrika (Army Group Africa) Type: Panzer Army

Personnel

  • Total: 47,200 (1,680 officers, 7,850 NCOs, 37,670 enlisted)

Subordinate Units

Army Headquarters

  • Commander: Gen.Lt. Weber (after May 9)
  • Strength: 700 personnel
  • Composition: Reduced HQ strength. Many staff evacuated to Italy April-May. Final HQ manages surrender operations.

Deutsches Afrikakorps (DAK) - Remnants

  • Commander: Generalleutnant Heinz Ziegler
  • Strength: 8,200 personnel
  • Composition: DAK dissolved as organized corps. Remnants of 15./21. Panzer-Division and 90. leichte Division absorbed into Kampfgruppen. Organizational structure fiction by May. Estimated ~8,200 survivors captured May 13 (vs Q1 25,875 = 68% reduction). No division-level TO&E files extracted for Q2 - units ceased to exist as formations.

10. Panzer-Division - Remnants

  • Commander: Generalmajor Fritz Freiherr von Broich
  • Strength: 3,800 personnel
  • Composition: Division destroyed Battle of Longstop Hill (April 22-27). Survivors reorganized as Kampfgruppe. Estimated ~3,800 survivors captured May 13 (vs Q1 12,800 = 70% reduction). Von Manteuffel (operations officer) evacuated to Italy April. Von Broich captured May 13.

Hermann Göring Division - Remnants

  • Commander: Generalmajor Joseph Schmid
  • Strength: 4,100 personnel
  • Composition: Division scattered in final fighting. Estimated ~4,100 survivors captured May 13 (vs Q1 9,000 = 54% reduction). Some elements evacuated to Sicily late April. Division reconstituted in Sicily June-July 1943 for Sicily defense using France-based elements + survivors.

334th Infantry Division - Remnants

  • Strength: 3,200 personnel
  • Composition: Division dissolved into Kampfgruppen. Estimated ~3,200 survivors captured (vs Q1 ~8,500 = 62% reduction).

Various Kampfgruppen and ad-hoc units

  • Strength: 6,500 personnel
  • Composition: Provisional battlegroups formed from division remnants, corps troops, supply units reorganized as infantry. Includes survivors of dissolved units, stragglers, rear-area personnel pressed into combat. Estimated ~6,500 troops in various ad-hoc formations May 13.

Italian Divisions (operational control) - Remnants

  • Strength: 15,700 personnel
  • Composition: Italian divisions largely collapsed. Estimated ~15,700 Italian troops still with 5. Panzerarmee May 13 (vs Q1 ~31,000 = 49% reduction). Many Italian soldiers deserted late April-early May. Remaining formations skeletal. Separate from Italian First Army POWs.

Wounded and non-combatants

  • Strength: 5,000 personnel
  • Composition: Estimated ~5,000 wounded in final perimeter unable to fight. Included in total personnel count but not combat effectives. Field hospitals overwhelmed, no medical supplies. Many wounded died untreated May 8-13. Humanitarian crisis motivating surrender.

Supply & Logistics

Supply Status: TOTAL COLLAPSE - Army completely cut off from resupply April 20 onward. Allied naval blockade and air supremacy prevent any shipping from Sicily/Italy. Tunisia ports (Tunis, Bizerte) fall to Allies May 7. Air bridge from Sicily shut down May 5 after catastrophic transport losses (100+ Ju-52 transports destroyed attempting supply flights). NO fuel deliveries after April 25. NO ammunition deliveries after April 30. NO food deliveries after May 5. Army consuming stockpiled reserves April 1-25, then complete exhaustion. Fuel completely gone May 8-10. Artillery ammunition exhausted May 10-12. Small arms ammunition rationed to 10 rounds/soldier final days. No medical supplies - field hospitals overwhelmed, no anesthetics, no bandages. Water adequate (northern Tunisia wells, spring rainfall). Starvation imminent by May 13 - surrender partly motivated by food exhaustion. Strategic situation: 230,000 Axis troops (German+Italian) trapped in 50km x 80km pocket with zero resupply. Hitler refused evacuation (unlike Dunkirk 1940 or later evacuations). Complete destruction inevitable - only question was timing of surrender.
