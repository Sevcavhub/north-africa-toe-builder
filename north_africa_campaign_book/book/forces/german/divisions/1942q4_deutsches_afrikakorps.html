Deutsches Afrikakorps (1942Q4)

Subordinate Units

15. Panzer-Division

  • Commander: Generalmajor Gustav von Vaerst (until 4 Nov when promoted to corps command)
  • Strength: 8,920 personnel
  • Tank Strength: 118 tanks
  • Composition: One of two panzer divisions in Afrika Korps. Elite formation, veteran of entire North African campaign since 1941. Suffered catastrophic losses at El Alamein: 90% of tanks, 50% of personnel.

21. Panzer-Division

  • Commander: Generalmajor Georg von Bismarck (KIA 31 Aug 1942), then Generalmajor Heinz von Randow (18 Sep - 21 Dec 1942)
  • Strength: 12,800 personnel
  • Tank Strength: 85 tanks
  • Composition: Second panzer division. Commander von Randow assumed command 18 September after von Bismarck killed by mine. Division destroyed at El Alamein, retreated to Tunisia.

90. leichte Afrika-Division

  • Commander: Generalmajor Theodor Graf von Sponeck (appointed 1 Sep, promoted Generalmajor 1 Nov 1942)
  • Strength: 9,847 personnel
  • Tank Strength: 0 tanks
  • Composition: Three Panzergrenadier regiments. Excellent desert warfare experience. Supply crisis particularly affected mobility.

164. leichte Afrika-Division

  • Commander: Generalmajor Carl-Hans Lungershausen
  • Strength: 8,400 personnel
  • Tank Strength: 0 tanks
  • Composition: Three Panzergrenadier regiments (125, 382, 433). Newer to Africa than other divisions. Mixed German-Italian transport (less reliable). Destroyed Tunisia May 1943.

  • Composition: Corps-level AT battalion, attached to divisions as needed. Critical asset given shortage of effective AT weapons.

Flak-Abteilung 606

Supply & Logistics

Supply Status: CATASTROPHIC throughout Q4 1942. Mediterranean supply crisis reached peak during El Alamein period. British interdiction (Royal Navy, RAF Malta-based bombers, ULTRA intelligence) sank majority of supply convoys. Critical tankers Tripolino and Ostia sunk 1 November, strangling fuel supply. Supply line 1,400km from Tripoli to El Alamein front exposed to constant air attack. Rommel's entire Panzerarmee estimated only 3-4 days fuel at start of El Alamein battle (23 Oct). Ammunition stocks critically low: artillery rationed 10-15 rounds/gun/day (vs. British unlimited fire). Water supply barely adequate at 4.5L/man/day (medical requirement 10-15L in desert heat). Food situation slightly better due to captured British supplies but convoy losses reduced reserves. Supply crisis primary factor in Axis defeat at El Alamein and prevented effective defense during retreat. By December, supply base shifted to Tunis but situation remained critical.
