North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

21. Panzer-Division (1942Q1)

Command Structure

Commander: Generalmajor Generalmajor Georg von Bismarck Parent Formation: Deutsches Afrikakorps (DAK) Type: Panzer Division

Personnel

  • Total: 11,565 (520 officers, 1,735 NCOs, 9,310 enlisted)

Subordinate Units

Panzer-Regiment 5

  • Commander: Unknown (Oberst rank)
  • Strength: 1,480 personnel
  • Composition: From 3. Panzer-Division. Deployed to Africa Feb 1941 with 5. leichte Division. Redesignated to 21. Panzer 1 Aug 1941. Tank strength devastated in Operation Crusader (Nov-Dec 1941): 165 → 79 tanks (52% loss). 5 Jan 1942 convoy brought ~25 tanks to division (DAK total 55 tanks). Primary medium tank now Pz.III Ausf J with superior long 50mm gun vs British Crusader/Valentine. Light tanks (Pz.II, Pz.I) reconnaissance role only.

Schützen-Regiment 104

  • Commander: Unknown
  • Strength: 2,380 personnel
  • Composition: Transferred to 21. Panzer-Division from 15. Panzer on 1 Sept 1941 (when 5. leichte redesignated 21. Panzer). Filled role of Schützen-Regiment 200 (which had unique MG battalion structure). Standard motorized infantry regiment with 2 battalions. Renamed Panzergrenadier-Regiment 104 on 5 July 1942. Reduced from Q2 1941 strength due to Crusader losses. MG-Btl. 8 attached until 1 April 1942 when it replaced I./Schützen-Regiment 104 (aufgerieben/worn out).

Schützen-Regiment 155

  • Commander: Unknown
  • Strength: 2,250 personnel
  • Composition: Designation changed from Schützen-Regiment 200 (which was formed from 2nd and 8th MG Battalions with unique structure). By Q1 1942 reorganized into standard two-battalion motorized infantry regiment. Renamed Panzergrenadier-Regiment 155 on 5 July 1942. Reduced from Q2 1941 (5. leichte) strength due to Crusader attrition. Provided second infantry regiment giving division standard panzer division structure.

Aufklärungs-Abteilung 3

  • Commander: Unknown
  • Strength: 485 personnel
  • Composition: From 3. Panzer-Division. Deployed to Africa Feb 1941 with 5. leichte Division. Redesignated to 21. Panzer 1 Aug 1941. Critical for desert reconnaissance and screening. Armored car strength reduced from 52 in Q2 1941 to 43 in Q1 1942 (Crusader losses). One zug used Volkswagen Kübelwagens for liaison. Renamed Panzer-Aufklärungs-Abteilung 580 (later 21) in 1943.

Artillerie-Regiment 155

  • Commander: Unknown
  • Strength: 895 personnel
  • Composition: Formed for 21. Panzer-Division when redesignated from 5. leichte (1 Aug 1941). Replaced single battalion (I./Artillerie-Regiment 75, 12 guns) with full three-battalion regiment (36 guns). Significantly strengthened artillery vs 5. leichte Q2 1941. Still lighter than 15. Panzer (3 battalions but 44 guns including 8.8cm Flak). Provided standard panzer division artillery structure.

Panzerjäger-Abteilung 39

  • Commander: Unknown
  • Strength: 340 personnel
  • Composition: From 3. Panzer-Division. Deployed to Africa Feb 1941. Towed AT guns (not self-propelled). 3.7cm 'door-knocker' increasingly inadequate vs British Matilda II (78mm frontal armor). 5cm PaK 38 increased from 6 (Q2 1941) to 16 (Q1 1942) - effective vs Crusader/Valentine. Renamed Panzerjäger-Abteilung 190 in late 1942.

Panzerjäger-Abteilung 605 (gepanzert)

  • Commander: Unknown
  • Strength: 185 personnel
  • Composition: Self-propelled battalion with Czech 47mm AT guns on Panzerjäger I chassis (Q2 1941: 9 guns). Devastated in Operation Crusader. By Q1 1942 reduced to cadre strength with most equipment lost. Battalion effectively absorbed into other units by Feb 1942. Not included in division artillery total due to minimal operational strength.

Flak-Bataillon 606 (Selbstfahrlafette)

  • Commander: Unknown
  • Strength: 220 personnel
  • Composition: Mobile AA defense for advancing formations. Critical against RAF fighter-bomber attacks. Reduced from 3 companies (36 guns) in Q2 1941 to 2 companies (22 guns) in Q1 1942 due to Crusader losses. Essential for mobile operations providing AA umbrella for panzer columns.

I./Flak-Regiment 33

  • Commander: Unknown
  • Strength: 385 personnel
  • Composition: From 3. Panzer-Division. 88mm guns proved devastatingly effective in anti-tank role vs British cruiser tanks (Crusader, Valentine). Rommel's preferred anti-tank weapon. Reduced from 3× 88mm batteries (12 guns) in Q2 1941 to 2 batteries (8 guns) in Q1 1942 (Crusader losses). Added 37mm battery for medium AA coverage. Essential for anti-tank defense when 3.7cm PaK inadequate.

Panzer-Pionier-Bataillon 200

  • Commander: Unknown
  • Strength: 430 personnel
  • Composition: z.b.V. (zur besonderen Verwendung) = for special employment. Formed 1 Aug 1941 when 5. leichte redesignated 21. Panzer. Mine warfare and fortification work critical for North Africa. Reduced from 485 personnel in Q2 1941 (as 200th z.b.V. Pionier-Bataillon) to 430 in Q1 1942 (Crusader losses). Desert engineering: mine laying/clearing, water point development, demolitions.

Panzer-Nachrichten-Abteilung 200

  • Commander: Unknown
  • Strength: 310 personnel
  • Composition: Communications critical for mobile desert operations and coordination with DAK HQ. Reduced from 345 personnel in Q2 1941 (as Nachrichten-Abteilung 39) to 310 in Q1 1942. Redesignated Panzer-Nachrichten-Abteilung 200 on 1 Aug 1941 (when 5. leichte became 21. Panzer). Radio communications essential for coordinating fast-moving operations in Jan 1942 counter-offensive.

Divisions-Nachschubführer 200

  • Commander: Unknown
  • Strength: 1,725 personnel
  • Composition: Extensive logistical tail essential for desert operations 850-1000km from Tripoli. Water and fuel supply paramount. Medical facilities for casualties and desert illnesses. Significantly reduced from Q2 1941 (2140 personnel) to Q1 1942 (1725) due to Crusader losses and vehicle attrition. Workshop strength increased (priority for post-Crusader tank repairs). Fuel/water tankers reduced (critical shortage). Renamed Kdr. der Pz.Div.Nachschubtruppen 200 on 1 Dec 1942.

Supply & Logistics

Supply Status: CRITICAL - Post-Operation Crusader depletion. Extended supply lines from Tripoli (850-1000+ km) via single coastal road (Via Balbia). British naval interdiction (Malta-based RAF/RN) destroys 30-40% of Axis convoys. Fuel reserves 5 days (same as DAK critical shortage), ammunition 8 days, water 11L/man/day (barely adequate desert minimum). DAK convoy arrived 5 Jan 1942 (55 tanks total enabling counter-offensive).
