XX Corpo d'Armata Motocorazzato (1942Q4)

Command Structure

Commander: Generale di Corpo d'Armata (Corps General) Generale di Corpo d'Armata Giuseppe De Stefanis Parent Formation: Panzerarmee Afrika Type: Motorized-Armored Corps

Personnel

  • Total: 25,250 (1,285 officers, 3,825 NCOs, 20,140 enlisted)

Subordinate Units

132ª Divisione corazzata Ariete

  • Commander: Generale di Brigata Francesco Antonio Arena
  • Strength: 7,200 personnel
  • Composition: Destroyed 4 November 1942 at Tel el Aqqaqir. Only XIII Tank Battalion and ~200 Bersaglieri with 6 Semoventi escaped initial encirclement. Division declared lost 8 December 1942.

133ª Divisione Corazzata Littorio

  • Commander: Generale di Divisione Gervasio Bitossi
  • Strength: 6,800 personnel
  • Composition: Destroyed in North Africa, December 1942. Heavy losses at El Alamein, further attrition during retreat across Libya.

101ª Divisione Motorizzata Trieste

  • Commander: Generale di Divisione Arnaldo Azzi
  • Strength: 11,200 personnel
  • Composition: Mostly annihilated 2-4 November 1942 at El Alamein during Operation Supercharge. Survivors reorganized at El Agheila 26 November with remnants of other destroyed divisions.

XX Corpo Staff and Support Units

  • Commander: Generale Giuseppe De Stefanis
  • Strength: 550 personnel
  • Composition: Corps HQ, signals, military police, rear services. Minimal compared to German corps staffs.

Supply & Logistics

Supply Status: Catastrophic shortage throughout Q4 1942. Corps positioned at extreme end of 1800km supply line from Tripoli, continuously interdicted by British air and naval forces. Fuel reserves exhausted during Second Battle of El Alamein, forcing tanks to fight in static positions. Ammunition sufficient for only 2-3 days sustained combat. Water rationing severe (3.5L/person/day). Food supplies critically low. Ariete Division immobilized by fuel exhaustion during final encirclement at Tel el Aqqaqir (4 Nov). Littorio barely operational during retreat. Trieste fuel/ammo/food all critically short. Supply crisis was primary factor in corps destruction at El Alamein.
