  • Composition: Regiment renamed 'Sabratha' in 1939. Each battalion: 4 rifle companies + 1 machine gun company + 1 mortar platoon. Personnel strength increased from Q3 (2,610 → 2,620) with November reinforcements. Regiment deployed in southern sector covering Wadi Derna and inland approaches.

42° Reggimento Artiglieria

  • Composition: Standard Italian divisional artillery organization. Mixed calibers for flexibility. Personnel increased from Q3 (1,205 → 1,210) with specialist fire control personnel. Artillery positioned in prepared firing positions with extensive ammunition stockpiles. Pre-registered defensive fire zones covering all approaches to Derna defensive line. Emphasis on indirect fire support from protected positions.

60ª Compagnia Controcarri

  • Composition: Divisional anti-tank company. 47mm guns effective against light armor only - could not penetrate British Matilda infantry tanks (78mm frontal armor). Personnel increased from Q3 (122 → 124). Guns positioned covering likely tank approaches along coastal road and wadi crossing points. Recognized inadequacy against heavier British tanks led to emphasis on dug-in positions and ambush tactics at close range.

42° Compagnia Genio

  • Composition: Division-level engineers. Personnel significantly increased from Q3 (185 → 195) to support extensive defensive preparation work. Responsible for Derna defensive fortifications including trench systems, strongpoint construction, and extensive minefield belts. Laid thousands of mines across approaches to defensive positions. Prepared demolitions on key infrastructure including Wadi Derna bridges and port facilities. Major effort throughout Q4 constructing defensive works.

60ª Compagnia Trasmissioni

  • Composition: Division signals. Personnel increased from Q3 (142 → 145). Limited radio equipment - relied heavily on wire communications. Extensive wire network established throughout Derna defensive positions linking all battalion and regimental command posts to division HQ. Wire buried where possible to protect against artillery fire. Radio communication primarily for higher headquarters and emergency use.

LXXIII Battaglione Camicie Nere

  • Composition: MVSN (Fascist Militia) legion attached to division. Personnel slightly increased from Q3 (655 → 660). Provided additional infantry but lower combat effectiveness than regular infantry. Employed in secondary defensive sectors and rear area security at Derna. Also used extensively for labor on defensive fortification work throughout Q4.

60ª Sezione Sanità

  • Composition: Division medical services. Personnel increased from Q3 (225 → 230) and capacity enhanced in preparation for combat operations. Medical stocks significantly increased throughout Q4. Field hospitals established in protected positions within Derna. Serious casualties evacuated via coastal road to Benghazi or by sea from Derna port. Medical planning emphasized mass casualty procedures for anticipated British offensive.

60ª Sezione Sussistenza

  • Composition: Division supply and logistics. Personnel increased from Q3 (285 → 290) to manage expanded supply operations. Operated large supply dumps at Derna utilizing coastal supply route from Benghazi and local port facilities. Static defensive position allowed extensive stockpiling of ammunition, fuel, water, and rations. Supply section managed distribution to battalion strongpoints and forward positions. Prepared for potential siege conditions under British attack.

60° Plotone Carabinieri

  • Composition: Military police. Personnel slightly increased from Q3 (46 → 47). Traffic control on coastal road (Via Balbia) through Derna - critical supply route. Prisoner handling procedures established for anticipated British POWs. Rear area security including prevention of looting and control of civilian movement in Derna city. Liaison with civil authorities.

60ª Banda Musicale

  • Composition: Ceremonial and morale purposes. Continued performing for troop morale during defensive preparations. Regular concerts for troops in rest areas behind defensive line.
