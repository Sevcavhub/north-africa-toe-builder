101st Trieste Division (Motorized) (1942Q3)

Command Structure

Commander: Generale di Brigata Francesco La Ferla Parent Formation: XX Motorized Corps Type: Motorized Infantry Division

Personnel

  • Total: 7,850 (245 officers, 810 NCOs, 6,795 enlisted)

Subordinate Units

65th Infantry Regiment "Valtellina"

  • Commander: Colonnello Unknown
  • Strength: 2,250 personnel
  • Composition: Two battalions operational. Third battalion (III Bn) destroyed at Ruweisat Ridge 22 July 1942 attempting counterattack against New Zealand forces. Heavy casualties from First Alamein artillery bombardments and infantry assaults.

66th Infantry Regiment "Valtellina"

  • Commander: Colonnello Unknown
  • Strength: 2,180 personnel
  • Composition: Two battalions. Defended coastal sector most exposed to British artillery fire from Mediterranean. Sustained casualties from naval bombardment and RAF attacks. Attrition from constant bombardment duty.

9th Bersaglieri Regiment

  • Commander: Colonnello Unknown
  • Strength: 1,950 personnel
  • Composition: Four battalions: XXVIII Cyclist, XXX Motorized, XXXII Machine-Gun, DVIII Support Weapons. Elite formation with distinguished performance at Ruweisat Ridge 21-22 July, described by British as 'tenacious defense.' Motorcycle reconnaissance capability valuable in desert.

21st Artillery Regiment "Po"

  • Commander: Colonnello Unknown
  • Strength: 815 personnel
  • Composition: Five groups: I (100mm howitzers), II (75mm guns), III (75mm guns), IX (105mm howitzers - partially returned from Trento), XXI Mixed AA. Combat losses 15% from British counter-battery fire. Outranged by British 25-pounders.

XI Medium Tank Battalion

  • Commander: Maggiore Unknown
  • Strength: 185 personnel
  • Composition: Rare organic tank battalion for Italian motorized division. Originally 45 M13/40 tanks (April 1942), reduced to 19 total (14 operational) by September through combat losses at Gazala (12 tanks), mechanical breakdowns (8 tanks), First Alamein (6 tanks). M13/40 outclassed by British Grant and Sherman tanks arriving summer 1942.

51st Mixed Engineer Battalion

  • Commander: Maggiore Unknown
  • Strength: 340 personnel
  • Composition: Minefield construction, defensive position preparation, obstacle clearance, vehicle recovery. Critical role in El Alamein defensive works. Worked alongside German engineers on extensive minefield belts.

90th Medical Section

  • Commander: Tenente Colonnello Medico Unknown
  • Strength: 110 personnel
  • Composition: Overwhelmed with casualties from First Alamein battles. Desert medical conditions challenging: heat exhaustion, dysentery, wounds complicated by sand and flies. Field hospitals targeted by British air attacks. 118th Field Hospital listed as 'missing' in Nafziger document.

176th Supply Section

  • Commander: Maggiore Unknown
  • Strength: 120 personnel
  • Composition: Responsible for divisional logistics, ammunition distribution, fuel management, water supply, bakery (25th Squadron). Supply operations severely constrained by long supply lines and British air interdiction.

Supply & Logistics

Supply Status: Critical. Division severely strained after Gazala offensive (May-June 1942) and First El Alamein defensive battles (July 1942). Supply lines stretched 550km from Benghazi, 250km from Tobruk (both under British air interdiction). Mediterranean convoy losses from Malta-based aircraft and submarines devastating. Fuel critically short, limiting tactical movement despite full motorization. Ammunition expenditure in defensive artillery duels exceeds resupply rate. Water supply critical in summer heat (42°C peaks). Italian supply priority below German units.
