North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

1940-Q2: Italy Enters the War (April-June 1940)

Strategic Situation

Italy declared war on France and Britain on June 10, 1940. Marshal Rodolfo Graziani commanded Italian forces in Libya (5th Army) with approximately 236,000 troops facing British Western Desert Force (36,000 troops) in Egypt.

Italian Forces: 10 divisions in Cyrenaica and Tripolitania British Forces: 7th Armoured Division, 4th Indian Division, garrison troops

Major Operations

June 10, 1940: Italy Declares War

  • Marshal Graziani hesitant to attack despite numerical superiority
  • Supply difficulties: 1,900 km from Tripoli to Egyptian border
  • Italian armor inferior (L3/35 tankettes, M11/39 light tanks)

June-August: Border Skirmishing

  • British armored cars conduct aggressive patrolling
  • 11th Hussars raid Italian positions
  • No major Italian offensive despite Mussolini's pressure

Order of Battle

Italian 10th Army (Cyrenaica):

  • 62nd Marmarica Division
  • 63rd Cirene Division
  • 61st Sirte Division
  • 1st Blackshirt Division
  • 2nd Blackshirt Division

British Western Desert Force:

  • 7th Armoured Division (200 tanks)
  • 4th Indian Division (infantry)
  • Support Group (artillery, motorized infantry)

Weather and Terrain

Temperature: 18-35°C (desert heat increasing) Terrain: Coastal plain, desert plateau, escarpments Challenges: Water scarcity (10L per man per day minimum), sandstorms, extreme heat

Supply Status

Italian: Adequate supplies in forward bases, but long supply line vulnerability British: Good supply from Alexandria/Port Said, short lines of communication

Historical Significance

Italy's entry transformed Mediterranean into active war zone. British numerical inferiority offset by superior training, motorization, and aggressive tactics. Stage set for Operation Compass (December 1940).
