North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

1943-Q1: Tunisia Campaign - Kasserine and Mareth (January-March 1943)

Strategic Situation

Axis forces trapped in Tunisia between British Eighth Army (advancing from Libya) and Allied First Army/II Corps (advancing from Algeria). German 5. Panzerarmee + Rommel's forces totaled 250,000 troops. Allies prepared pincers to crush Axis bridgehead.

Major Battles

January 14-31: Eighth Army Advances

  • January 23: Eighth Army captures Tripoli (1,400 miles from El Alamein)
  • Rommel retreats to Mareth Line (Tunisia-Libya border)
  • January 31: Panzerarmee Afrika enters Tunisia
  • Two Axis armies: Rommel (south), von Arnim's 5. Panzerarmee (north)

February 14-22: Battle of Kasserine Pass

  • February 14: German offensive vs US II Corps
    1. Panzer-Division, 21. Panzer-Division attack Sidi Bou Zid
  • US 1st Armored Division defeated (100+ tanks lost)
  • February 19-20: Attack on Kasserine Pass
    • US II Corps retreats 50 miles
    • 6,000 US casualties, 183 tanks lost
  • February 22: Rommel withdraws (Allied reinforcements arriving)

Kasserine Lessons:

  • First major US ground combat vs Germans
  • Tactical defeat exposed US inexperience
  • Leadership changes: Patton assumes II Corps command
  • Americans learned combined arms warfare hard way

March 6: Battle of Medenine

  • Rommel attacks Eighth Army positions (Medenine)
  • British defense: 400 AT guns in prepared positions
  • German tank losses: 52 destroyed
  • British losses: minimal
  • March 9: Rommel departs Africa (illness, never returns)

March 20-27: Battle of Mareth Line

  • March 20-21: XXX Corps frontal assault (50th Division)
    • Repulsed with heavy casualties
  • March 21-26: Left Hook (New Zealand Corps + 1st Armoured Division)
    • Outflank Mareth Line via Tebaga Gap
    • Italian positions penetrated
  • March 26-27: Axis retreat from Mareth
  • March 29: Eighth Army links with II Corps (pincers connected)

Kasserine Equipment Lessons

German Advantages:

  • Panzer IV Ausf G (75mm L/43 gun): Superior to M3 Lee/Grant
  • Tiger I debut (6 tanks): 88mm gun devastating
  • Experienced crews vs green US tankers

US Weaknesses:

  • M3 Lee/Grant obsolete (sponson 75mm gun, high profile)
  • M4 Sherman adequate but crews inexperienced
  • Tank destroyer doctrine flawed (thinly armored, poor tactics)

After Kasserine:

  • US II Corps reorganized
  • Patton enforces discipline, training
  • Combined arms coordination improved
  • M4 Shermans replace M3 Lees

Mareth Line Defenses

French-Built Fortifications (1930s):

  • Concrete bunkers, AT ditches
  • Minefields, wire obstacles
  • Wadi Zigzaou natural obstacle
  • Weakness: Open southern flank (Tebaga Gap)

British Tactics:

  • Frontal attack (XXX Corps) pinned defenders
  • Left hook (NZ Corps, 1st Armoured) outflanked via Tebaga Gap
  • Air superiority (constant bombing Axis supply lines)
  • Artillery concentrations (500+ guns massed)

Supply Crisis Worsens

Axis Supply Collapse:

  • Mediterranean sea routes interdicted (Allied air/naval superiority)
  • Malta-based aircraft sink 60-70% of supply ships
  • Tunis/Bizerte ports bombed daily
  • Fuel rationing: Tanks immobilized
  • Ammunition shortages critical

Allied Supply Dominance:

  • Tripoli port operational (captured January 23)
  • Sfax, Sousse ports secured
  • Allied air superiority: 3,000 aircraft vs 500 Axis
  • American logistics flooding North Africa

  • Air operations curtailed (muddy airfields)

Terrain:

  • Northern Tunisia: Mountains, narrow valleys
  • Mareth Line: Desert terrain, wadis
  • Tebaga Gap: Passable for armor (key to outflanking)

Command Changes

Axis Command Chaos:

  • Rommel vs von Arnim rivalry
  • Divided command (two armies, competing for supplies)
  • March 9: Rommel evacuated to Germany (illness, never returns)
  • von Arnim assumes overall command (too late)

Allied Command Unity:

  • Eisenhower supreme commander (Operation Torch forces)
  • Alexander deputy, coordinates Eighth Army + First Army
  • Montgomery (Eighth Army), Anderson (First Army), Patton (II Corps)

Air Superiority

Allied Air Dominance:

  • 3,000 Allied aircraft vs 500 Axis
  • Constant interdiction: Supply ships, convoys, airfields
  • Close air support improving (II Corps cooperation)
  • Luftwaffe fighters outnumbered, outperformed

Axis Air Weakness:

  • Fuel shortages ground fighters
  • Airfields bombed
  • Transport aircraft losses mounting (supply airlift 30% losses)

Historical Significance

Kasserine Pass (February 14-22) bloodied inexperienced US II Corps but taught crucial lessons applied later. Mareth Line (March 20-27) broke last major Axis defensive position. Pincers closing: Eighth Army from east, First Army/II Corps from west/north.

Casualties 1943-Q1:

  • Allied: 10,000 (mostly US at Kasserine)
  • Axis: 15,000 (mostly POWs, cannot evacuate wounded)

Strategic Impact:

  • Axis retreat to final perimeter (Tunis-Bizerte)
  • Trapped 250,000 troops (no sea evacuation possible)
  • April-May: Final Allied offensives would end campaign
  • Tunisia becoming "Tunisgrad" - Axis Stalingrad

Quarter of learning: US blooded at Kasserine, Eighth Army broke Mareth, Axis squeezed into shrinking perimeter
