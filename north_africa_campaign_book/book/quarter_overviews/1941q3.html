North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

1941-Q3: Desert Stalemate (July-September 1941)

Strategic Situation

Following failed Operation Battleaxe (June 1941), both sides paused for rebuilding. Tobruk siege continued with 25,000+ Allied garrison supplied by Royal Navy. British prepared Operation Crusader offensive for November. Germans reinforced DAK but suffered supply crisis from Malta interdiction.

Major Operations

July-September: No Major Offensives

Both sides engaged in:

  • Aggressive patrolling
  • Supply buildup
  • Training new formations
  • Equipment replacement

Tobruk Siege Operations

  • Daily artillery exchanges
  • German/Italian probing attacks repulsed
  • RAF vs Luftwaffe air battles
  • Royal Navy supply runs (destroyers, submarines)
  • Garrison rotations: Polish Carpathian Brigade, British 70th Division replace Australian units

Forces Building Up

British Eighth Army (redesignated from Western Desert Force):

  • XIII Corps (infantry): 4 divisions
  • XXX Corps (armored): 7th Armoured Division, 1st South African Division
  • Total: 118,000 troops, 700+ tanks arriving

Deutsches Afrikakorps/Panzergruppe Afrika:

  • DAK: 15. Panzer-Division, 21. Panzer-Division (redesignated from 5. leichte)
  • Italian XX Corps, XXI Corps
  • Total: 119,000 Axis troops, 278 tanks

Supply Crisis

British Supply Advantage:

  • Alexandria port: 250 miles from front
  • Tobruk supplied by sea (Royal Navy)
  • Tank reinforcements via Operation Crusader convoy

Axis Supply Crisis:

  • Malta interdiction: 30-40% of shipping sunk
  • Benghazi bombed repeatedly by RAF
  • Fuel shortages critical (tanks immobilized)
  • 1,900 km supply line from Tripoli

Equipment Developments

British Reinforcements:

  • American M3 Stuart light tanks arriving
  • Crusader cruiser tanks (unreliable, but 150+ arriving)
  • Valentine infantry tanks
  • 6-pounder AT guns (limited numbers)

German Equipment Issues:

  • Panzer III/IV mechanical wear from desert conditions
  • Shortage of spare parts
  • 88mm Flak guns critical (only 18 available)

Tactical Developments

British Training:

  • Combined arms exercises
  • Desert navigation training
  • Tank-infantry coordination drills

German Defensive Preparations:

  • Minefield belts along frontier
  • Tobruk siege lines reinforced
  • Mobile reserves positioned

Weather and Terrain

Temperature: 30-45°C (extreme summer heat) Sandstorms: Frequent, reduced visibility Vehicle Breakdowns: Heat-related engine failures common

Historical Significance

Stalemate period allowed British massive buildup for Operation Crusader. German supply crisis worsened despite lack of major operations. Malta interdiction proved decisive strategic factor. Both sides learned lessons applied in November offensive.

Build-up quarter: British gained numerical superiority, Axis suffered logistics crisis
