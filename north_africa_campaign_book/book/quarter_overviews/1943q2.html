North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

1943-Q2: Final Victory - Axis Surrender in Tunisia (April-May 1943)

Strategic Situation

Axis Armeegruppe Afrika (250,000 troops, 130 tanks) trapped in Tunis-Bizerte perimeter. Allied forces (300,000 troops, 1,400 tanks) prepared final offensive to destroy Axis bridgehead. No sea evacuation possible (Allied naval/air superiority). Axis defeat inevitable.

Major Battles

April 6-9: Battle of Wadi Akarit

  • Eighth Army attacks Axis delaying position
  • April 6 night: 4th Indian, 50th, 51st Highland Divisions assault
  • Fierce fighting, Axis retreat after 3 days
  • Result: Last defensive line before Tunis-Bizerte perimeter breached

April 19-30: First Assault on Tunis Perimeter

  • Allied attacks: First Army (north), II Corps (center), Eighth Army (south)
  • April 23: Battle of Longstop Hill (British 78th Division)
  • German resistance fierce (Hermann Göring, 334. Infantry Divisions)
  • Attacks repulsed, Allies regroup for final offensive

May 6-7: Operation Vulcan - Final Offensive

  • May 6: Concentrated attacks all sectors
  • British IX Corps breakthrough (Medjez el Bab)
  • US 1st, 9th Infantry Divisions advance
  • Luftwaffe flies last supply missions (heavy losses)

May 7: Fall of Tunis and Bizerte

  • May 7 morning: British 7th Armoured Division enters Tunis
  • US II Corps captures Bizerte simultaneously
  • Axis forces fragmented
  • Von Arnim orders each formation to fight independently

May 8-13: Collapse and Surrender

  • May 8: Organized resistance collapses
  • Axis forces retreat to Cap Bon peninsula (no escape route)
  • May 9: Von Arnim captured by British 4th Division
  • May 10-12: Mass surrenders
  • May 13, 1943: Final Axis units surrender (Italian First Army)

Equipment Balance

  • Tanks: 1,400 (Shermans, Grants, Churchills, Crusaders)
  • Artillery: 2,000+ guns

  • Supplies: Unlimited (Algiers, Tripoli, Sfax ports operational)

  • Tanks: 130 operational (April) → 60 (May) → 20 (May 13)

Final Battles Detail

Longstop Hill (April 23)

  • Key terrain dominating Tunis approach
  • British 78th Division attacks German 334. Infantry Division
  • Brutal fighting: Hill changed hands 3 times
  • Result: British secure hill, open route to Tunis

Medjez el Bab Breakthrough (May 6)

  • British IX Corps massed attack
  • 600 guns bombard German positions
  • 6th/7th Armoured Divisions exploit gap
  • German 15. Panzer Division shattered (20 tanks remaining)

Cap Bon Peninsula (May 10-13)

  • Axis forces retreat to Cape Bon (easternmost Tunisia)
  • No evacuation possible (Allied naval blockade, air superiority)
  • Italian First Army (12 divisions) surrenders en masse
  • German rearguards fight briefly, then surrender

Air and Naval Operations

Allied Air Supremacy:

  • 3,000 sorties daily (April-May)
  • Tunis/Bizerte airfields bombed constantly
  • Supply ships sunk (100% interdiction rate)
  • Luftwaffe transport losses: 432 aircraft destroyed March-May

Naval Blockade:

  • Royal Navy patrols Tunisia coast
  • No evacuation possible (Sicily 100 miles, too far under Allied air)
  • Contrast to Dunkirk: Axis has no "miracle escape"

Strategic Significance

Tunisia campaign ended Axis presence in North Africa. More German POWs than Stalingrad. Hitler's decision to reinforce Tunisia (instead of evacuating) cost Germany 250,000 irreplaceable veteran troops.

Allied Gains:

  • North Africa secure (Mediterranean shipping lanes open)
  • Seasoned armies ready for Sicily (July 1943) then Italy
  • US Army combat-tested (II Corps learned hard lessons)
  • Strategic initiative: Allies choose next objective

Axis Strategic Disaster:

  • Loss of North Africa, loss of 275,000 POWs
  • Italian morale shattered (home defense threatened)
  • Wehrmacht diverted reserves to Tunisia, weakened Eastern Front
  • Mediterranean opened to Allied shipping (shortened supply routes)

Command and Lessons

Allied Command Success:

  • Eisenhower coordinated multi-national force
  • Alexander deputy commander (Eighth + First Armies cooperation)
  • Combined arms doctrine perfected
  • Air-ground coordination effective

Axis Command Failure:

  • Divided command (Rommel vs von Arnim rivalry until March)
  • No unified strategy
  • Hitler's "no retreat" orders doomed trapped army
  • Supply crisis ignored until too late

Historical Significance - End of North African Campaign

May 13, 1943: Last Axis forces surrender. North African campaign ends after 2 years, 11 months (June 1940 - May 1943).

Campaign Statistics:

  • Major battles: 20+ (from Operation Compass to Tunisia)
  • Territory: 2,000-mile battlefield (Egypt to Morocco)
  • Duration: 1,067 days
  • POWs: 400,000+ Axis (130,000 Operation Compass, 275,000 Tunisia)

Churchill to Roosevelt: "The African shore is now clear of the enemy... We have in our hands the initiative and the advantage."

Next Phase: Operation Husky (Sicily invasion, July 10, 1943) - Italian campaign begins.

Final victory: 275,000 Axis POWs, larger than Stalingrad - North African campaign ends, Mediterranean opened, Italy invasion next
