North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

1940-Q4: Operation Compass (October-December 1940)

Strategic Situation

British Western Desert Force (31,000 troops, 275 tanks) launched surprise offensive against Italian 10th Army (80,000 troops, 120 tanks) on December 9, 1940. What began as a 5-day raid became a 2-month pursuit destroying 10 Italian divisions.

Forces Structure

Italian Forces

10ª Armata (Tenth Army)

Commander: Generale di Armata Generale di Armata Mario Berti Strength: 48,645 personnel Headquarters: Bardia, Libya (Cyrenaica)

  • Vehicles: 3,862 total

Subordinate Units
132ª Divisione Corazzata "Ariete"

Commander: Generale di Divisione Ettore Baldassarre Type: Armored Division Strength: 7,500 personnel Notes: Italy's premier armored division. In Italy Q4 1940 preparing for Libya deployment (arrived Jan 1941). Some tank battalions already deployed separately to Libya.

17ª Divisione di Fanteria "Pavia"

Commander: Generale di Divisione Pietro Zaglio Type: Infantry Division (Binary - Autotrasportabile) Strength: 10,978 personnel Notes: Tripolitania garrison, NOT at Sidi Barrani front. Survived Operation Compass.

25ª Divisione di Fanteria "Bologna"

Commander: Generale di Divisione Roberto Lerici Type: Divisione Autotrasportabile AS (Auto-transportable Infantry Division, North Africa Type) Strength: 10,978 personnel Notes: AS-type division optimized for North Africa. Participated in Italian invasion of Egypt Sept 1940.

27ª Divisione di Fanteria "Brescia"

Commander: Generale di Divisione Giuseppe Cremascoli Type: Autotrasportabile Infantry Division Strength: 7,520 personnel Notes: Coastal defense west of Tripoli. Reserve status during Operation Compass opening.

55ª Divisione di Fanteria "Savona"

Commander: Generale di Divisione Pietro Maggiani Type: Auto-transportable Infantry Division Strength: 12,169 personnel Notes: Gharyan garrison (south of Tripoli). NOT engaged in Operation Compass Dec 1940.

Weather & Logistics

Environmental Conditions:

  • Season: 1940-Q4 (October-December) - Autumn/Early Winter in North Africa
  • Temperature: 10-26°C
  • Terrain: Coastal plain (Cyrenaica) and rocky desert - mixed terrain favoring mechanized warfare
  • Daylight: 10.5 hours average

Logistics Status:

  • Supply Situation: Critical - Catastrophic logistics situation for 10th Army Q4 1940. Main supply base Tripoli 1800km from forward positions at Sidi Barrani. British Operation Compass (9 Dec 1940) exposed Italian supply vulnerabilities.   • Fuel Reserves: 8.1 days
  • Ammunition: 14.5 days stockpile
  • Water: 7.8L/man/day requirement
  • Operational Radius: 280km

Major Battles

December 9-11: Battle of Sidi Barrani

  • British attack Italian fortified camps at Sidi Barrani
  • 7th Armoured Division encircles, 4th Indian Division assaults
  • Results: 38,000 Italian POWs, 73 tanks captured, 237 guns captured
  • Maletti Group destroyed (M11/39 tanks eliminated)

January 3-5, 1941: Capture of Bardia

  • 6th Australian Division storms fortress garrison
  • 45,000 Italian POWs, 462 guns, 129 tanks captured
  • 62nd Marmarica Division destroyed

January 21-22: Battle of Tobruk

  • British encircle and assault Tobruk fortress
  • 25,000 POWs, 208 guns, 87 tanks captured
  • Port facilities intact (critical supply base)

February 5-7: Battle of Beda Fomm

  • 7th Armoured Division cuts off retreating Italian 10th Army
  • Final destruction: 20,000 POWs, 216 guns, 120 tanks
  • Total Operation Compass: 130,000 POWs, 400 tanks, 1,290 guns

Participating Forces

British Commonwealth:

  • 7th Armoured Division: 275 tanks (Cruiser, Matilda II, Light)
  • 4th Indian Division → replaced by 6th Australian Division
  • Support Group, artillery regiments
  • Royal Navy bombardment support

Italian 10th Army (destroyed formations):

  • 62nd Marmarica Division
  • 63rd Cirene Division
  • 61st Sirte Division
  • 1st/2nd/4th Blackshirt Divisions
  • Maletti Group (armored)
  • 60th Sabratha Division

Tactical Analysis

British Advantages:

  • Matilda II tanks: 78mm frontal armor (immune to Italian 47mm AT guns)
  • Superior tactics: Combined arms, radio communication
  • Aggressive leadership (O'Connor, Creagh)
  • Naval gunfire support

Italian Weaknesses:

  • Static defense in isolated camps
  • L3/35 tankettes ineffective (13mm armor, machine guns only)
  • M11/39 design flaws (37mm gun in hull, limited traverse)
  • Poor command and control

Historical Significance

Operation Compass destroyed Italian 10th Army, advanced 500 miles in 2 months, captured 130,000 POWs with only 500 British casualties. Victory demonstrated superiority of mobile armored warfare over static defense.

Strategic Impact: British conquest of Cyrenaica, capture of Tobruk (major port), but victory cut short by transfer of troops to Greece (February 1941), allowing German intervention.

Operation Compass remains one of most lopsided victories in military history
