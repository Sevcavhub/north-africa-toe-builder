North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

1941-Q1: Beda Fomm and German Arrival (January-March 1941)

Strategic Situation

British Western Desert Force (36,000 troops) completed destruction of Italian 10th Army at Beda Fomm (February 5-7). German Afrika Korps (two divisions) arrived Libya (February-March) to prevent Axis collapse. Quarter ended with British weakened by diversion of forces to Greece, Germans preparing first offensive.

Major Battles

January 3-5: Capture of Bardia

  • 6th Australian Division storms fortress
  • 45,000 Italian POWs, 462 guns, 129 tanks captured
  • Significance: Opened route to Tobruk

January 21-22: Capture of Tobruk

  • 6th Australian Division + 7th Armoured Division assault
  • 25,000 Italian POWs, 208 guns, 87 tanks captured
  • Strategic: Key port secured for British supply line

February 5-7: Battle of Beda Fomm

  • 7th Armoured Division cuts off retreating Italian 10th Army
  • Italian columns trapped between desert and sea
  • February 7: Italian surrender
  • Total Operation Compass results: 130,000 POWs, 400 tanks, 1,290 guns captured

February 12: Rommel Arrives Libya

  • Generalleutnant Erwin Rommel appointed commander Afrika Korps
  • Orders: Prevent British advance to Tripoli
  • Initial forces: 5. leichte Division (March arrival)

March 24: First German Attack

  • Rommel probes British positions at El Agheila
  • British weakened (4th Indian, 6th Australian divisions sent to Greece)
  • Rommel recognizes British vulnerability

Forces Engaged

British Western Desert Force (Lt-Gen Richard O'Connor):

  • 7th Armoured Division (worn out, 50% vehicles needing overhaul)
  • 6th Australian Division (transferred to Greece March)
  • 2nd Armoured Division (green, just arrived)
  • Total: 25,000 troops (February), reduced to 15,000 (March)

German Afrika Korps (Generalleutnant Erwin Rommel):

    1. leichte Division (March): 120 tanks (Panzer III, Panzer IV)
  • Reconnaissance units (February)
  • Total: 9,000 troops, 120 tanks (March 31)

Italian Forces (Remnants):

  • Ariete Armored Division (reformed)
  • Brescia, Pavia Infantry Divisions
  • Total: 40,000 troops (March), mostly rebuilt units

Operation Compass Final Results

December 9, 1940 - February 7, 1941:

  • Advance: 500 miles (Sidi Barrani to Beda Fomm)
  • Italian casualties: 130,000 POWs, 845 guns, 380 tanks captured
  • British casualties: 500 killed, 1,373 wounded, 55 missing
  • Cost-effectiveness: 133:1 POW ratio

Churchill's Demand: Continue to Tripoli (1,000 miles west) Wavell's Decision: Divert forces to Greece (political imperative) Result: Victory incomplete, Germans rescued Axis position

Equipment Lessons

Matilda II Dominance:

  • 78mm armor immune to all Italian AT guns
  • Destroyed Italian M11/39, M13/40 tanks at will
  • Weakness: Slow speed (15 mph), mechanical breakdowns

A13 Cruiser Problems:

  • Mechanical unreliability (50% breakdown rate)
  • Thin armor (14mm sides, vulnerable to Italian 47mm)
  • Advantage: Speed (30 mph) enabled encirclements

German Panzer III/IV Arrival:

  • 50mm gun (Panzer III) superior to British 2-pounder
  • 75mm gun (Panzer IV) HE capability (British lacked HE)
  • Radio communication standard (coordinated attacks)

Strategic Turning Point

British Dilemma:

  • Greece expedition (4 divisions) weakened Western Desert Force
  • Political commitment vs military logic
  • 7th Armoured Division worn out (2,000 miles of operations)

German Intervention:

  • Hitler's Directive 22 (January 11): Send Afrika Korps to Libya
  • Prevent Italian collapse
  • Rommel's aggressive leadership vs British weakness

April 1941 Preview:

  • Rommel would launch first offensive (April 2)
  • British retreat to Egyptian frontier
  • Tobruk besieged (April 11)
  • Western Desert Force's triumph reversed in 6 weeks

Historical Significance

Beda Fomm completed destruction of Italian 10th Army - greatest British victory of war to date. 130,000 POWs exceeded entire British force in Egypt. But diversion to Greece and German arrival transformed strategic situation. British squandered opportunity to conquer Libya, setting stage for 18-month seesaw campaign.

Casualties 1941-Q1:

  • Italian: 130,000 (mostly POWs from Bardia, Tobruk, Beda Fomm)
  • British: 2,000 (Operation Compass losses)
  • German: Negligible (not yet engaged)

Strategic Impact:

  • Operation Compass ended Italian threat to Egypt
  • German intervention prevented Axis collapse
  • British forces diverted to Greece (doomed campaign)
  • Rommel's arrival changed character of North African war

British triumph at Beda Fomm, then strategic blunder: Sent winners to Greece, faced Rommel with depleted garrison
