North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

1942-Q2: Battle of Gazala and Fall of Tobruk (April-June 1942)

Strategic Situation

British Eighth Army (110,000 troops, 850 tanks) defended Gazala Line against Panzerarmee Afrika (90,000 troops, 560 tanks). Rommel attacked May 26 seeking to destroy British armor and capture Tobruk. Six-week battle ended with British defeat, Tobruk's fall, and retreat into Egypt.

Major Battles

May 26-27: Rommel's Left Hook

  • Axis feint at Gazala Line northern sector
  • Main attack: DAK/Italian XX Corps sweep around southern flank (Bir Hakeim)
  • Night march: 10,000 vehicles around Free French position
  • May 27 morning: German armor attacks British rear areas

May 27-June 1: The Cauldron

  • British 7th Armoured Division counterattacks piecemeal
  • Tank battles: Grant debut (75mm gun effective)
  • German defensive box: "The Cauldron" inside British minefields
  • Rommel surrounded, desperate supply situation

May 27 - June 11: Siege of Bir Hakeim

  • 1re Brigade Française Libre (3,700 troops) holds southern anchor
  • General Koenig's Free French garrison
  • 16 days of siege: German/Italian assaults repulsed
  • June 10-11: Breakout - 2,700 escape, 1,000 casualties
  • Bir Hakeim falls June 11

June 1-6: 150th Brigade Box Destroyed

  • German assault on 150th Infantry Brigade Box
  • 3,000 British POWs (June 1)
  • Rommel breaks through minefields
  • Supply route to Cauldron opened

June 5-13: Destruction of British Armor

  • Operation Aberdeen: British armored counterattack fails
  • June 12-13: Knightsbridge tank battle
    • British lose 120+ tanks
    • 7th Armoured, 1st Armoured Divisions shattered
  • British armor retreats, Gazala Line collapses

June 14-21: Tobruk Siege and Capture

  • Rommel pursues to Tobruk (rejected bypassing)
  • June 20-21: Assault on Tobruk fortress
  • Luftwaffe dive bombers suppress defenses
  • June 21: Tobruk falls - 33,000 POWs, 2,000 vehicles, fuel/supply dumps
  • Churchill: "Defeat is one thing, disgrace is another"

June 23-30: Retreat to El Alamein

  • British Eighth Army retreats 350 miles
  • Mersa Matruh abandoned June 28
  • Stabilize at El Alamein Line (July 1)

  • XXX Corps: 1st Armoured, 7th Armoured Divisions
  • 1re Brigade Française Libre (Bir Hakeim)
  • Tanks: 850 (300 Grants, 170 Matildas, 380 Cruisers/Stuarts)

Panzerarmee Afrika (Generalfeldmarschall Erwin Rommel):

  • DAK: 15. Panzer, 21. Panzer Divisions
    1. leichte Division

  • Italian X/XXI Infantry Corps
  • Tanks: 560 (332 German, 228 Italian)

Equipment Performance

M3 Grant Tank:

  • 75mm gun (HE + AP capability) revolutionary
  • Sponson mount limited traverse
  • Effective vs Panzer III/IV
  • British finally had HE-firing tank

German 88mm Flak:

  • Destroyed Grants at 2,000+ meter range
  • Decisive in tank battles
  • British had no counter (6-pounder scarce)

Italian M13/40:

  • Outclassed by Grants
  • 47mm gun ineffective vs Grant 51mm armor
  • Ariete Division fought well despite equipment inferiority

Tactical Analysis

British Failures:

  • Dispersed armor (brigades fought separately)
  • No concentrated counterattack when Rommel vulnerable (May 28-31)
  • Infantry boxes isolated, defeated piecemeal
  • Poor combined arms coordination

German Successes:

  • Concentrated armor (massed panzer divisions)
  • Combined arms: tanks + 88mm AT screens + artillery
  • Aggressive leadership (Rommel forward command)
  • Exploitation: Pursued defeated enemy vigorously

Bir Hakeim Defense

Free French Achievement:

  • 16-day siege vs repeated assaults
  • Delayed Rommel's southern sweep
  • 70% garrison escaped
  • Restored French military honor (after 1940 defeat)
  • De Gaulle: "The whole world knows France is fighting"

Fall of Tobruk Impact

Strategic Shock:

  • 33,000 POWs (mostly South African 2nd Division)
  • 2,000 vehicles, 5,000 tons supplies captured
  • Fuel dumps: Allowed Rommel to pursue into Egypt
  • Churchill: Learned of Tobruk's fall while meeting Roosevelt in Washington

Rommel Promoted:

  • Youngest Generalfeldmarschall in Wehrmacht
  • "Desert Fox" legend at peak

Historical Significance

Gazala-Tobruk was Rommel's greatest victory: Destroyed British armor, captured fortress, advanced to El Alamein (60 miles from Alexandria). British command failure: Numerical superiority squandered by dispersed tactics and hesitant leadership.

Casualties:

  • British: 50,000 (killed, wounded, POW), 540 tanks lost
  • Axis: 3,360 Germans, 3,000 Italians casualties, 114 tanks lost

Strategic Impact: Rommel reached high-water mark (El Alamein July 1942). Overextended supply lines would prove fatal. British would rebuild and win decisively at Second Alamein (October-November 1942).

Rommel's masterpiece: Tactical brilliance vs British command dysfunction - Tobruk's fall shocked Allied world
