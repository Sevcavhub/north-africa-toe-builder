North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

62a Divisione di Fanteria "Marmarica"

Nation: Italian
Quarter: 1940-Q2 (April-June 1940) Commander: Generale di Divisione Unknown Type: Infantry Division (Binary - Autotrasportabile) Parent Formation: X Armata (Tenth Army) HQ: Bardia, Libya

1. Overview

The 62a Divisione di Fanteria "Marmarica" served as one of Italy's binary autotrasportabile divisions defending Libya when Italy entered WWII on June 10, 1940. With 10,864 soldiers and 357 vehicles (280+ trucks), Marmarica represented Italy's effort to create truck-mobile infantry divisions—though autotrasportabile meant sufficient trucks to transport the division in 2-3 lifts, NOT simultaneous movement.

Stationed at Bardia (Libya's easternmost fortress), Marmarica formed part of X Armata defending the Libyan-Egyptian frontier. Like Pavia and Brescia, Marmarica exemplified the March 1940 binary reorganization.

Key Features:

  • Binary autotrasportabile: 2 regiments + 280 trucks (vs. Cirene's 145)
  • Bardia fortress garrison, critical border strongpoint
  • X Armata assignment, main border defense force
  • Metropolitan division, Italian regulars

Critical Weaknesses:

  • Only 8 x 47/32mm AT guns (ineffective vs. Matilda 78mm armor)
  • Minimal AA: 4 x 20mm guns for 11,000 troops
  • No AFVs organic to division
  • Autotrasportabile NOT motorized: 2-3 lifts needed

2. Command and Equipment

Personnel: 10,864 total (404 officers, 2,200 NCOs, 8,260 enlisted) Staff: 302 (34 officers, 82 NCOs, 186 enlisted) Small Arms: Carcano M1891 (7,200), Breda M30 LMG (242), Fiat-Revelli HMG (74) Artillery (48 guns): 75/27 (24), 100/17 (12), 47/32 AT (8), 20/65 AA (4) Vehicles (357): Trucks (280+), motorcycles (45), support (32)

Autotrasportabile Assessment: 280 trucks could transport 3,000-3,500 troops (30-35%) per lift, requiring 3 round trips. Superior to foot-mobile Cirene (145 trucks) but inferior to fully motorized divisions.

3. Operations and Fate

1940-Q2 (April-June 1940):

  • Bardia fortress garrison, frontier defense
  • Border observation, intelligence gathering
  • Logistical preparation for war
  • Coordination with Cirene, Sirte divisions

Italy Entry (June 10, 1940): Active defense of Bardia sector.

Operation Compass Destruction: Marmarica destroyed during British Operation Compass (Dec 1940-Feb 1941). Bardia fell to 6th Australian Division (Jan 3-5, 1941):

  • 45,000 Italian POWs (multiple divisions including Marmarica)
  • Bardia capture opened coastal road to Tobruk
  • Division destroyed as combat formation

Factors:

  1. Static defense vs. British mobile mechanized warfare
  2. AT weakness: 8 x 47mm guns useless vs. Matilda
  3. Fortress mentality: fixed defenses vulnerable to bypass
  4. No armored support to counter British tanks
