North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

Deutsches Afrikakorps (DAK)

Nation: German Quarter: 1941-Q2 (April-June 1941) Commander: Generalleutnant Erwin Rommel Type: Panzer Corps Parent Formation: OKW Direct Command Formation Date: February 19, 1941

1. Overview

The Deutsches Afrikakorps achieved legendary status during 1941-Q2 as Generalleutnant Erwin Rommel's two-division panzer corps conducted its first major offensive in North Africa. With 31,160 personnel, 320 tanks (290 operational), and 2,866 vehicles, the DAK demonstrated German combined-arms mastery during Operation Sonnenblume—Rommel's audacious drive from Tripoli to the Egyptian border (March-May 1941) and the First Battle of Tobruk siege.

Formed February 19, 1941 as Germany's emergency response to Italy's catastrophic Operation Compass losses, the DAK initially comprised two divisions: 5. leichte Division (later 21. Panzer-Division) and 15. Panzer-Division. Under Rommel's aggressive leadership, this small force achieved operational victories far exceeding its numerical strength.

Key Operations:

  • First offensive (March 31 - May 30): recaptured Cyrenaica, 800km advance in 6 weeks
  • Tobruk siege: April 10 - December 1941 (isolated 25,000+ Commonwealth troops)
  • Operation Brevity repulse (May 15-16): defeated British counteroffensive

2. Command and Equipment

Commander: Generalleutnant Erwin Rommel (appointed Feb 6, 1941) Chief of Staff: Oberst Alfred Gause (appointed April 15, 1941) HQ Staff: 700 personnel

Subordinate Divisions:

    1. leichte Division: 12,000 personnel, 155 tanks
    1. Panzer-Division: 14,000+ personnel, 165 tanks

Tank Strength (320 total, 290 operational):

  • Panzer III variants (170): 50mm guns, 30-60mm armor
  • Panzer IV (28): 75mm short guns, HE support role
  • Panzer II (122): 20mm guns, reconnaissance

Anti-Tank: 50mm PaK 38 (48), 88mm Flak (24)—legendary dual-role AA/AT Artillery: 105mm leFH 18 (48), 150mm sFH 18 (12)

3. Operations: First Offensive

March 31 - April 10: El Agheila to Tobruk

  • Rommel attacked despite orders to remain defensive
  • Exploited British overextension (forces withdrawn to Greece)
  • 800km advance in 10 days
  • Captured Mechili (April 8): 2,000+ POWs including generals O'Connor and Neame

Tobruk Siege (April 10 onward):

  • 9th Australian Division isolated in fortress (25,000+ troops)
  • First assault (April 11-14): repulsed with heavy losses
  • Siege continued 8 months

Operation Brevity (May 15-16):

  • British counteroffensive repulsed
  • 88mm AT guns demonstrated devastating effectiveness

4. Historical Significance

Rommel Legend: 800km advance with two divisions established "Desert Fox" reputation Desert Warfare Mastery: Combined arms tactics, 88mm Flak in AT role, aggressive reconnaissance Logistical Constraints: 800km supply line from Tripoli became chronic limitation
