63ª Divisione di Fanteria "Cirene"

Nation: Italian Quarter: 1940-Q2 (April-June 1940) Commander: Generale di Divisione Unknown (not documented) Type: Metropolitan Infantry Division (Binary) Parent Formation: Comando Superiore Forze Armate Africa Settentrionale

1. Overview

The 63ª Divisione di Fanteria "Cirene" stood as one of Italy's pre-war metropolitan garrison divisions defending Eastern Cyrenaica when Italy entered World War II on June 10, 1940. With 12,169 soldiers permanently stationed in Libya, Cirene Division manned defensive positions along the Egyptian border. The division exemplified Italy's binary infantry division structure implemented March 1940—reducing regiments from three to two.

Unlike autotrasportabile divisions (Pavia, Brescia), Cirene remained a foot-mobile division with only 145 trucks, dependent on animal transport. The division's two infantry regiments (157° and 158° Reggimento "LIGURIA") plus attached Legione CC.NN. "Cirene" (1,305 militia) represented binary organization with political commissar complications.

Critical Weaknesses:

  • Only 8 × 47/32mm AT guns (ineffective vs. Matilda 78mm armor)
  • Minimal AA defense: 4 × 20mm guns
  • No AFVs organic to division
  • 145 trucks insufficient for 12,000 troops (20% mobility)

Historical Significance: Division destroyed during Operation Compass (December 1940-February 1941).

6. Historical Significance

Binary Division Experiment: March 1940 reorganization aimed to increase division count without expanding army. Reality: reduced combat depth (2 regiments vs. 3), insufficient reserves.

Operation Compass Destruction: Factors leading to destruction:

  1. Immobility: couldn't withdraw from British mechanized bypass
  2. AT weakness: 8 × 47mm guns useless vs. Matilda
  3. No armored support
  4. Static defense doctrine vs. mobile mechanized warfare

Results (Dec 9-11, 1940): 36,000 Italian POWs, eastern Libya lost, 800km British advance.
