North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

132ª Divisione corazzata "Ariete"

Italian Armored Division, 1942-Q4 (October-December 1942)

1. Overview

The fourth quarter of 1942 witnessed Ariete Division's destruction at Second Battle of El Alamein (October 23-November 7) under Generale di Brigata Francesco Antonio Arena. With 7,200 personnel and tanks ~100-110, the division fought desperate defensive battle against overwhelming British forces. On November 4, 1942, Ariete ceased to exist as fighting formation—practically annihilated covering German/Italian retreat. Division's sacrifice allowed partial Axis escape from encirclement.

Key Statistics (1942-Q4):

  • Personnel: 7,200 (start), ~500-1,000 survivors
  • Tanks: ~100-110 (start), 0 (end)
  • Commander: Generale di Brigata Francesco Antonio Arena
  • Operations: Second Alamein (Oct 23-Nov 7), destruction Nov 4
  • Strategic Result: Division destroyed, heroic sacrifice

2. Second Battle of El Alamein

Montgomery's offensive (October 23) opened with devastating artillery—million+ shells. Ariete held southern sector against British armored brigades. M13/40 tanks fought hull-down but British Shermans (75mm guns) destroyed Italian positions systematically. By November 2, Axis line collapsing—Italian infantry divisions surrendering, German tanks withdrawing.

3. Ariete's Last Stand (November 4, 1942)

Rommel ordered retreat November 3. Ariete assigned rearguard: delay British pursuit while German/Italian forces escaped. Division fought desperate delaying action at Tel el Aqqaqir—all tanks destroyed, most personnel killed/captured. Final radio message to Italian headquarters: "Tanks destroyed, Ariete surrounded, holding position." By day's end, division ceased to exist.

Survivors (estimated 500-1,000 from 7,200): scattered stragglers, wounded in hospitals, small groups escaping encirclement. Effective combat strength: zero.

4. Italian Sacrifice

Ariete's destruction enabled partial Axis escape: German panzer divisions withdrew relatively intact while Italian formations sacrificed. Division's two-year combat record (1940-1942) demonstrated Italian tactical competence, bravery, and professionalism despite chronic material inferiority. M13/40 tanks obsolescent by 1942, logistics inadequate, but crews fought to annihilation.

5. Historical Significance

Ariete Division's destruction symbolized Italian military tragedy in North Africa: brave soldiers, competent officers, inadequate equipment, systemic failures. Division earned German respect through sacrifice—Rommel praised Italian courage even while criticizing Italian high command. No reconstitution attempted—Italy's armored capability in Africa effectively ended November 4, 1942.

Churchill's famous quote applies: "Before Alamein we never had a victory. After Alamein we never had a defeat." For Ariete, Alamein meant total destruction defending lost cause.
