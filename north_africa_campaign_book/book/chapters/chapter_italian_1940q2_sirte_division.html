North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

61a Divisione di Fanteria "Sirte"

Nation: Italian Quarter: 1940-Q2 (April-June 1940)
Commander: Generale di Divisione Unknown Type: Infantry Division (Binary - Autotrasportabile) Parent Formation: Comando Superiore Africa Settentrionale HQ: Derna sector, Libya

1. Overview

The 61a Divisione di Fanteria "Sirte" served as one of Italy's binary autotrasportabile divisions in Libya's central coastal sector when Italy entered WWII. With approximately 10,800-11,000 soldiers and 280+ trucks, Sirte shared organization with sister divisions Pavia, Brescia, and Marmarica—representing Italy's attempt to create truck-mobile infantry capable of desert operations.

Stationed in the Derna sector (midway between Benghazi and Tobruk), Sirte formed part of the layered defense protecting Cyrenaica's coastal road. The division's autotrasportabile status theoretically enabled rapid repositioning, though in practice 2-3 lift requirements limited tactical flexibility.

Key Features:

  • Binary autotrasportabile: 2 regiments + 280 trucks
  • Derna sector: central Cyrenaica coastal defense
  • Metropolitan division: Italian regulars from mainland
  • March 1940 reorganization: binary structure

Critical Weaknesses:

  • Only 8 x 47/32mm AT guns (ineffective vs. Matilda)
  • Minimal AA: 4 x 20mm guns
  • No AFVs organic to division
  • 280 trucks = 3-lift requirement for full division

2. Command and Equipment

Personnel: ~10,800-11,000 total (binary division standard) Staff: ~300 personnel (divisional HQ) Small Arms: Carcano M1891 (~7,000), Breda M30 LMG (~240), Breda M37/Fiat-Revelli HMG (~75) Artillery (48 guns): 75/27 Mod. 06 (24), 100/17 Mod. 14 (12), 47/32 AT (8), 20/65 AA (4) Mortars: 81mm Mod. 35 (18), 45mm Brixia (36) Vehicles: Trucks (280+), motorcycles (40-45), support (30+)

Autotrasportabile Reality: While designated "auto-transportable," Sirte required 2-3 round trips to move completely. First lift: combat elements (~3,500 troops). Subsequent lifts: support units, heavy equipment. Total movement time: 6-12 hours.

3. Operations and Fate

1940-Q2 Activities:

  • Derna sector garrison, coastal defense
  • Desert training, acclimatization
  • Logistical preparation
  • Coordination with X Armata units

Italy Entry (June 10, 1940): Active defensive posture in Derna sector.

Operation Compass Destruction: Sirte shared the fate of other Libyan garrison divisions during British Operation Compass (Dec 1940-Feb 1941):

  • Division destroyed as organized formation
  • Survivors captured or dispersed during British advance
  • Derna captured by Australian forces (January 1941)
  • Part of Italy's catastrophic 36,000+ POW losses

Lessons:

  1. Binary divisions lacked depth for sustained combat (only 2 regiments)
  2. AT deficiency fatal vs. British armored attacks
  3. Static defense inappropriate vs. mobile mechanized warfare
  4. Autotrasportabile insufficient - needed fully motorized/mechanized forces
