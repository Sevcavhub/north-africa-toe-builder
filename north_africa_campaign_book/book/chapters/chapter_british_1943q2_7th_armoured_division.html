North Africa Campaign 1940-1943: Complete Order of Battle

7th Armoured Division

British Armoured Division, 1943-Q2 (April-June 1943)

1. Overview

The second quarter of 1943 witnessed both triumphant conclusion and new beginning for the 7th Armoured Division. After 43 months of continuous combat (June 1940 - May 1943), the "Desert Rats" completed the North African campaign with the Axis surrender on May 13, 1943—over 275,000 German and Italian prisoners taken.

The quarter divided into two distinct phases: combat operations (April-May) and transformation (May-June). The division fought the Battle of Wadi Akarit (April 6-7), participated in the final Tunisia offensive, then entered intensive rest and reorganization near Sousse and Sfax. With tank strength at 172 (100 Shermans) and commander Major-General George Erskine continuing, the division transformed from desert warfare specialists into amphibious assault-capable forces preparing for Operation Husky (Sicily invasion, July 1943).

Key Statistics (1943-Q2):

  • Personnel: 14,650 (reduced from 14,920 due to final battle casualties)
  • Tanks: 172 total (100 Shermans, 20 Crusader III, 52 Stuarts)
  • Commander: Major-General George Erskine
  • Operations: Wadi Akarit (Apr 6-7), final Tunisia offensive, Axis surrender (May 13)
  • Strategic Result: North Africa won, preparing for Sicily

2. Final Tunisia Battles: April-May 1943

Battle of Wadi Akarit (April 6-7, 1943)

The Wadi Akarit defensive line—Axis positions anchored on seasonal riverbed and hills—blocked routes to Tunis. The division participated in exploitation phase following infantry breakthrough (50th Division, Indian 4th Division). Rapid advance through Kairouan gap pursued withdrawing German forces. Breakthrough opened final Tunisia offensive.

Final Offensive (April-May 1943)

The division joined Eighth Army's final push coordinating with British First Army and US II Corps. Advance toward Tunis and Bizerte reduced Axis pockets systematically. Less intense combat than earlier Tunisia battles—Axis forces collapsing under overwhelming Allied pressure from three directions.

Axis Surrender (May 13, 1943)

Over 275,000 German and Italian prisoners surrendered May 13, 1943—entire Axis North African forces destroyed. The 7th Armoured Division's 43 months of continuous combat (June 1940 - May 1943) ended. The North African campaign, which had defined the "Desert Rats" identity, concluded in complete Allied victory.

3. Victory and Transformation: May-June 1943

Post-Victory Rest (May 1943)

Following surrender, the division moved to rest areas near Sousse and Sfax (Tunisia coast). After 43 months continuous operations, personnel required leave, casualties needed replacement, equipment demanded overhaul. Victory celebrations and ceremonial parades marked campaign's end. Morale peaked at 10/10—highest of entire war.

Intensive Maintenance (Early June 1943)

REME workshops conducted complete overhaul program: 172 tanks inspected and refurbished, 2,165 trucks serviced, armoured cars maintained. Operational rates improved dramatically—tanks from 91% (combat operations) to 97% (post-maintenance), vehicles from 94% to 98%. Crusader III phaseout continued (reduced from 32 to 20), Sherman allocation increased (92 to 100).

Sicily Training (Mid-Late June 1943)

The division began intensive training for Operation Husky (Sicily invasion, July 10, 1943)—first amphibious assault landing for the "Desert Rats." Training focused on:

  • Beach landing procedures (vehicle unloading from LSTs/LCTs)
  • Rapid movement off beaches (avoiding congestion)
  • Coordination with Royal Navy (ship-to-shore communications)
  • Tank waterproofing (exhaust extensions, sealed hatches)
  • Close air support coordination (RAF/USAAF)
  • Sicily terrain study (mountainous terrain vs desert/Tunisia plains)

This represented fundamental operational shift: from desert mobile warfare to Mediterranean amphibous operations and mountainous terrain.

4. Equipment Evolution

Sherman Dominance: Tank fleet now 100 Shermans (72 M4, 28 M4A2 diesel). Sherman reliability (98%+ operational after maintenance) and 75mm guns provided confidence. The M4A2 diesel variants increased for Sicily operations—better range than gasoline Shermans.

Crusader III Phaseout: Reduced to 20 tanks (from 32 Q1). Being replaced by Shermans. Some sent to workshops for parts cannibalization. May not deploy to Sicily—transitional status.

Stuart Light Tanks: 52 maintained (38 M3, 14 M5). Reconnaissance squadrons. Proven reliable in Tunisia.

6-pounder Standardization: All 18 anti-tank guns now 6-pounders. Last 2 remaining 2-pounders replaced. Standardization simplified ammunition supply and crew training.

5. Historical Significance

The second quarter of 1943 marked both ending and beginning for the 7th Armoured Division. The North African campaign—43 months defining the "Desert Rats" identity—concluded in complete victory. The division participated in Britain's first major WWII land victory (Operation Compass 1940-1941) and final triumph (Tunisia 1943).

Following victory, transformation began: desert warfare specialists became amphibious assault-capable combined-arms forces. June training established basic amphibious capability for Sicily invasion. The division retained core skills—combined arms mastery, aggressive exploitation, night operations, tank gunnery excellence—while adapting to new operational requirements.

The quarter demonstrated British industrial/logistics superiority. With multiple Tunisia ports operational (Bizerte, Tunis, Sousse, Sfax) and no combat consumption, supply situation reached unprecedented levels: 14 days fuel reserves (doubled from Q1), 21 days ammunition (nearly doubled). The division entered Sicily at peak combat effectiveness: 97% tank operational rate, 98% personnel strength, 10/10 morale.
