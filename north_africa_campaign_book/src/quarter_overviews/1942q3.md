# 1942-Q3: First El Alamein and Alam el Halfa (July-September 1942)

## Strategic Situation

Panzerarmee Afrika (96,000 troops, 200 operational tanks) pursued British Eighth Army (150,000 troops, 150 tanks) to El Alamein Line, 60 miles from Alexandria. July 1-27: First Battle of El Alamein halted Axis advance. August 31 - September 7: Battle of Alam el Halfa defeated Rommel's final offensive attempt.

## Major Battles

### July 1-4: First Alamein - Defensive Victory
- Rommel attacks El Alamein positions July 1
- British 1st South African Division holds Alamein Box
- **July 1**: DAK attacks Deir el Shein (British 18th Indian Brigade overrun)
- July 2-3: Auchinleck counterattacks, halts German advance
- Rommel pauses (exhaustion, fuel shortages)

### July 10-27: First Alamein - British Counterattacks
- **July 10-12**: Operation Bacon (New Zealand Division attacks Ruweisat Ridge)
  - Limited success, 15. Panzer Division counterattacks
- **July 14-16**: Operation Splendour (2nd New Zealand Division, 5th Indian Brigade)
  - Inconclusive, both sides exhausted
- **July 21-22**: Operation Manhood (Australian 9th Division attacks Tell el Eisa)
  - Captured Italian Sabratha Division positions
- **July 26-27**: Final attacks peter out
- **Result**: Stalemate - Axis advance halted, British too weak to exploit

### August 13: Command Changes
- General Bernard Montgomery assumes Eighth Army command
- General Harold Alexander becomes Middle East C-in-C
- Churchill visits, demands victory

### August 31 - September 7: Battle of Alam el Halfa
- **Rommel's Plan**: Left hook south, envelop British from Alam el Halfa ridge
- **Montgomery's Response**: Defensive battle, no dispersed armor
- **August 31 - September 1**: DAK night attack around southern flank
  - Bogged in soft sand, minefields
  - Fuel shortages critical
- **September 1-2**: German attacks on Alam el Halfa ridge repulsed
  - British 22nd Armoured Brigade (dug-in tanks, hull-down positions)
  - Grant tanks effective in defense
  - RAF dominates skies (1,000+ sorties daily)
- **September 3-7**: Rommel withdraws
  - Montgomery refuses pursuit (prepares for October offensive)
  - Rommel forced back to start positions

## Forces Engaged

**British Eighth Army** (Auchinleck, then Montgomery):
- X Corps: 1st, 10th Armoured Divisions (rebuilding)
- XIII Corps: 2nd New Zealand, 5th Indian, 7th Armoured Divisions
- XXX Corps: 9th Australian, 1st South African Divisions
- **Tanks**: 150 operational (July 1), 700+ operational (September 1)

**Panzerarmee Afrika** (Rommel):
- DAK: 15. Panzer, 21. Panzer, 90. leichte Divisions
- Italian X/XX/XXI Corps
- **Tanks**: 200 operational (July 1), 203 operational (September 1)
- **Critical**: Only 74 German tanks (129 Italian)

## Equipment Balance Shifts

**British Reinforcements (August-September)**:
- 300+ M4 Sherman tanks arrive (75mm gun, superior to Grant)
- M7 Priest 105mm self-propelled artillery
- 6-pounder AT guns in quantity
- Air superiority: 96 RAF squadrons vs 29 Axis

**Axis Shortages**:
- Panzer III/IV replacements inadequate
- Fuel critical (Malta interdiction 40% sinking rate)
- Ammunition rationed
- Italian M13/40 obsolete vs Shermans

## Alam el Halfa Tactical Innovations

**Montgomery's Defensive Doctrine**:
- Concentrated armor (no dispersal)
- Hull-down tank positions on ridge
- Extensive minefields channeling German attacks
- Artillery concentrations (350 guns massed)
- No pursuit (husband strength for Second Alamein)

**Rommel's Failure Factors**:
- Fuel shortage (only 3 days supply for offensive)
- Soft sand slowed advance
- RAF air dominance (constant bombing)
- British minefields thicker than expected
- Montgomery refused to react as Rommel predicted

## Supply Crisis

**Axis Supply Collapse**:
- Malta interdiction: 40% of shipping sunk August-September
- Benghazi-Tobruk bombed constantly
- Fuel reserves: 3 days (inadequate for offensive)
- Italian Navy reluctant to risk warships for supply runs

**British Supply Dominance**:
- Alexandria fully operational
- American equipment flooding in (Shermans, Grants, Priests)
- Short supply lines (60 miles from Alexandria to Alamein)
- RAF air superiority interdicts Axis supply columns

## Weather and Terrain

**Temperature**: 35-45°C (extreme summer heat)
**Terrain**:
- Alamein Line: Mediterranean coast to impassable Qattara Depression (40 miles)
- No open southern flank (unlike earlier battles)
- Ridge lines: Ruweisat, Alam el Halfa (key terrain)

## Command and Morale

**British Morale Transformation**:
- July: Defeat, retreat, low morale
- September: Montgomery's leadership, reinforcements, confidence restored
- "No withdrawal" orders: Fight at Alamein or die

**Axis Exhaustion**:
- Troops exhausted from July battles
- Rommel ill (desert sores, stomach ailment)
- Italian divisions demoralized
- German veterans aware of supply crisis

## Historical Significance

First El Alamein (July 1-27) halted Axis advance at Egypt's doorstep. Alam el Halfa (August 31 - September 7) defeated Rommel's final offensive attempt. Montgomery's defensive victory set conditions for Second Alamein offensive (October 23).

**Casualties First Alamein**:
- British: 13,000 (killed, wounded, captured)
- Axis: 13,000 (7,000 German, 6,000 Italian)

**Casualties Alam el Halfa**:
- British: 1,750
- Axis: 2,900 (plus 50 tanks, 15 aircraft)

**Strategic Impact**:
- Axis high-water mark: 60 miles from Alexandria, advance halted permanently
- British rebuilt strength: 1,000+ tanks, air superiority, supply dominance
- October: Montgomery would attack with 2:1 superiority, destroy Panzerarmee Afrika

---
*Turning point: Auchinleck halted Rommel's advance, Montgomery won defensive battle, set stage for Second Alamein victory*
