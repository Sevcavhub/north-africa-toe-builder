# 1942-Q4: Second El Alamein and Operation Torch (October-December 1942)

## Strategic Situation

British Eighth Army (195,000 troops, 1,029 tanks) launched Second Battle of El Alamein October 23 against Panzerarmee Afrika (104,000 troops, 489 tanks). Simultaneously, Allied Operation Torch landings Morocco/Algeria (November 8) created two-front threat. Quarter ended with Axis retreat 1,400 miles to Tunisia.

## Second Battle of El Alamein (October 23 - November 11)

### October 23-24: Operation Lightfoot Opens
- **2115 hours October 23**: 1,000-gun artillery barrage
- XXX Corps infantry assault: 4 divisions attack German/Italian positions
- Objective: Penetrate minefields, create corridors for armor
- **First night**: 4 miles penetration, heavy casualties

### October 24-November 1: Attritional Phase ("Crumbling")
- X Corps armor passes through minefields slowly
- German counterattacks: 15. Panzer, 21. Panzer, Littorio Divisions
- **October 26-27**: Operation Supercharge planned (new thrust line)
- Tank battles: Kidney Ridge, Miteirya Ridge
- Axis tank strength eroding: 489 tanks → 102 operational (November 1)

### November 2-4: Operation Supercharge
- 2nd New Zealand Division, 9th Armoured Brigade attack
- Breakthrough achieved Tel el Aqqaqir
- Rommel requests permission to withdraw (Hitler: "Victory or death")
- **November 4**: Rommel retreats despite Hitler's order

### November 4-11: Breakout and Pursuit
- British armor breaks through
- Axis retreat accelerates
- Italian infantry divisions (no transport) abandoned, surrender
- **Rain November 6-7**: Delays British pursuit
- Axis escapes, but loses 30,000 Italian infantry POWs

## Operation Torch Landings (November 8)

### November 8: Allied Landings Morocco/Algeria
- **Western Task Force** (US): Casablanca, Morocco (35,000 troops)
- **Center Task Force** (US): Oran, Algeria (39,000 troops)
- **Eastern Task Force** (US/British): Algiers, Algeria (33,000 troops)
- **Total**: 107,000 Allied troops land first wave

### November 8-11: Vichy French Resistance
- French Morocco/Algeria garrisons resist (confused loyalties)
- Casablanca: 3 days fighting before armistice
- Oran: 2 days fighting
- Algiers: Minimal resistance (coup by Free French officers)
- **November 11**: Cease-fire, French North Africa joins Allies

### November 9-December 31: Race for Tunisia
- German airlift: 5. Panzerarmee forming Tunisia
- British First Army advances from Algiers
- **November 25**: British reach 20 miles from Tunis, halted
- December: Winter rains, stalemated front

## Axis Retreat Across Libya

### November 11 - December 31: 1,400-Mile Retreat
- Rommel retreats from El Alamein to El Agheila (November 11-23)
- Montgomery cautious pursuit (fears Rommel counterattack)
- **November 15**: British recapture Tobruk (third time)
- **November 20**: Benghazi liberated (third time)
- December 12: Montgomery attacks El Agheila position
- **December 23**: Rommel withdraws to Buerat (continues retreat)
- December 31: Panzerarmee at Buerat, 600 miles west of Alamein

## Forces Engaged

**Second Alamein - British Eighth Army** (Montgomery):
- XXX Corps: 9th Australian, 51st Highland, 2nd New Zealand, 1st/4th South African Divisions
- XIII Corps: 7th Armoured, 44th, 50th Infantry Divisions
- X Corps: 1st, 10th Armoured Divisions
- **Tanks**: 1,029 (252 Shermans, 170 Grants, 216 Crusaders, 391 Stuarts/Valentines)
- **Artillery**: 908 field guns, 1,451 AT guns

**Second Alamein - Panzerarmee Afrika** (Rommel, then Stumme, then Ritter von Thoma):
- DAK: 15. Panzer, 21. Panzer, 90. leichte Divisions
- Italian XX Motorized Corps: Ariete, Littorio, Trieste Divisions
- Italian X/XXI Infantry Corps: Brescia, Folgore, Pavia, Bologna, Trento Divisions
- **Tanks**: 489 (238 German, 251 Italian)
- **Artillery**: 552 guns

**Operation Torch Forces**:
- US II Corps, British First Army, Free French forces
- Air/naval support: Mediterranean Fleet, Gibraltar Force
- **Total**: 107,000 first wave, 250,000 by December

## Equipment Balance

**British Superiority at Alamein**:
- 2:1 tank superiority (1,029 vs 489)
- Sherman tanks dominant (75mm gun, 75mm frontal armor)
- Air superiority: 1,500 aircraft vs 350 Axis
- Artillery 2:1 advantage

**Axis Shortages**:
- Fuel critical (Italian Navy lost tankers to RAF/submarines)
- Ammunition rationed
- No tank replacements available
- German Panzer IV Ausf F2 (75mm L/43 gun) only 30 present

## Second Alamein Casualties

**British Eighth Army**:
- 13,560 casualties (killed, wounded, missing)
- 332 tanks destroyed (mostly repairable)
- 111 guns lost

**Panzerarmee Afrika**:
- 30,542 casualties (mostly Italian POWs - infantry divisions without transport)
- 450 tanks destroyed/captured
- 1,000 guns lost
- **German**: 1,149 killed, 3,886 wounded, 7,900 POW
- **Italian**: 1,200 killed, 1,600 wounded, 20,000 POW

## Tactical Analysis

**Montgomery's Method**:
- Attritional battle (wear down Axis tanks)
- Concentrated artillery (1,000-gun barrage)
- Methodical minefield clearance
- No hasty pursuit (avoid Rommel counterattack)

**Rommel's Dilemma**:
- Fixed defense (fuel shortages prevented mobile operations)
- Minefields absorbed Allied artillery
- Tank attrition unsustainable (no replacements)
- Hitler's "stand fast" order delayed retreat, cost Italian infantry

## Strategic Impact - Two-Front Threat

**Alamein Victory**:
- Axis expelled from Egypt permanently
- Libya recaptured (third time, permanently)
- 1,400-mile advance in 10 weeks
- British morale restored, "Desert Fox" defeated

**Operation Torch**:
- Second front created in French North Africa
- Vichy French forces neutralized, joined Allies
- Race for Tunisia: Germans won short-term, Allies would win long-term
- Strategic pincers: Eighth Army from east, First Army from west

**Axis Strategic Collapse**:
- Reinforcing Tunisia while losing Libya (divided forces)
- Two armies (Rommel's + 5. Panzerarmee) competing for scarce supplies
- Hitler's decision to hold Tunisia doomed 250,000 Axis troops

## Historical Significance

Second El Alamein was decisive British victory: First major defeat of German army in WWII. Churchill: "Before Alamein we never had a victory, after Alamein we never had a defeat."

Operation Torch opened second Mediterranean front, forced Hitler to reinforce Tunisia, led to Axis surrender May 1943 (250,000 POWs).

**Combined impact**: North African campaign outcome decided. Axis defeat inevitable. Sicily invasion next (July 1943).
