### Primary Subordinate Units

**1. Schützen-Regiment 155**
- Type: Motorized Rifle Regiment
- Strength: 2,800 personnel
- Composition: 3 battalions (I, II, III) formed from III./Inf.Rgt. 241, III./Inf.Rgt. 255, and III./Inf.Rgt. 347
- Note: Regiment officially formed September 4, 1941 from three separate infantry battalions already in North Africa. Motorized in name but weak transport capability.

**2. Infanterie-Regiment 361 (Afrika-Regiment)**
- Type: Infantry Regiment
- Strength: 2,400 personnel
- Commander: Oberstleutnant Hans-Lewin von Barby
- Composition: 2 battalions (I, II)
- Note: Unique unit: composed largely of former French Foreign Legion volunteers (anti-communist Germans/Austrians who served in Légion Étrangère). Formed June 15, 1941 in Greece, arrived North Africa late summer 1941. Initially designated 'Sonderverband (Special Unit) 288', renamed Infanterie-Regiment 361 in August. Tough, experienced troops but poorly equipped initially.

**3. III./Infanterie-Regiment 255**
- Type: Infantry Battalion
- Strength: 850 personnel
- Composition: 1 battalion (3 rifle companies, 1 heavy weapons company)
- Note: Attached battalion from 255th Infantry Regiment. Later incorporated into Schützen-Regiment 155 formation.

**4. III./Infanterie-Regiment 347**
- Type: Infantry Battalion
- Strength: 850 personnel
- Composition: 1 battalion (3 rifle companies, 1 heavy weapons company)
- Note: Attached battalion from 347th Infantry Regiment. Later incorporated into Schützen-Regiment 155 formation.

**5. gemischte Aufklärungs-Kompanie 580**

**6. II./Artillerie-Regiment 155**
- Type: Motorized Artillery Battalion

## 11. Tactical Doctrine and Capabilities

### Doctrinal Role

Light infantry division formed for static siege operations around Tobruk and limited mobile operations in desert. Hastily assembled from disparate units already in North Africa (August-November 1941). Designed to free up panzer divisions (15. and 21. Panzer) for mobile operations by manning defensive sectors and containing British Tobruk garrison. Lighter than standard infantry division but heavier than security units.

### Special Capabilities

- Desert operations experience (units acclimated to North Africa before division formation)
- Static defense and siege warfare (Tobruk perimeter)
- Former French Foreign Legion personnel in Inf.Rgt. 361 (combat experienced)
- Flexible improvised organization suitable for fluid desert warfare
- Integration of captured British equipment and weapons

### Tactical Innovations

- Heavy reliance on captured British vehicles/weapons out of necessity
- Adaptation to severe supply constraints through improvisation
- Mixed-unit composition enabling rapid deployment from available forces
- Use of former Foreign Legion troops familiar with colonial/desert warfare

### Known Issues and Limitations

- Weak motorization (1,200 trucks vs 1,500+ for panzer divisions) limits mobility
- Hastily assembled from disparate units - cohesion concerns
- Afrika-Regiment 361 initially arrived without heavy weapons
- Limited organic artillery (only 2 battalions, 24 guns vs 3+ battalions for standard divisions)
- No tank component - pure light infantry division, vulnerable to enemy armor
- Extended supply lines (950km from Tripoli) strain logistics
- Dependent on captured British equipment for transportation and supply
- Division still forming throughout Q3 1941 - not fully operational until November

### Desert Adaptations

Division formed specifically for North African operations. Units adapted to extreme heat (45°C summer), water scarcity (10L/man/day minimum), desert terrain. Tropical uniforms, pith helmets/peaked caps, desert boots issued. Vehicles equipped with sand filters, desert tires. Navigation by sun compass and dead reckoning. Higher vehicle breakdown rates necessitate extensive workshop support. Camouflage nets for concealment. Reliance on captured British water, fuel, and ammunition stocks around Tobruk.
