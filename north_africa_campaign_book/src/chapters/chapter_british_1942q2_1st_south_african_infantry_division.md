## 11. Tactical Doctrine and Capabilities

### Doctrinal Role

Commonwealth infantry division organized on British model. Primary role Q2 1942: defensive operations along coastal sector of Gazala Line (April-May), then fighting withdrawal during Battle of Gazala (26 May - 14 June). First quarter with full 3-brigade strength (3rd Brigade transferred from 2nd SA Division on 21 March). Successfully defended positions against Italian Sabratha Division breakthrough attempt. Conducted orderly withdrawal along coastal road to Mersa Matruh without catastrophic losses (unlike 2nd SA Division at Tobruk).

### Special Capabilities

- Experienced desert warfare division (East African Campaign, Operation Crusader)
- Strong integration with British 8th Army XIII Corps command structure
- Locally-produced South African Marmon-Herrington armored cars adapted for desert reconnaissance
- Comprehensive support services with integrated non-European personnel (5,000 of 17,000)
- First deployment with full 3-brigade strength provided increased tactical flexibility
- Strong artillery concentration (96x 25-pdrs plus attached medium artillery) effective in defensive operations

### Tactical Innovations

- Use of South African-built Marmon-Herrington armored cars (Mk II and Mk III variants)
- Combined-arms coordination developed through East African and North African operations
- Defensive box system along Gazala Line with interlocking fields of fire
- Concentrated artillery fire support doctrine (later demonstrated at First El Alamein July 1942)
- Integrated non-European personnel in support roles maximizing combat strength

### Known Issues and Limitations

- Anti-tank capability severely limited - 2-pounders inadequate against German Panzer III/IV
- Limited 6-pdr guns issued by June 1942 - most batteries still had 2-pdrs
- Infantry divisions lacked organic armor for mobile operations
- Defensive positions on Gazala Line vulnerable to outflanking (demonstrated by Rommel's 'left hook' May 26)
- Coastal sector positions exposed to naval bombardment risk
- Communication challenges coordinating with attached British units (67th/68th Medium Regiments RA)
- Rising summer temperatures (30-35°C) reduced operational effectiveness during June withdrawal

### Desert Adaptations

Division fully adapted to Western Desert by Q2 1942 with extensive combat experience. Vehicles equipped with sand filters, extra water capacity, and improved cooling systems. Personnel trained in desert navigation, water discipline, defensive position construction, and withdrawal procedures. Marmon-Herrington armored cars specifically designed for African conditions. Support services incorporated extensive non-European personnel experienced in desert logistics.
