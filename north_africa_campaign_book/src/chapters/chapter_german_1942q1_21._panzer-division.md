### Primary Subordinate Units

**1. Panzer-Regiment 5**
- Type: Panzer Regiment
- Strength: 1,480 personnel
- Commander: Unknown (Oberst rank)
- Composition: 2 Panzer Abteilungen (I. and II.), each with staff company, 2 medium companies (Pz III), 1 light company (Pz II). Maintenance and reserve detachments.
- Note: From 3. Panzer-Division. Deployed to Africa Feb 1941 with 5. leichte Division. Redesignated to 21. Panzer 1 Aug 1941. Tank strength devastated in Operation Crusader (Nov-Dec 1941): 165 → 79 tanks (52% loss). 5 Jan 1942 convoy brought ~25 tanks to division (DAK total 55 tanks). Primary medium tank now Pz.III Ausf J with superior long 50mm gun vs British Crusader/Valentine. Light tanks (Pz.II, Pz.I) reconnaissance role only.

**2. Schützen-Regiment 104**
- Type: Motorized Infantry Regiment (Panzergrenadier from 5 July 1942)
- Strength: 2,380 personnel
- Composition: I. and II. Bataillone (motorized infantry), each with 4 rifle companies, 1 heavy weapons company (HMGs, mortars, AT rifles). Support: 13. Heavy Infantry Gun Company (sIG 150mm), 14. Pioneer Company. Regiment transferred from 15. Panzer-Division to 21. Panzer on 1 Sept 1941.
- Note: Transferred to 21. Panzer-Division from 15. Panzer on 1 Sept 1941 (when 5. leichte redesignated 21. Panzer). Filled role of Schützen-Regiment 200 (which had unique MG battalion structure). Standard motorized infantry regiment with 2 battalions. Renamed Panzergrenadier-Regiment 104 on 5 July 1942. Reduced from Q2 1941 strength due to Crusader losses. MG-Btl. 8 attached until 1 April 1942 when it replaced I./Schützen-Regiment 104 (aufgerieben/worn out).

**3. Schützen-Regiment 155**
- Type: Motorized Infantry Regiment (became Panzergrenadier 5 July 1942)
- Strength: 2,250 personnel
- Composition: I. and II. Bataillone (motorized infantry), each with 4 rifle companies, 1 heavy weapons company. Support: 13. Heavy Infantry Gun Company (sIG 150mm), 14. Pioneer Company. Originally formed from elements of Schützen-Regiment 200 (formerly MG battalions).
- Note: Designation changed from Schützen-Regiment 200 (which was formed from 2nd and 8th MG Battalions with unique structure). By Q1 1942 reorganized into standard two-battalion motorized infantry regiment. Renamed Panzergrenadier-Regiment 155 on 5 July 1942. Reduced from Q2 1941 (5. leichte) strength due to Crusader attrition. Provided second infantry regiment giving division standard panzer division structure.

**4. Aufklärungs-Abteilung 3**
- Type: Reconnaissance Battalion
- Strength: 485 personnel
- Composition: 1 light armored car company (SdKfz 222, 231, 221, 223), 1 motorcycle company, 1 support company (engineer platoon, AT platoon with 3.7cm PaK, infantry gun section with leIG 75mm), supply column
- Note: From 3. Panzer-Division. Deployed to Africa Feb 1941 with 5. leichte Division. Redesignated to 21. Panzer 1 Aug 1941. Critical for desert reconnaissance and screening. Armored car strength reduced from 52 in Q2 1941 to 43 in Q1 1942 (Crusader losses). One zug used Volkswagen Kübelwagens for liaison. Renamed Panzer-Aufklärungs-Abteilung 580 (later 21) in 1943.

**5. Artillerie-Regiment 155**
- Type: Motorized Artillery Regiment
- Strength: 895 personnel
- Composition: Regiment HQ with signals and calibration detachments, 3 motorized battalions: I. Abteilung (light, 10.5cm), II. Abteilung (light, 10.5cm), III. Abteilung (heavy, 15cm)
- Note: Formed for 21. Panzer-Division when redesignated from 5. leichte (1 Aug 1941). Replaced single battalion (I./Artillerie-Regiment 75, 12 guns) with full three-battalion regiment (36 guns). Significantly strengthened artillery vs 5. leichte Q2 1941. Still lighter than 15. Panzer (3 battalions but 44 guns including 8.8cm Flak). Provided standard panzer division artillery structure.

**6. Panzerjäger-Abteilung 39**
- Type: Motorized Anti-Tank Battalion
- Strength: 340 personnel
- Composition: Battalion HQ, 3 motorized panzerjäger companies with towed AT guns
- Note: From 3. Panzer-Division. Deployed to Africa Feb 1941. Towed AT guns (not self-propelled). 3.7cm 'door-knocker' increasingly inadequate vs British Matilda II (78mm frontal armor). 5cm PaK 38 increased from 6 (Q2 1941) to 16 (Q1 1942) - effective vs Crusader/Valentine. Renamed Panzerjäger-Abteilung 190 in late 1942.

**7. Panzerjäger-Abteilung 605 (gepanzert)**
- Type: Armored (Self-Propelled) Anti-Tank Battalion
- Strength: 185 personnel
- Composition: Armored HQ section, signals detachment, 2 self-propelled AT companies (reduced from 3 in Q2 1941)
- Note: Self-propelled battalion with Czech 47mm AT guns on Panzerjäger I chassis (Q2 1941: 9 guns). Devastated in Operation Crusader. By Q1 1942 reduced to cadre strength with most equipment lost. Battalion effectively absorbed into other units by Feb 1942. Not included in division artillery total due to minimal operational strength.

**8. Flak-Bataillon 606 (Selbstfahrlafette)**
- Type: Self-Propelled Flak Battalion
- Strength: 220 personnel
- Composition: Self-propelled HQ section, 2 self-propelled flak companies (reduced from 3 in Q2 1941)
- Note: Mobile AA defense for advancing formations. Critical against RAF fighter-bomber attacks. Reduced from 3 companies (36 guns) in Q2 1941 to 2 companies (22 guns) in Q1 1942 due to Crusader losses. Essential for mobile operations providing AA umbrella for panzer columns.

**9. I./Flak-Regiment 33**
- Type: Flak Battalion (Heavy/Light AA)
- Strength: 385 personnel
- Composition: Battalion HQ with signals, 2× 88mm batteries (4 guns each = 8 total, reduced from 12 in Q2 1941), 2× 20mm batteries (4 guns each = 8 total), 1× 37mm battery (13 guns), light supply column
- Note: From 3. Panzer-Division. 88mm guns proved devastatingly effective in anti-tank role vs British cruiser tanks (Crusader, Valentine). Rommel's preferred anti-tank weapon. Reduced from 3× 88mm batteries (12 guns) in Q2 1941 to 2 batteries (8 guns) in Q1 1942 (Crusader losses). Added 37mm battery for medium AA coverage. Essential for anti-tank defense when 3.7cm PaK inadequate.

**10. Panzer-Pionier-Bataillon 200**
- Type: Engineer Battalion (Motorized)
- Strength: 430 personnel
- Composition: 3 motorized pioneer companies (consolidated from companies attached to MG battalions Q2 1941), battalion HQ with engineering equipment depot
- Note: z.b.V. (zur besonderen Verwendung) = for special employment. Formed 1 Aug 1941 when 5. leichte redesignated 21. Panzer. Mine warfare and fortification work critical for North Africa. Reduced from 485 personnel in Q2 1941 (as 200th z.b.V. Pionier-Bataillon) to 430 in Q1 1942 (Crusader losses). Desert engineering: mine laying/clearing, water point development, demolitions.

**11. Panzer-Nachrichten-Abteilung 200**
- Type: Signals Battalion
- Strength: 310 personnel
- Composition: Signals company, radio company, telephone company, mapping platoon, printing detachment
- Note: Communications critical for mobile desert operations and coordination with DAK HQ. Reduced from 345 personnel in Q2 1941 (as Nachrichten-Abteilung 39) to 310 in Q1 1942. Redesignated Panzer-Nachrichten-Abteilung 200 on 1 Aug 1941 (when 5. leichte became 21. Panzer). Radio communications essential for coordinating fast-moving operations in Jan 1942 counter-offensive.

**12. Divisions-Nachschubführer 200**
- Type: Divisional Supply Troops
- Strength: 1,725 personnel
- Composition: Multiple supply battalions (619th, 533rd reduced capacity), light/heavy supply columns (reduced from Q2 1941), water columns (641st Heavy, 645th - critical), maintenance (workshops reduced), medical (631st/633rd ambulance cos reduced, 4/572nd field hospital, 1/83rd medical co, 877th medical supply), administration (683rd/681st admin battalions reduced, 341st admin section), field services (735th field post, 309th MP, 503rd butcher, 531st bakery)
- Note: Extensive logistical tail essential for desert operations 850-1000km from Tripoli. Water and fuel supply paramount. Medical facilities for casualties and desert illnesses. Significantly reduced from Q2 1941 (2140 personnel) to Q1 1942 (1725) due to Crusader losses and vehicle attrition. Workshop strength increased (priority for post-Crusader tank repairs). Fuel/water tankers reduced (critical shortage). Renamed Kdr. der Pz.Div.Nachschubtruppen 200 on 1 Dec 1942.

## 11. Tactical Doctrine and Capabilities

### Doctrinal Role

Panzer division for mobile operations in North Africa. Full panzer division structure (vs 'leichte' Q2 1941) with two infantry regiments, three-battalion artillery regiment, standard reconnaissance/AT/engineer/signals battalions. Heavier and more capable than 5. leichte Division but still lighter than European panzer divisions. Primary roles: mobile offense (Jan 1942 counter-offensive), exploitation, mobile defense. Rebuilt after Operation Crusader (Nov-Dec 1941) devastation.

### Special Capabilities

- Mobile desert warfare with combined arms coordination
- Rapid exploitation and pursuit (Jan 1942 counter-offensive: Mersa el Brega → Benghazi in 9 days)
- 88mm FlaK dual-role (AA and devastating AT) - effective vs all British armor
- Self-propelled AA battalion for mobile air defense
- Extensive reconnaissance capability (Aufklärungs-Abteilung 3 with armored cars, motorcycles)
- Mobile workshops for battlefield repairs (critical post-Crusader)
- Superior tank quality: Pz.III Ausf J long 50mm outranges British Crusader/Valentine

### Tactical Innovations

- 8.8cm FlaK in mobile anti-tank role (devastating vs British armor)
- Rapid desert exploitation using sun compass/dead reckoning navigation
- Capture and immediate use of British supply dumps (essential when own logistics inadequate)
- Mobile AT gun screens (5cm PaK 38, 8.8cm FlaK) to lure British armor into kill zones
- Concentration of armor at decisive points (Schwerpunkt doctrine - Agedabia, Benghazi)
- Mobile workshops following panzer columns for immediate battlefield repairs

### Known Issues and Limitations

- Smaller tank force (79 vs 86 for 15. Panzer, vs 165 in 5. leichte Q2 1941) - 52% reduction post-Crusader
- Extended supply lines (850-1000+ km from Tripoli) via single coastal road
- Fuel crisis (5 days reserves - lowest of war) limits operational tempo
- British naval/air interdiction destroys 30-40% of Axis supply convoys (Malta-based RAF/RN)
- 3.7cm PaK 35/36 inadequate against British Matilda II infantry tanks (78mm frontal armor)
- Water scarcity (11L/man/day barely adequate) limits operations and soldier endurance
- Desert conditions: heat, sand (vehicle breakdowns), navigation challenges, supply constraints
- Limited spare parts (vehicles/tanks cannibalized)
- Vulnerability of soft-skinned vehicles to RAF air attacks
- Post-Crusader attrition: personnel 12,000 (Q2 1941 as 5. leichte) → 11,565 (Q1 1942); tanks 165 → 79 (52% loss)

### Desert Adaptations

Tropical uniforms (khaki), pith helmets, desert boots issued. Water discipline critical: 11L/man/day minimum (vs 10L in Q2 1941 - increased for health). Vehicles equipped with sand filters (Feifel air cleaners), desert tires, extra radiator capacity. Navigation by sun compass, dead reckoning, celestial navigation. Camouflage nets for concealment. Goggles and face scarves for sandstorms. Spare parts stockpiled for high breakdown rates. Mobile workshops follow panzer columns. Capture of British supply dumps essential for sustained operations when own logistics inadequate.
