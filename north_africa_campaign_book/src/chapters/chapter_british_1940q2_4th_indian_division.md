# 4th Indian Infantry Division

**Nation:** British (Indian Army)
**Quarter:** 1940-Q2 (April-June 1940)
**Commander:** Major-General Philip Neame
**Type:** Infantry Division (Mixed British-Indian)
**Parent Formation:** Western Desert Force
**HQ:** Fayed, Egypt (Ismailia Governorate)

## 1. Overview

The 4th Indian Infantry Division achieved the historic distinction of being the first Indian division deployed overseas in World War II, arriving in Egypt during 1940-Q2 for desert warfare training at Fayed. With 15,000 personnel comprising 40% British and 60% Indian troops organized across three infantry brigades, this mixed-composition force represented the Indian Army commitment to the Allied cause in North Africa.

Under Major-General Philip Neame command (February-August 1940), the division underwent intensive desert training during April-June 1940, preparing for what would become Operation Compass (December 1940). The division exemplified the Indian Army brigade structure with each of its three brigades (5th, 7th, 11th Indian Infantry) combining British and Indian battalions.

**Key Features:**
- First Indian division overseas in WWII (historic milestone)
- Mixed composition: 40% British (6,000), 60% Indian (9,000) personnel
- Three infantry brigades with combined British-Indian battalions
- 72x 25-pounder field guns (three field regiments)
- 48x 2-pounder AT guns (inadequate for future German armor)
- Well-supplied from Egyptian base infrastructure

## 3. Organization

**5th Indian Infantry Brigade** (Brigadier Theophilus J. Ponting)
- 1st Royal Fusiliers, 3/1st Punjab Regiment, 4/6th Rajputana Rifles

**7th Indian Infantry Brigade**
- Renumbered from 4th Brigade in June 1940

**11th Indian Infantry Brigade**
- 1/6th Rajputana Rifles, 2nd Cameron Highlanders, 4/7th Rajput Regiment

**Divisional Artillery** (Brigadier P. Maxwell, from June 1940)
- Three field regiments (72x 25-pdr), one AT regiment (48x 2-pdr)

## 4. Operations: Desert Training (April-June 1940)

**Training at Fayed, Egypt:**
- Desert navigation and water discipline
- Heat management (18-35C temperatures)
- Combined arms coordination

**Supply Status:**
- Fuel reserves: 7 days
- Ammunition: 14 days
- Water: 10L/man/day (150,000L daily)
- Operational radius: 100 km

## 5. Historical Significance

**First Indian Division Overseas:** Historic milestone for Indian Army in WWII. Prepared for Operation Compass (December 1940) where division would destroy Italian 10th Army.

**Mixed British-Indian Model:** Brigade organization became standard for Indian divisions throughout WWII.
