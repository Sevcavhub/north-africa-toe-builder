### 69th Infantry Brigade
- **Commander**: Brigadier Unknown
- **Strength**: 4,286 personnel
- **Composition**: Held northern sector of 50th Division's Gazala Line positions. Survived Battle of Gazala intact and participated in withdrawal. Later fought in First El Alamein (July) and Tunisia Campaign.

### 150th Infantry Brigade
- **Commander**: Brigadier C.W. Haydon
- **Strength**: 4,500 personnel
- **Composition**: DESTROYED 1 JUNE 1942. Moved to southern defensive box in late April with 20-mile perimeter, 6 miles from 69th Brigade north, 10 miles from Free French at Bir Hakeim south. Attacked by Rommel 29 May from rear using 15th Panzer, Trieste Motorised, and 90th Light Divisions plus heavy Luftwaffe bombing. Held 72 hours without resupply. Overrun noon 1 June 1942. All three infantry battalions, attached artillery regiment, engineers, and support units captured. Brigadier Haydon killed in action 1 June. Brigade never reformed.

## Supply & Logistics

**Supply Status**: Adequate for defensive operations along Gazala Line during April-May. Division held 25-mile sector with prepared defensive positions and static supply depots. 150th Brigade isolated in southern box 20-mile perimeter, 6 miles from 69th Brigade, 10 miles from Free French at Bir Hakeim. Rommel's offensive (26 May) severed supply lines to 150th Brigade box, which held 72 hours (29-31 May) without resupply before surrender 1 June. Loss of 150th Brigade, 90th Field Regiment RA, and attached units reduced division strength by ~4,500 men. Remainder of division (69th and 151st Brigades) maintained supply lines during withdrawal to Mersa Matruh late June.
