# 21. Panzer-Division
## German Panzer Division, 1941-Q3 (July-September 1941)

---

## 1. Overview

The 21. Panzer-Division was formally redesignated on **August 1, 1941** from the 5. leichte Division, marking the maturation of German armored forces in North Africa. Under **Generalmajor Johann von Ravenstein**, the division received substantial reinforcements from 15. Panzer-Division including Schützen-Regiment 104, Kradschützen-Bataillon 15, and MG-Bataillon 8. With 110 tanks and approximately 12,500 personnel, the division operated under Deutsches Afrikakorps during the extended Siege of Tobruk.

The redesignation represented organizational maturation: from "light division" (leichte Division) to full panzer division status, aligning with standard German armored organization. The division absorbed additional infantry and support units, transforming into a more balanced combined-arms formation capable of sustained operations in desert warfare.

**Key Statistics (1941-Q3):**
- Personnel: 12,500
- Tanks: 110 total
- Commander: Generalmajor Johann von Ravenstein
- Operations: Siege of Tobruk continuation, defensive operations
- Strategic Result: Reinforcement and reorganization for Operation Crusader

---

## 2. Redesignation from 5. leichte Division

The transformation from 5. leichte Division to 21. Panzer-Division (August 1, 1941) involved significant organizational changes:

**Units Retained from 5. leichte:**
- Panzer-Regiment 5 (received additional II. Abteilung from Panzer-Abteilung 190)
- Schützen-Regiment 200
- I./Artillerie-Regiment 75
- Aufklärungs-Abteilung (mot.) 3
- Panzerjäger-Abteilung 39
- Pionier-Bataillon
- Panzer-Divisions-Nachrichten-Abteilung 200

**Units Added from 15. Panzer-Division:**
- Schützen-Regiment 104 (I. and II. Battalions)
- Kradschützen-Bataillon 15 (motorcycle infantry)
- MG-Bataillon (mot.) 8 (machine gun battalion from Züllichau)

This reorganization doubled infantry strength and provided additional tank battalion, transforming the division into full-strength panzer division matching Eastern Front standards.

---

## 3. Command: von Ravenstein

**Generalmajor Johann von Ravenstein** assumed command with redesignation (August 1, 1941). Experienced panzer commander, previously led 6. Panzer-Division (September 1940 - May 1941) in France and preparations for Eastern Front. Brought mechanized warfare expertise to Africa theater.

Von Ravenstein commanded through remainder of 1941 until captured by New Zealand troops near Tobruk on **November 29, 1941** during Operation Crusader—first German general to become Allied POW in WWII. His capture during Crusader's climactic battles left division temporarily leaderless during critical period.

---

## 4. Siege of Tobruk Operations

The division continued operations against Tobruk's British garrison throughout Q3 1941. Following failed April assaults, German forces maintained siege while preparing for renewed offensive operations. The 21. Panzer-Division held defensive positions south and southeast of Tobruk perimeter, conducting patrol operations and responding to British breakout attempts.

Tobruk's garrison (Australian 9th Division, British armor, Polish Brigade) conducted aggressive patrolling and periodic raids. The 21. Panzer-Division's role combined containing Tobruk garrison while maintaining mobile reserve for potential British relief operations from Egyptian frontier.

---

## 5. Equipment and Tank Strength

With 110 tanks, the division operated mixed Panzer III and IV variants. Exact distribution unavailable but estimated 70-75 Panzer III (3.7cm and 5cm guns) and 30-35 Panzer IV (7.5cm short). Tank strength adequate for defensive operations but insufficient for renewed Tobruk assault without 15. Panzer-Division support.

Standard German infantry weapons: 8,400+ Karabiner 98k rifles, 420+ MG34 machine guns, 310+ MP40 submachine guns. Artillery support from Artillerie-Regiment 75 provided divisional fire support.

---

## 6. Historical Significance

Q3 1941 marked transition period: redesignation formalized division status while stalemate at Tobruk continued. The reinforcements positioned division for Operation Crusader (November 1941), where von Ravenstein's capture and subsequent German defeat forced Axis withdrawal from Cyrenaica.

The division's August reorganization demonstrated German commitment to North Africa despite Operation Barbarossa (USSR invasion, June 1941) consuming primary resources. Rommel's forces received sufficient reinforcement to maintain offensive capability, setting stage for November's decisive battles.
