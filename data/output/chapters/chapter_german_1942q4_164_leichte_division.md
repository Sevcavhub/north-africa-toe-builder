# 164. leichte Afrika-Division

**Nation:** German
**Quarter:** 1942-Q4 (October-December 1942)
**Commander:** Generalmajor Carl-Hans Lungershausen
**Type:** Light Motorized Division
**Parent Formation:** XXI. (ital.) Korps / Deutsch-italienische Panzerarmee
**HQ:** El Alamein sector, Egypt (Oct-Nov), retreat to Tunisia (Nov-Dec)

## 1. Overview

The 164. leichte Afrika-Division served as one of the German Army's specialized light motorized divisions during the climactic battles of 1942-Q4: Second Battle of El Alamein (October 23 - November 11, 1942) and the subsequent 2,000-kilometer retreat to Tunisia. With 8,400 personnel and 1,424 vehicles (no organic tanks), the division exemplified German light division doctrine—highly mobile motorized infantry with strong anti-tank and anti-aircraft capabilities but dependent on attached panzer units for armored support.

Formed in 1942 from elements of Infanterie-Regiment 382, Brigade Marcks, and Festungs-Stamm units, 164. leichte represented Germany's attempt to create mobile divisions suitable for desert warfare without the full mechanization and tank strength of panzer divisions. Under Generalmajor Lungershausen's command, the division fought in the critical northern sector at El Alamein before conducting a masterful fighting withdrawal across Libya.

**Key Features:**
- Light motorized division: 1,424 vehicles (100% motorized infantry)
- No organic tanks: relied on attached DAK panzer support
- Strong AT: 24 x 50mm, 12 x 75mm AT guns plus towed 88mm Flak
- El Alamein northern sector: defensive operations Oct 23 - Nov 11
- 2,000km retreat: El Alamein to Tunisia (Nov-Dec 1942)

**Critical Period:** Second El Alamein marked turning point—division fought desperately to prevent British breakthrough, then conducted skillful retreat under constant Commonwealth air attack and armored pursuit.

## 3. Operations: El Alamein and Retreat

**Second Battle of El Alamein (Oct 23 - Nov 11, 1942):**
- Northern sector defense alongside 15. Panzer-Division
- Absorbed massive Commonwealth artillery bombardments (1,000+ guns)
- Counterattacked British penetrations (Operation Lightfoot, Supercharge)
- Suffered 40% casualties by November 4
- Ordered retreat November 4 after Rommel's "Victory or Death" order countermanded

**The Great Retreat (Nov 4 - Dec 31, 1942):**
- 2,000km fighting withdrawal: El Alamein → Mersa Matruh → Tobruk → Benghazi → El Agheila → Tunisia
- Rearguard actions against 7th Armoured, 2nd NZ, 4th Indian Divisions
- Constant RAF attacks on convoy columns
- Fuel shortages forced vehicle abandonments
- Arrived Tunisia December 1942 with ~5,000 effectives (40% casualties)

**Key Engagements:**
- Kidney Ridge defense (Oct 26-28): blocked 1st Armoured Division breakthrough attempt
- Tel el Aqqaqir (Nov 2): desperate counterattack vs. 9th Armoured Brigade
- Mersa Matruh rearguard (Nov 7-8): covered Afrika Korps withdrawal
- El Agheila stand (Dec 12-17): brief defensive stand before final retreat to Tunisia

## 4. Historical Significance

**Light Division Doctrine:** 164. leichte demonstrated strengths and weaknesses of German light divisions:
- **Strengths:** High mobility (100% motorized), strong AT defense (50mm/75mm/88mm guns), flexible combined-arms tactics
- **Weaknesses:** No organic tanks (vulnerable without panzer support), lighter artillery than standard infantry divisions, vulnerable to Commonwealth air superiority

**El Alamein Turning Point:** Division's 40% casualties (3,360 of 8,400) reflected ferocity of Commonwealth offensive. British numerical/material superiority (195,000 vs. 104,000 troops, 1,029 vs. 547 tanks) overwhelmed Axis defenses despite German tactical skill.

**Retreat Mastery:** Lungershausen's skillful rearguard actions prevented encirclement, preserving division for Tunisia campaign. German retreat discipline contrasted with Italian collapse (many divisions captured/destroyed).

**Tunisia Defensive:** Division's arrival in Tunisia (December 1942) reinforced Axis bridgehead, extending North Africa campaign until May 1943 surrender.
