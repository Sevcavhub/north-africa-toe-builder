# 21. Panzer-Division
## German Panzer Division, 1942-Q1 (January-March 1942)

---

## 1. Overview

The first quarter of 1942 witnessed 21. Panzer-Division's remarkable recovery and counteroffensive under new commander **Generalmajor Georg von Bismarck**. Rebuilding from Operation Crusader's defeat, the division received tank reinforcements (including new Panzer III Ausf J with long 5cm L/60 guns) and launched Rommel's January 21 counteroffensive. With 79 tanks (67 operational) and 11,565 personnel, the division spearheaded reconquest of Cyrenaica, recapturing Benghazi (January 29) and advancing to Gazala Line by March.

Von Bismarck, grandson of Otto von Bismarck and experienced panzer commander, restored divisional effectiveness following von Ravenstein's capture. His leadership combined aggressive tactics with logistical prudence, enabling sustained offensive operations despite extended supply lines from Tripoli.

**Key Statistics (1942-Q1):**
- Personnel: 11,565 (reduced from Crusader losses)
- Tanks: 79 total (67 operational, 85% readiness)
- Commander: Generalmajor Georg von Bismarck
- Operations: Counteroffensive (Jan 21), Benghazi recapture, Gazala Line
- Strategic Result: Reconquest of Cyrenaica

---

## 2. Von Bismarck's Command

**Generalmajor Georg von Bismarck** (1891-1942) assumed command January 1, 1942. Distinguished lineage (grandson of Iron Chancellor) combined with proven combat record: commanded Panzer-Regiment 7 (10. Panzer-Division) before Africa assignment. Brought Eastern Front mechanized warfare experience to desert theater.

Von Bismarck commanded through 1942's critical battles: Gazala (May-June), First Alamein (July), Alam Halfa (August-September). Killed in action August 31, 1942 when command vehicle struck British anti-tank fire near Alam Halfa Ridge—one of highest-ranking German casualties in North Africa.

His Q1 1942 leadership rebuilt division from Crusader's wreckage, restored morale through January's swift victories, and positioned division for summer's decisive battles.

---

## 3. January Counteroffensive

British Eighth Army, extended after Crusader pursuit, held positions from Agedabia to Benghazi with depleted formations. Rommel, receiving tank reinforcements via Tripoli, launched surprise counteroffensive January 21.

**January 21-28:** The 21. Panzer-Division (alongside 15. Panzer-Division and Italian units) struck British positions at Mersa el Brega, achieving complete surprise. British forces, expecting defensive stalemate, retreated precipitously. German armor advanced 300+ miles in one week.

**January 29:** Benghazi recaptured—major port and supply hub. British withdrew to Gazala Line (west of Tobruk), abandoning entire Cyrenaica province within days.

**February-March:** German forces consolidated at Gazala Line, facing British defensive positions anchored on coast (Gazala) and extending 50 miles south to Bir Hacheim. Stalemate resumed as both sides rebuilt for summer offensive.

---

## 4. Tank Strength Evolution

Starting strength: 79 tanks (67 operational, 85% readiness)—dramatic reduction from pre-Crusader levels (139 tanks) reflecting battle losses and mechanical attrition.

**Panzer III Ausf J (48 tanks, 42 operational):** New variant with long 5cm KwK 39 L/60 gun—superior penetration compared to earlier L/42. Effective against British Crusader and Valentine tanks. First deliveries arrived late 1941/early 1942. Represented primary medium tank.

**Other Panzer III variants:** Mix of surviving Ausf H/J with short 5cm guns—being supplemented by new long-gun variants.

**Panzer IV variants:** Small numbers of Ausf E/F with short 7.5cm L/24 guns—infantry support role. Long-gun Panzer IV F2/G (7.5cm L/43) not yet arrived in significant numbers.

**Light tanks (Panzer II):** 31 tanks for reconnaissance squadrons.

Tank strength gradually increased through Q1 as reinforcements arrived via Tripoli. By March, division operated approximately 90-100 tanks—adequate for defensive operations but insufficient for renewed major offensive without 15. Panzer-Division support.

---

## 5. Gazala Line Stalemate

By February, German offensive momentum exhausted. Gazala Line—British defensive positions from coast to Bir Hacheim—blocked further advance. Both sides entered preparation phase:

**German preparations:** Stockpiling supplies via Benghazi/Tripoli, rebuilding tank strength, training replacements, planning summer offensive (Battle of Gazala, May 26).

**British preparations:** Fortifying Gazala positions, bringing 8th Army to full strength, integrating new equipment (Grant tanks with 75mm guns arriving March-April).

The 21. Panzer-Division held central sector, conducting patrol operations and minor raids while preparing for Operation Venezia (Rommel's planned May offensive).

---

## 6. Historical Significance

Q1 1942 demonstrated German operational resilience: decisive defeat in November-December 1941 transformed into complete reversal within two months. Rommel's counteroffensive surprised British command, recaptured strategic territory, and restored German prestige.

For 21. Panzer-Division, period marked recovery: new commander established authority, tank replacements restored combat power, swift victories rebuilt morale. January's success positioned division for Gazala battles that would define 1942 North Africa campaign.

Logistically, rapid advance reopened Benghazi port (closer than Tripoli), improving German supply situation. However, extended lines remained vulnerability—British could rebuild faster via short Egyptian routes.
