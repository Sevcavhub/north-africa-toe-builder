### Primary Subordinate Units

**1. 15. Panzer-Division**
- Type: Panzer Division
- Strength: 8,920 personnel
- Commander: Generalmajor Gustav von Vaerst (until 4 Nov when promoted to corps command)
- Note: One of two panzer divisions in Afrika Korps. Elite formation, veteran of entire North African campaign since 1941. Suffered catastrophic losses at El Alamein: 90% of tanks, 50% of personnel.

**2. 21. Panzer-Division**
- Type: Panzer Division
- Strength: 12,800 personnel
- Commander: Generalmajor Georg von Bismarck (KIA 31 Aug 1942), then Generalmajor Heinz von Randow (18 Sep - 21 Dec 1942)
- Note: Second panzer division. Commander von Randow assumed command 18 September after von Bismarck killed by mine. Division destroyed at El Alamein, retreated to Tunisia.

**3. 90. leichte Afrika-Division**
- Type: Light Motorized Division
- Strength: 9,847 personnel
- Commander: Generalmajor Theodor Graf von Sponeck (appointed 1 Sep, promoted Generalmajor 1 Nov 1942)
- Note: Three Panzergrenadier regiments. Excellent desert warfare experience. Supply crisis particularly affected mobility.

**4. 164. leichte Afrika-Division**
- Type: Light Motorized Division
- Strength: 8,400 personnel
- Commander: Generalmajor Carl-Hans Lungershausen
- Note: Three Panzergrenadier regiments (125, 382, 433). Newer to Africa than other divisions. Mixed German-Italian transport (less reliable). Destroyed Tunisia May 1943.

## 8. Supply and Logistics

### Supply Status

CATASTROPHIC throughout Q4 1942. Mediterranean supply crisis reached peak during El Alamein period. British interdiction (Royal Navy, RAF Malta-based bombers, ULTRA intelligence) sank majority of supply convoys. Critical tankers Tripolino and Ostia sunk 1 November, strangling fuel supply. Supply line 1,400km from Tripoli to El Alamein front exposed to constant air attack. Rommel's entire Panzerarmee estimated only 3-4 days fuel at start of El Alamein battle (23 Oct). Ammunition stocks critically low: artillery rationed 10-15 rounds/gun/day (vs. British unlimited fire). Water supply barely adequate at 4.5L/man/day (medical requirement 10-15L in desert heat). Food situation slightly better due to captured British supplies but convoy losses reduced reserves. Supply crisis primary factor in Axis defeat at El Alamein and prevented effective defense during retreat. By December, supply base shifted to Tunis but situation remained critical.

## 11. Tactical Doctrine and Capabilities

### Doctrinal Role

Mobile armored warfare and combined-arms operations in desert environment. Offensive exploitation, mobile defense, counterattack. By Q4 1942, forced into static defensive role at El Alamein due to fuel/ammunition shortages, then conducting fighting retreat.

### Special Capabilities

- Elite desert warfare expertise (continuous operations North Africa since Feb 1941)
- Superior combined-arms coordination (panzer, panzergrenadier, artillery, AT, engineers)
- Aggressive use of 88mm FlaK guns in anti-tank role (tactical innovation)
- Flexible Kampfgruppe organization (mixed battle groups for specific missions)
- Highly mobile defensive tactics with counterattack capability
- Night operations to avoid British air superiority
- Rapid maneuver and exploitation (when fuel available)

### Tactical Innovations

- Integration of 88mm FlaK batteries into mobile anti-tank screens
- All-arms Kampfgruppen mixing tanks, infantry, artillery, AT guns
- Aggressive use of armored cars for deep desert reconnaissance
- Anti-tank gun ambush tactics (lure British tanks onto dug-in AT screens)
- Reverse slope defense utilizing limited terrain features
- Integration of captured British equipment (especially trucks, rations)
