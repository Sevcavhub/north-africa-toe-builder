## 4. Organization

### Primary Subordinate Units

**1. Panzer-Regiment 5**
- Type: Panzer Regiment
- Strength: 1,680 personnel
- Commander: Oberst Gerhard Müller (acting, then Oberst Olbrich)
- Composition: 2 Panzer Abteilungen (I. and II.), each with staff company, 1 medium company, 2 light companies
- Note: From 3. Panzer-Division. Initial Africa deployment Feb 1941: 25× Pz.I, 45× Pz.II, 75× Pz.III, 20× Pz.IV. By Q2 reinforced with newer Pz.III Ausf G.

**2. Schützen-Regiment 200**
- Type: Motorized Infantry Regiment
- Strength: 2,850 personnel
- Composition: 2 Motorized Machine Gun Battalions (2nd and 8th), each with 1 motorcycle company, 3 MG companies, support companies with AT guns and mortars. 3 pioneer companies (later 200th z.b.V. Pioneer Battalion).
- Note: Unique 'regiment' structure: actually 2 separate MG battalions without standard rifle companies. Reflects improvised leichte division organization.

**3. Aufklärungs-Abteilung 3**
- Type: Reconnaissance Battalion
- Strength: 580 personnel
- Composition: 1 light armored car company, 1 motorcycle company, 1 support company (engineer platoon, AT platoon, infantry gun section), supply column
- Note: From 3. Panzer-Division. Critical for desert reconnaissance and screening. One zug used Volkswagen Kübelwagens.

**4. I./Artillerie-Regiment 75**
- Type: Motorized Artillery Battalion
- Strength: 420 personnel
- Composition: Battalion HQ with signals and calibration detachments, 3 motorized batteries
- Note: From 3. Panzer-Division. Only field artillery organic to 5. leichte Division (significantly lighter than standard panzer division with 3 battalions). Additional artillery support from corps assets.

**5. Panzerjäger-Abteilung 39**
- Type: Motorized Anti-Tank Battalion
- Strength: 385 personnel
- Composition: Battalion HQ, 3 motorized panzerjäger companies
- Note: Towed AT guns. 3.7cm 'door-knocker' increasingly inadequate vs British Matilda II. 5cm PaK 38 in limited supply Q2 1941.

**6. Panzerjäger-Abteilung 605 (gepanzert)**
- Type: Armored (Self-Propelled) Anti-Tank Battalion
- Strength: 320 personnel
- Composition: Armored HQ section, signals detachment, 3 self-propelled AT companies
- Note: Self-propelled variant using captured Czech 47mm AT guns. Mobile AT capability. Better penetration than 3.7cm.

**7. Flak-Bataillon 606 (Selbstfahrlafette)**
- Type: Self-Propelled Flak Battalion
- Strength: 380 personnel
- Composition: Self-propelled HQ section, 3 self-propelled flak companies
- Note: Mobile AA defense for advancing formations. Critical against RAF fighter-bomber attacks.

**8. I./Flak-Regiment 33**
- Type: Flak Battalion
- Strength: 440 personnel
- Composition: Battalion HQ with signals, 2× 20mm batteries (4 guns each), 3× 88mm batteries (4 guns each), light supply column
- Note: From 3. Panzer-Division. 88mm guns proved devastatingly effective in anti-tank role against British cruiser tanks. Rommel's preferred anti-tank weapon.

**9. 200th z.b.V. Pionier-Bataillon**
- Type: Engineer Battalion
- Strength: 485 personnel
- Composition: 3 motorized pioneer companies (originally attached to 2nd and 8th MG Battalions, later consolidated)
- Note: z.b.V. (zur besonderen Verwendung) = for special employment. Mine warfare and fortification critical for Tobruk siege.

**10. Nachrichten-Abteilung 39**
- Type: Signals Battalion
- Strength: 345 personnel
- Composition: Signals company (3/39th), radio company, mapping platoon (200th), printing detachment (200th)
- Note: Communications critical for mobile desert operations and coordination with DAK HQ.

**11. Divisions-Nachschubführer**
- Type: Divisional Supply Troops
- Strength: 2,140 personnel
- Composition: Multiple supply battalions (619th, 533rd), numerous light/heavy supply columns, water columns, maintenance, medical (631st/633rd ambulance cos, 4/572nd field hospital, 1/83rd medical co, 877th medical supply), administration (683rd/681st admin battalions, 341st admin section), field services (735th field post, 309th MP, 503rd butcher, 531st bakery)
- Note: Extensive logistical tail essential for desert operations 900km from Tripoli. Water and fuel supply paramount. Medical facilities for casualties and desert illnesses.

## 11. Tactical Doctrine and Capabilities

### Doctrinal Role

Light motorized division for mobile operations in North Africa. Lighter than standard panzer division but more mobile and suitable for desert warfare with extended supply lines. Primary roles: Tobruk siege operations, mobile defense, rapid counterattacks, exploitation.

### Special Capabilities

- Mobile desert warfare with reduced logistical footprint vs. panzer division
- Combined arms coordination (tanks, motorized MG battalions, AT guns, artillery)
- Rapid redeployment capability on interior lines
- Self-propelled AT and AA battalions for mobile operations
- Extensive reconnaissance capability (Aufklärungs-Abteilung 3, armored cars, motorcycles)
- 88mm FlaK dual-role (AA and devastating AT) - Rommel's 'secret weapon'

### Tactical Innovations

- 8.8cm FlaK in anti-tank ambush role (Operation Battleaxe success)
- Mobile hedgehog defense with AT guns and tanks in overwatch
- Rapid concentration of force using interior lines around Tobruk
- Self-propelled AT/AA battalions enabling mobile all-arms groups
- Exploitation of British supply dumps for sustainment

### Known Issues and Limitations

- Lighter artillery (only 1 battalion vs. 3 in panzer division) - relies on corps artillery support
- 3.7cm PaK 35/36 'door-knocker' inadequate vs. Matilda II infantry tanks (only 88mm and 5cm effective)
- Extended supply lines (900km from Tripoli) with Tobruk garrison disrupting coastal road
- Smaller tank force (165 vs. 15. Panzer's 155, but 15. Panzer was also understrength)
- Unique 'MG battalion' structure less flexible than standard rifle regiments
- Water scarcity limits operational tempo and soldier endurance
- Desert conditions: heat, sand, vehicle breakdowns, navigation challenges
- Vulnerable soft-skinned vehicles to RAF air attacks

### Desert Adaptations

Tropical uniforms, pith helmets, and desert boots issued. Water discipline critical: 10L/man/day minimum. Vehicles equipped with sand filters (Feifel air cleaners), desert tires, and extra radiator capacity. Navigation by sun compass, dead reckoning, and celestial navigation. Camouflage nets for concealment. Goggles and face scarves for sandstorms. Spare parts stockpiled for high breakdown rates.
